# Book I — The World

Purpose: make first-century Galilee/Judea intelligible enough that Yeshua does not appear from theological vacuum.

## Proposed chapters

1. **Under Other Men's Flags**
   Roman power, Herodian rule, taxation, memory of conquest.

2. **The God of Israel**
   Covenant, Torah, Temple, scripture, divine kingship, prophetic memory.

3. **One People, Many Judaisms**
   Priests, Pharisees, sectarian communities, apocalyptic groups, ordinary village practice.

4. **The House on the Hill**
   Jerusalem and the Temple as sacred center, political institution, economy, and contested symbol.

5. **Galilee**
   Villages, fields, fishing, debt, Sepphoris, Antipas, local identity.

6. **The End Was Always Near**
   Danielic/apocalyptic imagination, resurrection, judgment, messianic hopes, angels/adversaries.

7. **The River**
   Yohanan/John, baptism, repentance, judgment, and why people went into the wilderness.

8. **A Child With Too Many Stories**
   Birth/family traditions presented as later competing memories rather than forced biography.

## Closing movement

An adult Yeshua approaches John.

The book ends before telling the reader what the experience means.
