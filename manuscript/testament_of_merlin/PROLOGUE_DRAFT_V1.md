# Prologue Draft V1 — The Name

Status: AUTHORED CONSTRUCTION WITH SOURCE-BOUND FRAME

The battle received four words.

Centuries later, the man received three.

A scribe bent over an older reckoning of years and found Arfderydd waiting there: a battle compressed until blood, weather, shouting, horses, broken shields, brothers, kings and whatever remained for the crows had all become a mark in a book.

The old notice did not tell him what the dying had said.

It did not tell him who had run.

It did not tell him whether anyone walked away from the field unable to become himself again.

It only remembered that the battle had happened.

That was not enough.

So memory did what memory does when silence becomes unbearable.

It supplied a man.

Merlin went mad.

Three words, and the emptiness acquired a witness.

The scribe could not have known the man at Arfderydd. No grandfather of his grandfather had known him. Whatever voice once crossed the northern forest had passed through generations that changed its words, moved its borders, renamed its enemies, Christianized its terrors, and taught its prophecies to predict victories that had already occurred.

Still, the sentence looked backward with complete confidence.

Merlin went mad.

Perhaps someone had.

Perhaps a survivor had watched his lord fall and walked away from human company.

Perhaps he slept beneath trees because walls reminded him of men.

Perhaps birds became easier to understand than kings.

Perhaps hunger sharpened him.

Perhaps grief broke the ordinary sequence of time so thoroughly that memory and anticipation began to feel like the same thing.

Perhaps people asked him what would happen next because he had become very good at seeing what they refused to see now.

Perhaps none of that happened.

The battle did.

The man is harder.

Long after Arfderydd, one tradition would call a wild prophet Lailoken. Another would speak in the authority of Myrddin. An older story knew a fatherless boy named Ambrosius who understood the dragons beneath a king's collapsing tower.

Then a writer gathered fragments that had not been born together and gave them one body.

Merlin.

By the time the magician entered Arthur's court, the forest had become difficult to hear inside him.

This is not his recovered testimony.

There is no recovered testimony.

This is the story of what may have stood before the name—and of what the name did afterward.
