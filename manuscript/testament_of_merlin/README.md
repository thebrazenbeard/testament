# The Testament of Merlin

Working subtitle: **The Prophet Who Was Made**

Status: EXPLORATORY LITERARY BRANCH / NON-CANONICAL

This branch develops a source-critical literary work around the Merlin/Myrddin/Lailoken complex while preserving the upstream uncertainty that a single historical "Merlin" may never have existed.

The governing question is not:

> Who was the real wizard Merlin?

It is:

> What happens when history remembers a catastrophe more securely than it remembers the person who survived it?

## Core premise

A battle is remembered.

A man may have survived it.

The oldest surviving poetic core does not securely give him the name later generations know.

One tradition calls the wilderness prophet Lailoken.
Another calls a prophetic authority Myrddin.
An older British story gives the dragon-prophecy role to Ambrosius.
Geoffrey of Monmouth combines these currents into Merlin.

The literary work follows that transformation rather than hiding it.

## Narrative form

The book should be polyphonic and stratified.

The sixth-century layer is intimate, embodied, and uncertain.

The prophetic-poetry layer shows memory being reshaped by later political needs.

The hagiographic layer turns madness and wilderness into moral signs.

The Geoffrey layer shows a writer encountering incompatible fragments and making a single man from them.

The later Arthurian layer shows the manufactured figure escaping his maker.

No narrator receives omniscient authority over the historical question.

## Central themes

- catastrophe and altered perception;
- whether "madness" and insight can coexist;
- memory versus biography;
- the political use of prophecy;
- names as containers imposed after a life;
- wilderness as exile, refuge, and epistemic threshold;
- the difference between seeing patterns and seeing the future;
- how institutions domesticate dangerous outsiders;
- how literature can preserve truth while inventing facts;
- whether a person survives becoming a symbol.
