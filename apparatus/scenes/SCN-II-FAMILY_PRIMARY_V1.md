## Primary source inventory

### Mark 3:20–35
- family/close-associate intervention tradition;
- redefinition of kinship in Markan literary context.

Historical question:
does this preserve actual family conflict, Markan characterization, or both?

### Mark 6:1–6
- hometown rejection;
- "carpenter";
- "son of Mary" in the mainstream critical reading;
- named brothers;
- sisters.

Textual hazard:
Mark 6:3 has an alternative reading that reduces/removes the stark matronymic formulation.

Therefore:
paternity arguments must bind exact witness/edition evidence.

Recent literary scholarship also argues that Mark's matronymic may perform narrative characterization rather than encode illegitimacy.

### Paul
- Galatians 1:19: James called "brother of the Lord";
- 1 Corinthians 9:5: "brothers of the Lord";
- 1 Corinthians 15:7: appearance tradition naming James.

These are earlier extant than Gospel family narratives.

## Panthera firewall

Celsus/Origen and later rabbinic Panthera/Pandera traditions are later countertraditions/reception evidence.

They do not establish biological paternity.

Forbidden circularity:
later Panthera accusation -> reinterpret Mark 6:3/John 8:41 as first-century proof -> count those verses as independent confirmation.

## Historical minimum

Strong source-level facts:
- Paul identifies James as "brother of the Lord" and refers to "brothers of the Lord";
- early Gospel tradition names Yeshua's mother, brothers, and sisters;
- Nazareth/Galilee is strongly associated with Yeshua.

Historically plausible:
- James was Yeshua's brother in an ordinary familial sense;
- some family tension or distance existed during at least part of the movement.

Unresolved:
- Joseph's historical role after infancy;
- exact nature of Mark 3 conflict;
- whether Mark 6:3 encodes stigma;
- biological paternity beyond ordinary inference;
- Panthera relationship.

## Construction budget
