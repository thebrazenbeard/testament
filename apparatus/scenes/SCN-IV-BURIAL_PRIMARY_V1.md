# Scene Packet — Burial V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-IV-BURIAL

## Historical question

What can responsibly be said about disposition of Yeshua's body after crucifixion?

## Source inventory

### 1 Corinthians 15:3–4
Earliest extant Christian layer says:
- died;
- was buried;
- was raised.

It does not identify:
- Joseph of Arimathea;
- a rock-cut tomb;
- women visiting;
- guards;
- tomb ownership.

### Mark 15:42–47
- Joseph of Arimathea;
- request from Pilate;
- linen;
- rock-hewn tomb;
- stone;
- women observe location.

### Matthew 27:57–66
- probable Markan dependence;
- develops Joseph as disciple;
- adds guard/sealing narrative.

### Luke 23:50–56
- probable Markan dependence;
- develops Joseph's moral/council characterization;
- women prepare spices.

### John 19:38–42
- Joseph plus Nicodemus;
- large spice quantity;
- garden/new tomb.

Relationship of John to Synoptic burial tradition:
unit-specific and disputed.

## Roman/Jewish context

Roman crucified bodies could be:
- exposed;
- denied normal burial;
- or released/buried under some circumstances.

No universal Roman rule settles Yeshua's case.

Jewish burial practice and Passover/Sabbath timing make prompt disposal historically intelligible.

## Historical minimum

Strong:
- Yeshua died by crucifixion.
- earliest Christian tradition includes burial.

Plausible:
- burial/disposal before Sabbath;
- Joseph of Arimathea may be historical.

Unresolved:
- exact agent(s);
- exact tomb type;
- ownership;
- location;
- spices;
- guard;
- whether burial was honorable, temporary, or otherwise.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- making Matthew's guard part of Mark/Paul's historical floor;
- assuming Joseph's exact social status/motivation across all Gospels;
- naming a specific archaeological tomb;
- omniscient claim about body location after burial.

Allowed:
- source-specific burial scene;
- explicitly provisional Joseph reconstruction;
- cut away before inaccessible post-burial events.

## Manuscript strategy

Book IV may narrate a burial scene only if the apparatus states which Gospel framework is selected and where Paul provides only the minimal "buried" claim.
