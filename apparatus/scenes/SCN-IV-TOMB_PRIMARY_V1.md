## Historical question

What do the canonical tomb narratives actually agree on, and what cannot be fused?

## Source inventory

### Mark 16:1–8
Under current ECM-related scholarship, the earliest attainable Markan text ends at 16:8.

Core features:
- women;
- stone already moved;
- young man/messenger;
- proclamation;
- instruction concerning Galilee;
- fear/silence ending.

### Matthew 28:1–10
Probable Markan dependence.
Distinctives include:
- earthquake/angel descent;
- guards;
- women encounter Yeshua.

### Luke 24:1–12
Probable Markan dependence.
Distinctives include:
- expanded women list;
- two men/messengers;
- apostolic disbelief;
- Jerusalem trajectory.

### John 20:1–18
Distinctive sequence:
- Mary Magdalene initially alone in narrative foreground;
- Peter/beloved disciple;
- two angels;
- Mary encounter.

## Mark-ending textual control

Mark 16:9–20 remains important canonical/reception text.

It is not used as the earliest attainable Markan ending in this project unless a future textual-critical review overturns the current control.

## Historical minimum

Strong textual fact:
- empty-tomb discovery narratives are present in all four canonical Gospels in differing forms.

Historical inference:
- empty-tomb historicity remains debated.

Paul:
- does not narrate women finding an empty tomb;
- his "buried/raised" language does not by itself provide the canonical tomb-discovery scene.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- fused visitor list;
- one angel becoming two without apparatus;
- Matthew's guards imported into other accounts;
- omniscient narration of the moment the tomb became empty;
- treating an empty tomb as proof of a resurrection mechanism.

Allowed:
- source-specific discovery scene;
- multiple incompatible memories presented side by side;
- narration that stays with a named witness and preserves uncertainty.

## Manuscript strategy

The safest literary strategy remains:
follow people, not the inaccessible event.
