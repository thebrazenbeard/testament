## Historical question

What can be responsibly narrated about Yeshua's baptism and the voice/Spirit traditions attached to it?

## Source inventory

### Mark 1:9–11
Role:
- earliest extant canonical narrative under common dating;
- Yeshua comes from Nazareth/Galilee and is baptized by John;
- heavenly opening, Spirit/dove imagery, second-person voice.

Dependence:
- Matthew/Luke likely use Mark at this point under Markan-priority models.

Historical ceiling:
- strong evidence for early baptism tradition;
- literary form does not prove observable heavenly mechanics.

### Matthew 3:13–17
Distinctives:
- dialogue in which John resists;
- explicit fulfillment/righteousness framing;
- voice presented in a more public third-person form.

Dependence:
- probable Markan literary dependence plus Matthean redaction.

### Luke 3:21–22
Distinctives:
- baptism is narrated with "all the people";
- prayer emphasized;
- John's role is narratively displaced by the prior arrest notice.

Text-critical hazard:
Luke 3:22 has a consequential variant.

Codex Bezae D/05 and early Latin/patristic evidence preserve a Psalm 2:7-type form commonly represented as:
"you are my son; today I have begotten you."

The dominant modern critical-text form instead aligns more closely with the beloved-son wording.

Scholarship disagrees over the earliest recoverable reading and its Christological significance.

Controls:
- WIT-D05-CODEX-BEZAE;
- Peter Doble, DOI 10.1163/9789004273931_010;
- Shaily Shashikant Patel, DOI 10.1163/15685365-12341684.

Consequence:
Testament may not quote "Luke's exact baptismal voice" without an edition/witness note.

### John 1:29–34
John does not narrate Yeshua's baptism in the Synoptic form.

It preserves Baptist testimony about Spirit descent and recognition.

Relation to Synoptic baptism tradition:
unit-specific and disputed.

### Josephus, Antiquities 18.116–119
Establishes John as an independently attested baptizing moral/religious movement leader.

Does not say that John baptized Yeshua.

## Minimum defensible reconstruction

- John was a real and influential baptizing figure.
- Yeshua's association with John is historically strong.
- Yeshua being baptized by John is a strong/probable historical reconstruction.
- exact heavenly voice, dove/Spirit phenomenology, who perceived it, and exact wording remain source/text dependent.

## Conflicts / differences

- who hears/sees what;
- voice wording/address;
- John-Yeshua dialogue;
- narrative placement of John's arrest;
- Luke 3:22 textual variant.

## Construction budget

Allowed:
- physical river setting consistent with contextual evidence;
- actions necessary to depict baptism;
- silence/uncertainty around internal experience.

Not allowed as neutral fact:
- omniscient statement that God audibly spoke;
- omniscient statement that no voice occurred;
- exact internal thoughts;
- synthetic voice assembled from Matthew/Mark/Luke;
- "today I have begotten you" or "beloved Son" presented as uncontested Luke wording.

## Manuscript strategy

Prefer limited viewpoint.

The scene can show Yeshua entering the water and can preserve competing reports of what followed.

If prose must decide the metaphysical mechanism, it has exceeded Book II's evidence ceiling.
