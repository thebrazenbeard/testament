## Historical question

What, if anything, can be responsibly narrated about Yeshua's birth and childhood from the surviving sources?

## Source inventory

### Mark
Mark begins with adult public activity.

It supplies no infancy narrative.

### Paul
Paul's undisputed letters do not narrate a virgin conception, Bethlehem birth, magi, shepherds, massacre, or census journey.

Romans 1:3 preserves Davidic-descent language in an early Christological formula/tradition.

That is not an infancy narrative.

### Matthew 1–2
Distinctive elements:
- genealogy through Joseph;
- conception/angel dream tradition;
- Bethlehem;
- magi/star;
- Herod;
- massacre;
- Egypt;
- later settlement in Nazareth.

### Luke 1–2; 3:23–38
Distinctive elements:
- annunciation cycles;
- census/Quirinius framing;
- Nazareth -> Bethlehem;
- shepherds;
- Temple presentation;
- return to Nazareth;
- different genealogy placed at adult-baptism horizon.

## Genealogy textual-transmission control

Luke 3:23–38 is not textually frictionless.

Andrew J. Patton's 2026 JTS study reports substantial manuscript variation in the Lukan genealogy and argues that columnar manuscript formatting contributed to copying errors in a significant minority of Greek witnesses.

DOI:
10.1093/jts/flag024

Consequence:
genealogy comparison must bind a declared critical text/witness where exact names/order matter.

## Historical minimum

Strong:
- Yeshua is securely associated with Nazareth/Galilee in adult tradition;
- Matthew and Luke preserve distinct infancy narratives;
- both connect Yeshua with Davidic/Bethlehem symbolism/tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact birth year;
- exact birthplace;
- census mechanism;
- virgin conception as historical mechanism;
- magi/star;
- massacre;
- Egypt;
- exact genealogy;
- exact family residence sequence.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- fused magi + shepherds Christmas scene presented as history;
- harmonized Herod/Quirinius chronology without declared hypothesis;
- exact childhood personality;
- exact Joseph biography after infancy;
- using later Panthera traditions to "solve" infancy silence;
- treating theological scriptural fulfilment as neutral event report.

Allowed:
- present Matthew and Luke as competing literary/traditional portraits;
- dramatize uncertainty explicitly;
- avoid a birth scene entirely if prose would require false knowledge.

## Manuscript strategy

Book I can make the absence of recoverable childhood data part of the story.

Uncertainty is preferable to a seamless Nativity composite.
