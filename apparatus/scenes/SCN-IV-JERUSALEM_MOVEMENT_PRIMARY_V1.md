# Scene Packet — Earliest Jerusalem Movement V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-IV-JERUSALEM

## Historical question

What can be reconstructed about the earliest post-death Yeshua movement in Jerusalem before later church structures are projected backward?

## Earliest extant Pauline evidence

### Galatians 1–2
Paul reports:
- Cephas;
- James "the brother of the Lord";
- John;
- Jerusalem visits;
- conflict over gentile/Torah/table practice.

This is first-person evidence for Paul's contacts and disputes, not a full constitutional history of Jerusalem leadership.

### 1 Corinthians
Relevant:
- 1 Cor 15:3–8 appearance/leadership names;
- 1 Cor 16 collection instructions.

### Romans 15
Paul's Jerusalem collection/mission concern is explicit.

## Acts

Acts 1–6, 11, 15 and related material narrate:
- Jerusalem leadership;
- Temple activity;
- communal sharing;
- conflicts;
- councils/decisions;
- mission expansion.

Historical use rule:
Acts is a later narrative source and must be tested proposition by proposition against Paul's letters and external context.

## Collection control

Paul's letters make the Jerusalem collection a major element of his mission.

Luke-Acts is strikingly quiet/ambiguous about that project.

Current discussion:
Zachary J. Cole, "On Luke's Silence Concerning Paul's Collection for Jerusalem," JTS (2026), DOI 10.1093/jts/flag034.

This is a concrete warning against treating Acts as a transparent duplicate of Pauline history.

## Josephus

Antiquities 20.200 may independently witness James in Jerusalem and his death, subject to textual/authenticity review.

It does not describe the earliest communal structure.

## Historical minimum

Strong:
- an earlier Yeshua movement existed before Paul's participation;
- Jerusalem mattered;
- Cephas and James were major early figures;
- Paul interacted and sometimes conflicted with earlier leaders;
- gentile/Torah/table questions were real early disputes.

Plausible:
- Temple participation by at least some Jerusalem followers;
- organized charity/support to Jerusalem.

Unresolved:
- exact Twelve governance;
- exact role hierarchy;
- exact daily Temple pattern;
- literal extent/duration of pooled property;
- seamless unity implied by some Acts summaries;
- exact chronology of Jerusalem meetings when Acts and Paul are harmonized.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- Acts 2/4 communal-property summaries as unqualified documentary fact;
- one unified apostolic cabinet;
- suppression of Galatians conflict;
- invented speeches from Acts treated as stenography.

Allowed:
- source-specific Jerusalem scenes;
- competing accounts of leadership;
- visible conflict and negotiation.

## Manuscript strategy

Book IV should make the earliest movement look unfinished.

A finished "Church" with later offices and doctrine would be an anachronism.
