# Scene Packet — Paul's Call / Reorientation V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-IV-SAUL-CALL

## Historical question

What does Paul himself say happened to him, and how do later Acts narratives develop that event?

## Paul's own evidence

### Galatians 1:13–17
Paul reports:
- former persecution;
- zeal in ancestral traditions;
- divine disclosure/revelation of the Son;
- call language with prophetic resonance;
- Arabia/Damascus before later Jerusalem contact.

### 1 Corinthians 9:1
Paul appeals to having seen Jesus the Lord.

### 1 Corinthians 15:8
Paul places his own appearance experience after the received appearance list.

### Philippians 3
Provides autobiographical identity/revaluation material, not a road narrative.

## "Conversion" versus "call"

Modern scholarship debates whether "conversion" imports an anachronistic religion-switch model.

Paul can also be read through:
- prophetic call;
- apocalyptic revelation;
- mission commission;
- radical reorientation within Jewish identity.

Testament should use neutral "call/reorientation" language until a specific interpretive frame is argued.

## Acts

Acts narrates the event three times:
- Acts 9;
- Acts 22;
- Acts 26.

These forms differ in:
- narrative perspective;
- commissioning detail;
- companion description;
- speech content;
- rhetorical setting.

They are not three independent witnesses.

## Historical minimum

Strong:
- Paul persecuted the movement;
- Paul later became a major proclaimer;
- Paul attributed the change to divine revelation/appearance/call involving Jesus;
- Paul understood himself as commissioned toward gentiles.

Unresolved:
- exact phenomenology;
- exact location;
- road mechanics;
- exact words heard;
- what companions perceived;
- how quickly Paul's theology developed;
- whether "conversion" is the best category.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- one fused Acts 9/22/26 transcript;
- putting Ananias material into Paul's own memory as if Paul reports it in Galatians;
- exact external-vs-internal voice mechanics;
- psychological diagnosis;
- using Acts chronology to overwrite Galatians.

Allowed:
- narrate Paul's own sparse self-report;
- separately present Acts as later narrative elaboration;
- preserve uncertainty about phenomenology.
