# Scene Packet — Final Meal / Last Supper V1

Variant controls:
- VAR-LUKE-22-15-20-FINAL-MEAL

## Historical question

What can be responsibly reconstructed about Yeshua's final meal, its Passover status, and the words later attached to bread/cup tradition?

## Earliest extant witness

### 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

Paul preserves a received/handed-on final-meal tradition before the narrative Gospels.

Historical value:
- final-meal/Eucharistic interpretive tradition is early;
- Paul does not provide the full Gospel narrative setting;
- Paul's wording cannot simply be assumed to be a transcript.

## Synoptic sources

### Mark 14:12–26
- Passover setting under normal narrative reading;
- bread/cup interpretation;
- covenant language;
- betrayal prediction.

### Matthew 26:17–30
- substantial Markan dependence probable;
- Matthean wording/theology must not be counted as independent repetition by default.

### Luke 22:7–20
- Passover setting;
- distinctive cup/meal sequence;
- significant textual variation at 22:15–20.

## Luke 22 textual-critical hazard

The passage exists in multiple longer/shorter forms.

Recent work argues that "longer" versus "shorter" is itself an oversimplification because shorter witnesses are internally diverse.

Rob James, "Variant Readings of Luke 22:15-20 and the Relationship of Codex Bezae to Curetonian Syriac," JTS 75 (2024), DOI 10.1093/jts/flae045.

Consequence:
Testament must not synthesize one exact Lukan institution wording without declaring its textual base.

## John

John 13–17 gives a final meal before Passover chronology but does not reproduce the Synoptic/Pauline bread-cup institution wording in the same way.

This creates two distinct problems:
1. chronology;
2. tradition/form.

## Passover contradiction

Synoptic normal reading:
final meal = Passover meal.

Johannine chronology:
Yeshua dies before the Passover meal, on preparation chronology.

See:
- CONTR-PASSION-001.

## Minimum defensible reconstruction

Strong:
- Yeshua shared a final meal with followers before arrest;
- an interpretive bread/cup tradition is early, already present in Paul.

Unresolved:
- exact calendrical status;
- exact guest list;
- exact wording;
- exact sequence of cups/bread;
- relation among Pauline, Markan, and Lukan forms.

## Construction budget

Forbidden as recovered history:
- one seamless "institution narrative" made by combining Paul + Mark + Matthew + Luke;
- exact Passover date asserted without apparatus;
- exact Judas dialogue unless source-specific;
- Luke's longer/shorter form silently chosen.

Allowed:
- choose one source framework for literary scene, explicitly declared;
- or narrate through partial/fragmented memory that preserves source divergence.

## Manuscript strategy

The historical prose should privilege the existence and significance of the final meal over invented transcript precision.
