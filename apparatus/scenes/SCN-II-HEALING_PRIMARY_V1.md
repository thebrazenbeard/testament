# Scene Packet — Healing and Exorcism V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-HEALING

## Historical question

What historical claim is defensible beneath the many healing/exorcism narratives?

## Source density

Synoptic tradition repeatedly portrays Yeshua as:
- healer;
- exorcist;
- ritual/religious authority;
- figure whose healings are linked to kingdom proclamation.

Representative Markan clusters:
- Mark 1:21–34;
- Mark 2:1–12;
- Mark 5:1–43;
- Mark 7:24–37;
- Mark 8:22–26;
- Mark 9:14–29;
- Mark 10:46–52.

Matthew and Luke often inherit/rework Markan material and add distinctive healing traditions.

John preserves a different "signs" architecture and must be reviewed separately.

## Ancient-category control

Ancient possession/illness categories are not modern diagnoses.

Forbidden automatic translations:
- demon = psychosis;
- paralysis = psychosomatic illness;
- blindness = one known biomedical diagnosis;
- healing = supernatural violation of physics;
- healing = fraud/placebo.

Any of these may be hypotheses in particular discussions.
None is the lexical meaning of the ancient narrative.

## Historical minimum

Strong textual fact:
- healing/exorcism is a major feature of early narrative Jesus tradition.

Plausible/probable:
- Yeshua engaged in practices experienced/interpreted as healing and exorcism and acquired such a reputation.

Unresolved:
- mechanism;
- diagnosis;
- exact event count;
- which stories are historical, doubled, symbolic, or redactional;
- nature-miracle historicity.

## Scene selection rule

Book II should not create one synthetic "representative miracle" from multiple stories and then imply it happened.

If a composite is useful literarily:
- mark COMPOSITE;
- list every source contribution;
- do not count it as a historical event.

## Construction budget

Allowed:
- source-bound physical actions;
- witness reaction;
- social consequences;
- limited-viewpoint uncertainty.

Forbidden:
- narrator diagnosis;
- narrator supernatural mechanism;
- narrator debunking mechanism;
- invented patient backstory presented as factual;
- exact internal compassion motive unless source/literary construction is declared.

## Manuscript strategy

Describe what people do, perceive, and report.

Do not make the historian solve the metaphysics in Book II.
