# Scene Packet — Crucifixion V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-CRUCIFIXION

## Historical question

What can be narrated with high confidence about Yeshua's execution, and where do the Passion accounts become source-specific?

## Source inventory

### Mark 15
Core Markan crucifixion narrative under common dating.

### Matthew 27
Probable Markan dependence with Matthean additions and scriptural/theological framing.

### Luke 23
Probable Markan dependence with distinctive sayings, criminals, crowd and innocence themes.

### John 19
Distinct chronology/details, extended fulfilment motifs, different sayings and social relations at the cross.

### Tacitus, Annals 15.44
Later Roman witness to execution under Pilate/Tiberius.

## Material context

### Yehohanan remains
Demonstrate at least one first-century Judaean crucifixion involving a nail through the heel.

Do not infer:
- Yeshua's exact nail placement;
- number of nails;
- cross shape;
- ropes versus nails;
- identical body treatment.

## Historical minimum

Very strong:
- Yeshua was crucified under Pontius Pilate;
- Roman authority was responsible for the execution;
- execution occurred around Passover in the early 30s CE, with exact year unresolved.

Plausible:
- political/royal charge language such as "King of the Jews" belongs to the execution's Roman intelligibility.

Unresolved:
- exact date/hour;
- precise mechanics;
- exact companions;
- exact crowd composition;
- exact last sayings;
- darkness/earthquake/saints phenomena as historical observation versus theological narrative;
- exact spear episode.

## Last-words control

Mark/Matthew foreground a cry of abandonment.

Luke and John preserve different final sayings.

See CONTR-CROSS-001.

No "Seven Last Words" harmony is allowed as recovered transcript.

## Construction budget

Allowed:
- historically grounded Roman execution setting;
- physical degradation consistent with known crucifixion practice;
- limited-viewpoint sensory detail that does not assert a unique mechanism.

Forbidden:
- importing Yehohanan's exact nail pattern;
- combining every canonical saying/sign into one timeline;
- omniscient theological narration of atonement inside Book II;
- pretending exact medical cause of death is known.

## Manuscript strategy

Book II should end in historically recoverable Roman violence and human uncertainty.

Later theological interpretations belong in Book IV/V.
