# Scene Packet — Meals with Tax Collectors / "Sinners" V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-MEALS

## Historical question

What can responsibly be said about Yeshua's meal practices and the social boundaries attached to them?

## Source clusters

### Mark 2:13–17
- meal associated with Levi;
- tax collectors/"sinners";
- scribal/Pharisaic objection;
- physician/calling saying.

Matthew 9:9–13 and Luke 5:27–32:
- probable Markan dependence with redaction.

### Lukan special meal scenes
Examples:
- Luke 7:36–50;
- Luke 14;
- Luke 19:1–10.

These are important for Lukan theology and reception.

They are not automatically independent historical confirmation of every Synoptic meal motif.

### Broader traditions
- fasting disputes;
- banquet parables;
- feeding stories;
- final meal.

Each has distinct historical/source questions.

## Category control

"Tax collectors" and "sinners" are source labels/categories that require contextual interpretation.

Do not silently translate them into:
- "the marginalized";
- "outcasts";
- "criminals";
- "ritually unclean."

Those may overlap in some cases but are not lexical identities.

## Purity control

Meal association with tax collectors/"sinners" does not by itself establish violation of ritual-purity law.

Cecilia Wassen argues that social boundary concerns are more directly evidenced than purity-law violation in these traditions:
DOI 10.1163/17455197-01402004.

## Historical minimum

Plausible/probable:
- Yeshua acquired a reputation for association and meals with people labeled tax collectors/"sinners";
- that practice could generate social/religious criticism.

Unresolved:
- frequency;
- exact guest composition;
- whether every meal controversy is historical;
- exact relation to purity;
- whether Yeshua followed one universal open-table policy.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- "Jesus ate with everyone";
- "Jesus deliberately broke purity law" without scene-specific evidence;
- modern inclusion slogans placed in Yeshua's mouth;
- invented guest biographies used as historical exemplars.

Allowed:
- source-specific meal;
- visible status/hospitality dynamics;
- attributed criticism;
- uncertainty over why a meal was controversial.

## Manuscript strategy

Let the table show social order physically.

Do not let the narrator explain the scene with twenty-first-century categories before the ancient categories have done their work.
