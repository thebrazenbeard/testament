# Scene Packet — Arrest, Council, and Pilate V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-ARREST-TRIAL

## Historical question

What can be responsibly reconstructed between Yeshua's arrest and Roman crucifixion?

## Source inventory

### Mark 14:43–15:15
- arrest;
- chief-priestly/council hearing material;
- morning transfer;
- Pilate interrogation;
- Barabbas/crowd sequence;
- crucifixion sentence.

### Matthew 26:47–27:26
- probable Markan dependence;
- expands Judas, council, Pilate, crowd and handwashing material.

### Luke 22:47–23:25
- probable Markan dependence plus distinctive material;
- reshapes hearing chronology;
- adds Herod Antipas episode;
- distinct innocence emphases.

### John 18–19
- distinctive Annas/Caiaphas sequence;
- extended Pilate dialogue;
- political/legal-theological framing differs markedly.

## External controls

### Pilate inscription
Establishes Pilate as prefect of Judaea.

It says nothing about this case.

### Philo, Legatio ad Gaium 299–305
Near-contemporary context for Pilate's administration.

Use:
administrative/political context.

Not use:
direct reconstruction of Yeshua's trial.

### Tacitus, Annals 15.44
Later Roman witness associates Christus's execution with Pontius Pilate under Tiberius.

Does not verify Gospel proceedings.

## Council/Sanhedrin problem

"The Sanhedrin" must not be treated as one fully recoverable modern-style supreme court whose exact procedure is known.

Questions remain:
- what council assembled;
- its competencies;
- timing;
- legal versus political function;
- relation to chief-priestly authority;
- relation to Roman jurisdiction.

Recent scholarship:
- Nathanael Andrade, *Killing the Messiah* (2025), chapter "Jesus and 'the Sanhedrin'": treats council identity/competence as a live historical problem.
- T. C. Schmidt (2025) argues more positively for an official-trial framework and possible documentary records.

These are competing reconstructions, not project facts.

## Historical minimum

Strong:
- Yeshua was arrested before crucifixion;
- Pilate's Roman authority ordered/authorized crucifixion.

Plausible:
- Jerusalem priestly/local elite actors were involved in arrest, interrogation, accusation, or transfer to Roman authority.

Unresolved:
- exact council constitution;
- exact charges in each stage;
- exact sequence;
- exact words;
- Barabbas episode mechanics;
- Pilate's private motives;
- whether every Gospel hearing reflects one underlying procedure.

## Anti-blame firewall

Roman crucifixion is Roman state violence.

Local elite involvement does not justify:
- collective Jewish guilt;
- "the Jews killed Jesus";
- treating Gospel blame allocation as neutral courtroom record.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- verbatim council transcript;
- verbatim Pilate transcript;
- reading Pilate's mind;
- fused Annas/Caiaphas/Sanhedrin chronology;
- later Mishnah/Tosefta procedure projected backward as certain first-century law.

Allowed:
- source-specific hearing sequence;
- declared composite scene if apparatus marks each source contribution;
- political-risk interpretation as attributed reconstruction.

## Manuscript strategy

If Book II chooses one procedural sequence for readability, the apparatus labels it literary reconstruction.

No dialogue may be presented as stenographic.
