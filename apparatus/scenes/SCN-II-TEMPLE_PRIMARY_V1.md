# Scene Packet — Temple Action V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-TEMPLE

## Historical question

What can be responsibly narrated about Yeshua's action at the Jerusalem Temple, its timing, and its contribution to arrest/execution risk?

## Primary source inventory

### Mark 11:15–19
- Temple action in final Jerusalem sequence.
- Vendors/money changers; vessel-traffic restriction in Mark.
- scriptural interpretation embedded in the scene.
- chief-priestly/scribal response follows in narrative.

### Matthew 21:12–17
- probable Markan dependence.
- action placed immediately after entry.
- additional healing/children material and Matthean scriptural framing.

### Luke 19:45–48
- probable Markan dependence.
- compressed action and teaching.
- Lukan redaction must not count as independent repetition.

### John 2:13–22
- action near beginning of public ministry.
- distinctive whip/cattle/sheep detail.
- temple/body saying and retrospective resurrection interpretation.

Relationship to Synoptic tradition:
- unit-specific and disputed;
- canonical ubiquity != four independent witnesses.

## Chronology conflict

See CONTR-MINISTRY-001.

Synoptic narrative:
final Jerusalem phase.

John:
early ministry.

Possible models:
- John relocates one historical event;
- Synoptics relocate one historical event;
- two events;
- different traditions shaped into one literary scene.

No model is installed silently.

## Historical minimum

Historically plausible/probable:
- an underlying Temple confrontation/action occurred;
- it happened in Jerusalem in a Passover/festival context;
- it may have materially increased political/priestly risk.

Strong textual fact:
- every canonical Gospel contains a Temple-action tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact date;
- exact scale;
- exact target;
- whether action was anti-commerce, anti-priestly, anti-sacrificial, eschatological-symbolic, or some combination;
- exact sayings;
- exact institutional response.

## Primary-context controls

The Temple is:
- sacrificial center;
- pilgrimage center;
- economic/logistical institution;
- priestly administrative center;
- politically sensitive under Rome.

Therefore:
vendors/money changers are not automatically evidence of "corruption."

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- treating four canonical forms as four independent witnesses;
- omniscient motive ("he wanted to abolish sacrifice");
- omniscient Caiaphas/Pilate reaction;
- cinematic riot scale unsupported by sources;
- fused Synoptic + Johannine props/details.

Allowed:
- choose one literary framework and declare it;
- portray public disruption at a scale consistent with a symbolic action;
- show uncertainty about how observers interpreted it.

## Manuscript strategy

Book II may narrate one selected scene.

Apparatus must expose:
- selected chronology;
- source dependencies;
- omitted rival details;
- motive uncertainty.
