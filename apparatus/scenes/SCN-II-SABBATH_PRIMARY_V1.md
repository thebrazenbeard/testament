# Scene Packet — Sabbath Controversies V1

Status: PRIMARY_BINDING_ACTIVE / NOT_PROSE_READY
Scene ID: SCN-II-SABBATH

## Primary clusters

### Grain-plucking
- Mark 2:23–28;
- Matthew 12:1–8;
- Luke 6:1–5.

### Withered-hand healing
- Mark 3:1–6;
- Matthew 12:9–14;
- Luke 6:6–11.

Additional Sabbath healings occur in Luke and John and require separate unit review.

## Dependence

Matthew/Luke repetition of Markan material is not independent attestation by default.

Mark 2:27 ("Sabbath made for humankind..." in familiar translation) is absent from the Matthean/Lukan parallels and must not be made the universal key to every Sabbath scene without argument.

## Jewish legal context

The historical question is not:
"Did Jesus choose compassion instead of Judaism?"

It is:
how did Yeshua reason inside Jewish scriptural/halakhic debate about:
- permitted action;
- need;
- healing;
- precedent;
- authority;
- Sabbath purpose?

Maurice Casey argued that the grain-plucking dispute can be reconstructed as intelligible in an Aramaic/Jewish setting:
DOI 10.1017/S0028688500022177.

That is one reconstruction, not automatic historicity.

## Historical minimum

Strong textual fact:
- early Synoptic tradition repeatedly frames Yeshua inside Sabbath disputes.

Plausible:
- some historical Sabbath interpretation/conflict lies behind the tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact incidents;
- exact sayings;
- Mark 2:27 authenticity/status;
- degree of later community redaction.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- Pharisees as anti-human legalists;
- Yeshua "abolishing the Sabbath";
- one Gospel's argument inserted into another scene;
- later Christian Sunday/Sabbath polemic back-projected.

Allowed:
- serious Jewish legal disagreement;
- uncertainty about what specific act was considered prohibited;
- scriptural reasoning and competing values.

## Manuscript strategy

If a reader can summarize the scene as "Jesus versus Jewish law," the scene has failed.
