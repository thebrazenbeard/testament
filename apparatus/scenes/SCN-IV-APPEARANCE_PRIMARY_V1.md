## Historical question

What is the earliest recoverable evidence that Yeshua's followers believed they encountered him after death, and how do later narratives diverge?

## Earliest extant layer

### 1 Corinthians 15:3–8

Paul says he received and transmitted a sequence including:
- death;
- burial;
- raising;
- appearance to Cephas;
- the Twelve;
- a larger group;
- James;
- apostles;
- Paul.

This is earlier extant evidence than the narrative Gospel appearance stories.

Historical ceiling:
- early proclamation and named appearance tradition;
- not direct access to mechanism;
- not an empty-tomb visitor narrative.

## Mark

### Earliest attainable ending control

Current ECM-related textual scholarship reaffirms 16:8 as the earliest attainable ending of Mark.

Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus end at 16:8.

Mark 16:9–20 is important later textual/canonical/reception evidence but must not silently be used as the earliest recoverable Markan appearance narrative.

Control:
- WIT-MARK-SINAITICUS-ENDING
- WIT-MARK-VATICANUS-ENDING

## Matthew

Matthew 28:
- women encounter/appearance tradition;
- strong Galilee orientation;
- commissioning scene.

## Luke

Luke 24:
- Jerusalem-centered sequence;
- Emmaus;
- embodied demonstration/eating;
- instruction to remain in/around Jerusalem in the narrative.

## John

John 20–21:
- Mary Magdalene;
- disciples in Jerusalem;
- Thomas;
- Galilee/Sea of Tiberias epilogue tradition.

John 21's literary relation to the rest of the Gospel remains a compositional question.

## Core contradictions / divergences

- first named appearance: Cephas-priority tradition versus women/Mary traditions;
- appearance geography: Galilee emphasis versus Jerusalem emphasis;
- embodiment details;
- sequence and number of appearances;
- relation between tomb discovery and appearances.

## Minimum defensible reconstruction

Strong:
- resurrection/appearance belief is early;
- Cephas and James are named in early Pauline tradition;
- multiple later communities preserve embodied/revelatory encounter narratives.

Unresolved:
- what generated the experiences;
- exact first appearance;
- exact geography;
- exact sequence;
- physical mechanism;
- whether/how an empty tomb relates causally to appearances.

## Construction budget

Forbidden:
- omniscient resurrection event;
- merged Matthew/Luke/John appearance tour;
- treating Mark 16:9–20 as uncontested original Mark;
- host-level re-instantiation in Book IV;
- diagnosing grief hallucination as fact;
- asserting bodily resurrection mechanism as historical observation.

Allowed:
- source-specific encounter scenes;
- multiple-memory structure;
- phenomenological narration that reports what a character believes they encountered without deciding ontology.

## Manuscript strategy

Book IV follows people and claims.

Book VII may later ask what the same evidence would mean under naturalist, theological, or simulation ontologies.
