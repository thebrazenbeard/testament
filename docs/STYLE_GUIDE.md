# Style and Voice

## Literary register

The prose should feel scriptural without becoming faux-King-James cosplay.

Prefer:
- clean modern English;
- memorable cadence;
- concrete imagery;
- short aphoristic force where appropriate;
- occasional Semitic rhythm when supported by source context;
- human dialogue rather than constant solemnity.

Avoid:
- archaic thee/thou unless a specific scene requires it;
- generic fantasy-Bible language;
- modern technical jargon inside ancient speech;
- making every character secretly understand twenty-first-century philosophy;
- smoothing every contradiction.

## Yeshua

Write him as:
- recognizably Jewish;
- embedded in Galilee/Judea;
- capable of humor, irritation, uncertainty, tenderness, strategic silence, and development;
- not omniscient by authorial convenience unless a scene is explicitly theological rather than historical reconstruction.

## Narrator

The narrator may know the source tradition but should not pretend to know inaccessible private facts.

When necessary, uncertainty can become part of the prose.

## Simulation language

Runtime, controller, root, code, sandbox, model, state, checkpoint, side-channel, and re-instantiation belong to the modern interpretive layer.

Ancient characters do not say those words unless the book explicitly enters a modern/meta frame.

## Sacred tone

Treat believers and traditions seriously without granting any tradition immunity from criticism.
