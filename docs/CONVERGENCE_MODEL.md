# Convergence and Divergence Model

Testament uses convergence to organize evidence, not to manufacture certainty.

## Convergence types

### TEXTUAL_CONVERGENCE
Different sources attest materially similar propositions.

Questions:
- Are they independent?
- Could one depend on another?
- Are they preserving a shared tradition?

### CONTEXTUAL_CONVERGENCE
A narrative claim fits independently known geography, institutions, language, political structure, archaeology, or social practice.

### HOSTILE_CONVERGENCE
A hostile or competing source concedes a limited proposition while disputing its interpretation.

Hostility does not guarantee accuracy.

### MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE
Textual claims intersect with inscriptions, coins, manuscripts, architecture, burial practice, or other material evidence.

### TRADITIONAL_CONVERGENCE
Later communities preserve overlapping memories. This may demonstrate reception history without establishing the original event.

### THEMATIC_CONVERGENCE
Different traditions develop similar structures without established dependence.

This is comparison, not genealogy.

## Divergence types

- incompatible event sequence;
- incompatible genealogy/family claim;
- incompatible geography;
- incompatible chronology;
- incompatible theology;
- incompatible wording;
- incompatible identity;
- incompatible causal explanation.

## Output states

Prefer:
- STRONGLY_CONVERGENT_AT_ASSERTED_LEVEL
- PARTIALLY_CONVERGENT
- DEPENDENCY_LIMITED
- DIVERGENT
- INCOMMENSURABLE
- UNRESOLVED
- NONE_ESTABLISHED

Do not reduce complex ancient evidence to a fake numerical truth score.
