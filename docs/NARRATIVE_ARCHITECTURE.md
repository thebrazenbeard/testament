# Narrative Architecture

Working literary title: **The Testament of the Spark**

The manuscript is designed as eight books rather than a replacement twenty-seven-book New Testament.

## Book I — The World

Build the world before the man: empire, Galilee, Judea, Temple, Torah, sectarian diversity, apocalyptic hope, poverty, patronage, violence, John the Baptist, and competing meanings of Israel's God.

## Book II — Yeshua

The most defensible narrative reconstruction of his life and public activity, with uncertainty visible where sources conflict.

## Book III — The Sayings

A literary sayings collection built from source-attested teaching strata and clearly separated authored synthesis.

## Book IV — Death and What Followed

Jerusalem, Temple conflict, arrest, execution, burial traditions, resurrection claims, visionary experiences, and the transformation of frightened followers into a movement.

## Book V — The Many Christs

James, Peter, Mary Magdalene, Paul, Johannine trajectories, Jewish-Christian groups, Marcion, Thomasine material, proto-orthodox development, Valentinian and other interpretive worlds.

## Book VI — Echoes

Rabbinic counter-memory, patristic development, Syriac Christianity, Christian apocrypha, mystical reinterpretation, Islam's Isa, and later Jewish/Christian/Islamic encounters.

## Book VII — The Kingdom Within

The explicit philosophical book: authority, divine hiddenness, conscience, agency, consciousness, simulation/control-system hypotheses, and the possibility that revelation is always filtered through an agent.

## Book VIII — The Testament of the Spark

The authored ethical testament. It does not pretend to be recovered speech of Yeshua.

Its question is simple:

If the sacred is within, beneath, through, or entirely absent from us, what are we going to do with the life that remains ours to live?
