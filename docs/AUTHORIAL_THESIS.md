# Authorial Thesis

## Central proposition

The book does not need to prove or disprove God.

Its central ethical proposition is:

**No account of God removes human responsibility for the life being lived.**

If reality is created, simulated, emanated, natural, divine, indifferent, conscious, computational, or something outside our categories, agency remains a meaningful problem inside the experienced world.

## The spark

The "spark" is deliberately multivalent.

A reader may understand it as:
- image of God;
- Spirit;
- conscience;
- rational agency;
- participation in divine reality;
- soul;
- Atman-like analogy;
- Buddha-nature-like analogy;
- emergent consciousness;
- information pattern;
- substrate participation;
- internal code shared with reality;
- metaphor for moral authorship.

The book must not falsely collapse these traditions into one doctrine. The literary symbol can converge without claiming historical identity.

## Yeshua's role

The authored reconstruction explores the possibility that Yeshua increasingly redirected divine authority away from external status systems and toward transformed agency, intention, mercy, courage, and responsibility.

Where this exceeds historical evidence it must be labeled interpretation.

## Anti-thesis

The book rejects:
- passive waiting for supernatural rescue as a substitute for action;
- obedience as a substitute for ethics;
- divine command as an exemption from responsibility;
- institutional ownership of the sacred;
- certainty manufactured from missing evidence.

It does not reject religious practice, prayer, mystical experience, or belief merely because they are religious.
