# Historical Yeshua Baseline

Status: INITIAL RECONSTRUCTION / SUBJECT TO ON-THEO REVIEW

This document is a starting constraint for narrative work, not a final historical verdict.

## Strong starting points

At the level broadly accepted in historical scholarship and already compatible with On-Theo's method:

- Yeshua/Jesus was a first-century Jew associated with Galilee.
- He was associated with Yohanan/John the Baptist.
- He became a teacher/preacher with followers.
- The kingdom/reign of God is central in early Jesus tradition.
- He participated in activity that brought him to Jerusalem.
- He was executed by Roman crucifixion under Pontius Pilate.
- His followers continued after his death and very early proclaimed that he had been raised/appeared to them.
- Paul knew leaders of the earlier movement, including Cephas/Peter and James, and wrote before the narrative Gospels.

## Probable but more reconstruction-dependent

- healing/exorcism reputation;
- traditions of legal/halakhic dispute involving Sabbath, purity, status, and authority, some plausibly historical but with exact scope unresolved;
- a disruptive Temple action;
- expectation of divine transformation/judgment;
- women followers/supporters;
- meal and association traditions involving tax collectors/"sinners," with their exact historical scope unresolved;
- some perception by followers or opponents that his activity carried messianic or royal implications.

Each requires source-specific treatment rather than being assumed scene-by-scene.

## Unresolved / contested

- exact birth year and place;
- infancy narratives as biography;
- biological paternity;
- the Panthera/Pandera traditions;
- exact duration of ministry;
- historicity, composition, and symbolic intent of "the Twelve" as a group instituted by Yeshua;
- exact chronology of the final week;
- exact words at trial/execution;
- burial details;
- empty-tomb historicity and sequence;
- nature of resurrection experiences;
- Yeshua's own self-conception;
- whether he expected imminent cosmic intervention, social transformation, both, or something else;
- how much Johannine discourse reflects the historical speaker;
- undocumented childhood/adolescent/young-adult years.

## Panthera guard

Origen preserves Celsus's report of a Panthera paternity accusation. A Roman soldier named Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera is independently attested. These facts do not establish that the soldier was Yeshua's father.

The manuscript may explore stigma, disputed paternity, and countertradition only when it preserves the distinction between attested accusation and biography.

## Narrative rule

Where confidence is low, the narrator may:
- leave the event unresolved;
- present competing memories;
- avoid the scene;
- create explicitly authored connective tissue;
- or dramatize uncertainty itself.

The manuscript must never solve an evidentiary problem merely because a clean story needs an answer.
