## Core distinction

The manuscript should visibly distinguish:

`YESHUA`
from
`JESUS CHRIST`

without turning that distinction into:

`TRUE MAN`
versus
`FALSE MYTH`.

For literary purposes:

**Yeshua** =
the historically reconstructed first-century Jewish man.

**Jesus Christ** =
the layered theological, liturgical, cosmic, ecclesial, and eventually imperial
identity built around that man.

The transition is gradual and begins immediately after his death.

## Linguistic rule

"Christ" is not treated as a surname.

`Iesous Christos` is approximately:
`Jesus the Anointed / Jesus the Messiah`.

By Paul, `Christos` is already functioning so closely with Jesus' identity that
it can behave like an honorific/epithet and sometimes almost like a name.

The manuscript should occasionally remind the reader of that linguistic fact,
because English "Jesus Christ" conceals the original claim inside what sounds
like a full personal name.

## Structural consequence

The book should ask two different questions:

### Book II / historical Yeshua
Who was the man?

### Books III–VI / Jesus Christ
What did his followers, rivals, theologians, institutions, and empires make of
him?

This prevents later Christology from leaking backward into scenes about the
historical Yeshua.

## Suggested transformation interlude

Working title:

**When Yeshua Became Christ**

Sequence:

1. **The Man Dies**
   - Roman crucifixion.
   - followers scatter / grieve / reinterpret.

2. **God Raised Him**
   - earliest resurrection/vindication proclamation.
   - whatever happened historically, this is the point at which biography begins
     to become eschatology.

3. **The Anointed**
   - `Christos` / Messiah language.
   - not surname, but claim.

4. **Paul's Christ**
   - death/resurrection becomes salvation architecture.
   - "in Christ."
   - Adam/Christ.
   - Lord.
   - cosmic language.

5. **Mark's Christ**
   - theology becomes narrative biography.

6. **Matthew and Luke**
   - extraordinary identity reaches back into conception and birth.

7. **John**
   - identity reaches back before creation:
     Logos becomes flesh.

8. **Second-Century Christ**
   - explicit divine Logos.
   - universal salvation.
   - philosophical ontology.

9. **Nicene Christ**
   - metaphysical relation to Father becomes conciliar doctrine.

10. **Imperial Christ**
   - Constantine and successors supply money, architecture, law, councils,
     liturgy, and public power.

## Literary test

Whenever a scene puts later Christology into Yeshua's mouth, ask:

- Is this saying plausibly early?
- Is it first attested in Paul?
- Mark?
- Matthew/Luke?
- John?
- second century?
- later creed?

If the answer is later, the scene must either:
- omit it from Yeshua's historical voice;
- place it in a later narrator/character;
- or label it as deliberate literary/theological construction.

## "Myth" rule

The manuscript may use "myth" only with explicit meaning.

Preferred sense:
**a sacred/theological identity and narrative that exceeds recoverable
biography**.

Avoid using "myth" as shorthand for:
"obviously false."

The manuscript's question is stronger than that:

> Which claims belong to the man, which belong to the earliest interpretation of
> the man, and which belong to the centuries that needed him to become something
> larger?

## Constantine placement

Constantine comes late.

He is not the point where Yeshua becomes Christ.

He is the point where one mature Christian Christ can increasingly become:
- imperial;
- monumental;
- legally privileged;
- calendrically standardized;
- institutionally amplified.

So the manuscript's arc should be:

`YESHUA -> CHRIST -> COSMIC CHRIST -> IMPERIAL CHRIST`

not:

`YESHUA -> CONSTANTINE INVENTS CHRIST`.

## Literary payoff

The book should let the reader feel the transformation.

At first:
a Jewish man speaks.

Then:
people say God raised him.

Then:
they call him the Anointed.

Then:
his death becomes cosmic.

Then:
his birth becomes miraculous.

Then:
his existence begins before creation.

Then:
philosophers define his ontology.

Then:
bishops define acceptable language about him.

Then:
emperors build in his name.

The question should never be reduced to:
"Which step was the lie?"

The harder question is:

> At which step are we no longer reconstructing Yeshua's life and instead
> reconstructing the history of what people believed Yeshua meant?


## Revised backbone: two different mysteries

The research now separates two questions that the manuscript must never collapse.

### Mystery One — How Yeshua became Christ

Critical interval:
`~30–55 CE`.

By Paul's surviving letters, the movement already contains inherited:
- Christ/Messiah language;
- resurrection/vindication proclamation;
- salvific interpretation of death;
- direct Lord invocation, including Aramaic `Maranatha`;
- expected return;
- powerful Son/Lord language.

Paul then gives us the earliest surviving extensive architecture:
- `in Christ`;
- Adam/Christ;
- participation in death/resurrection;
- eschatological rule;
- creation/redemption language.

This is the project's highest-value origin problem.

### Mystery Two — Which Christ won

Longer interval:
`~70–400 CE`.

This includes:
- Gospel narrativization;
- infancy Christologies;
- Logos Christology;
- rival second-century Christologies;
- four-Gospel consolidation;
- third-century metaphysics;
- Nicaea;
- imperial selection pressure.

Constantine belongs here.

## Do not write a simple ascent ladder

The literary structure must not imply:

`ordinary man -> slightly divine -> very divine -> Nicene God`.

The upstream research shows branching:
- resurrection/exaltation;
- Lord devotion;
- Adamic Christology;
- baptismal/Spirit sonship;
- preexistence;
- Logos;
- recapitulation;
- Marcionite dualism;
- Valentinian cosmology;
- eternal-generation theology.

Some branches disappear.
Some merge.
Some are rejected.
Some become orthodox.

The reader should see competition, not inevitability.

## Claim-clock visual logic

For each major claim, the manuscript apparatus should show four things:

`CLAIM`
`EARLIEST RECOVERABLE SOURCE`
`CAN IT BE TRACED TO YESHUA?`
`LATER AMPLIFICATION`

Example:

**Virgin conception**
- earliest clear sources: Matthew/Luke;
- not present in undisputed Paul or Mark;
- historical status unresolved;
- later becomes creedal/orthodox.

**Preexistent Logos**
- earliest clear source: John;
- historical-Yeshua self-claim not established;
- amplified by Justin and later metaphysical theology.

**Jesus as Christ**
- already pre-Pauline;
- possible Yeshua self-identification unresolved;
- becomes foundational Christian identity before any Gospel survives.

This apparatus should visually teach the reader not to confuse:
`EARLY`
with
`HISTORICAL`,
or:
`LATE`
with
`FALSE`.

## Strongest literary transition

The manuscript should make the reader feel that something has already happened
before Paul appears.

Possible structural line:

> Paul did not meet a dead Galilean teacher and decide to call him Christ.
> He entered a movement that was already doing it.

Then immediately complicate it:

> What Paul inherited and what Paul transformed are not the same question.

That is the transition the research now needs to reconstruct.
