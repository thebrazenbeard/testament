- Constantine did not create Christianity.
- Nicaea did not select the 27-book New Testament.
- Constantine did directly patronize Christian institutions, intervene in

  sacred Scriptures.

  the modern canon.

  Christian memories competed.

This is not a conspiracy claim.

## Literary thesis

The strongest formulation is:

> Rome did not need to invent Yeshua in order to inherit him.

Constantine does not solve the first-century mystery.

He creates a different historical fact:

> Once the empire began paying for churches, transporting bishops, adjudicating
> schisms, commissioning scriptures, and rewarding ecclesial unity, Christian
> interpretation acquired an imperial political environment it had never
> possessed before.

## Relation to the Roman-cultivation hypothesis

Constantine should function as the strongest **control case**, not as retroactive
proof.

He demonstrates:
`IMPERIAL_CAPTURE_WITHOUT_ORIGINAL_INVENTION_IS HISTORICALLY REAL`.

He does not establish:
`TIBERIUS STARTED THE PROJECT`.

This allows Testament to ask the larger question without pretending the answer
is known:

> If an empire can adopt and reshape a religion three centuries after its
> founder dies, how much of what later believers inherit belongs to the founder,
> and how much belongs to the institutions that learned how to carry his name?

## Canon narrative

The canon story should be staged after Constantine, not collapsed into Nicaea.

Important dates:
- 325: Nicaea — doctrinal/disciplinary council, not New Testament canon vote.
- 330s: imperial commission for fifty Scripture copies.
- 367: Athanasius' Festal Letter gives the first surviving exact 27-book New
  Testament list matching the modern collection.
- later fourth-century regional decisions and usage continue consolidation.

This chronology is dramatically stronger than the false "Constantine picked the
Bible" story because it shows canon becoming stable inside a rapidly changing
institutional ecology.
