# Names, Languages, and Identity

## Primary naming rule

Use the name appropriate to narrative layer and linguistic setting.

Possible forms include:
- Yeshua — common modern reconstruction of Hebrew/Aramaic name form;
- Iesous — Greek textual form;
- Jesus — normal English form;
- Jesus Christ — later confessional/title form;
- Mashiach / Messiah — Hebrew-derived title;
- Christos / Christ — Greek title;
- Isa — Qur'anic/Arabic form;
- Miriam / Mary / Maryam — language/tradition dependent.

No one form is allowed to imply superior historicity merely by sounding older.

## Panthera / Pandera

Panthera, Pantera, Pandera, Pandeira, Stada, and related forms must remain source-specific.

Do not call the historical subject "Yeshua Panthera" as an established personal name.

## Divine names

YHWH, Elohim, El, Adonai, Theos, Deus, Allah and mystical divine designations are not automatically interchangeable in historical analysis.

The literary text may intentionally create symbolic convergence only when the apparatus records the move.

## Translation discipline

Important source terms should retain a lexical note when translation materially changes theology.

Priority terms include:
- malkut / basileia — kingdom/reign;
- ruach / pneuma — spirit/breath/wind;
- mashiach / christos — anointed/messiah/christ;
- ben-adam / huios tou anthropou — son of man/human one;
- torah / nomos — instruction/law;
- metanoia / teshuvah analogies — repentance/turning;
- logos — word/reason/discourse, never silently "code";
- sheol / hades / gehenna;
- resurrection vocabulary;
- faith/trust/belief terms.

## Identity rule

A title used by followers, opponents, narrator, later church, rabbinic source, Qur'an, or modern author may describe different propositions.

"Jesus is Messiah" and "followers called Jesus Messiah" are not the same claim.
