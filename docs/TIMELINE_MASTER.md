# Master Timeline

Status: ORIENTATION / DATE RANGES PROVISIONAL

Dates are approximate unless an exact year is strongly established. Composition date and surviving witness date remain separate.

## Deep background

- Persian period: major development/redaction of Jewish scriptural and legal traditions.
- Hellenistic period after Alexander (late 4th century BCE onward): intensified Greek/Jewish cultural interaction.
- 3rd–1st centuries BCE: Septuagint translation history develops over time, not in one event.
- 3rd century BCE–1st century CE: Dead Sea Scrolls manuscript horizon broadly spans late Second Temple Judaism.
- 2nd century BCE: Maccabean revolt and Hasmonean state reshape Jewish political/religious life.
- 63 BCE: Pompey intervenes in Jerusalem; Roman dominance becomes decisive.
- 37–4 BCE: reign of Herod the Great.

## Yeshua horizon

- Late 1st century BCE / early 1st century CE: birth traditions place Yeshua before Herod's death, while Luke's census framing creates a chronological tension with the later Quirinius census.
- 4 BCE: death of Herod the Great.
- 6 CE: Judaea reorganized under direct Roman administration; census under Quirinius.
- 20s CE: John the Baptist movement.
- 26–36 CE: Pontius Pilate governs Judaea.
- Late 20s / early 30s CE: Yeshua's public activity.
- c. 30 or 33 CE: leading crucifixion chronologies; exact year remains debated.

## Earliest movement

- 30s CE: Jerusalem movement associated with Peter/Cephas, James, and other followers.
- 30s CE: Paul's conversion/call belongs after the movement already exists.
- 40s–50s CE: expanding gentile missions and disputes about Torah, circumcision, table fellowship, and identity.
- c. 50–60 CE: major undisputed Pauline letters.
- 60s CE: deaths/traditions concerning major first-generation leaders; exact details vary.
- 66–73 CE: First Jewish-Roman War.
- 70 CE: destruction of the Jerusalem Temple.

## Gospel and early Christian textual horizon

- c. 65–75 CE: common scholarly range for Mark.
- c. 80–95 CE: common ranges for Matthew and Luke-Acts.
- c. 90–110 CE: broad range often assigned to final Johannine forms, with possible earlier strata.
- 93/94 CE: Josephus completes Jewish Antiquities.
- late 1st / early 2nd century: Didache, 1 Clement and other early church-order/reception materials occupy overlapping debated ranges.
- early 2nd century: Ignatius, Polycarp traditions, Papias fragments, continued gospel formation and competition.
- 2nd century: Marcion, Justin, Valentinian trajectories, expanding apocryphal gospel/acts/apocalypse production.
- c. 170–180 CE: common broad placement of Celsus's True Doctrine.
- c. 180 CE: Irenaeus's anti-heretical synthesis.
- c. 200 CE: Mishnah redaction conventionally placed around this period.
- 3rd century: Origen and major textual/theological systematization.
- c. 248 CE: Origen's Contra Celsum preserves/rebuts Celsus.

## Late antique reception

- 3rd–4th centuries: many surviving papyrus/codex witnesses of canonical and noncanonical Christian works.
- 4th century: Nag Hammadi codices copied; many contained compositions are earlier than the manuscripts.
- 325 CE: Council of Nicaea.
- 4th century: Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus.
- 367 CE: Athanasius's Festal Letter lists the twenty-seven-book New Testament familiar today.
- 4th–6th centuries: rabbinic and Christian textual traditions continue extensive redaction, commentary, and expansion.
- 5th–6th centuries: Babylonian Talmud reaches major redactional forms.
- early 7th century: Qur'anic proclamation and formation.
- 7th century onward: Islamic Jesus traditions develop through tafsir, hadith, historiography, polemic, and mysticism.

## Later mystical reception

- late antique/early medieval: Hekhalot/Merkavah textual complexes develop.
- Sefer Yetzirah: dating and redaction debated; later antique/early medieval textual history.
- 12th–13th centuries: Sefer ha-Bahir and major medieval Kabbalistic development.
- late 13th century onward: Zoharic corpus.

These later mystical texts illuminate reception and comparative metaphysics, not first-century biography.
