## 1 Corinthians 7:10 — transcription/apparatus collation

Critical comparison text contains the infinitive:
- `... γυναῖκα ἀπὸ ἀνδρὸς μὴ χωρισθῆναι`

P46 is reported with the distinctive imperative:
- `χωριζεσθω`

This is a real witness-specific textual difference, not merely orthographic normalization.

A published textual study explicitly distinguishes the three early forms:
- `χωρισθηναι`;
- `χωριζεσθαι`;
- P46 `χωριζεσθω`.

The same study interprets P46's imperative as possible assimilation to the nearby imperative sequence (`μενετω`, `καταλλαγητω`, and later imperatives). That is a scholarly explanation of the variant, not an observed scribal motive.

Current evidence typing:
- P46 `χωριζεσθω`: **PUBLISHED_TRANSCRIPTION_APPARATUS_EVIDENCE**;
- direct visual confirmation of the letters on folio 45v: **PENDING**.

## 1 Corinthians 7:11 — transcription/apparatus collation

Published P46 comparison data report the sequence:
- `εαν δε και χωρισθη`;
- `μενετω αγαμος`;
- `η τω ανδρι`;
- `καταλλαγητω`;
- `και ανδρα γυναικα μη ...`.

An online witness-comparison table transcribes the final infinitive for P46 as `αφειεναι`, while modern critical text normally prints `ἀφιέναι`.

## Historical consequence

P46 remains strong physical evidence for the early transmission of Paul's 1 Corinthians 7 Lord-command passage.

The witness-specific `χωριζεσθω` variant demonstrates textual variation inside that transmission. It does not recover Yeshua's exact divorce wording, establish which Synoptic formulation Paul knew, or convert P46 into independent historical attestation of a Gospel saying.
