# Luke 11:2 — Gregory of Nyssa Spirit-Petition Collation V1

Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY GREEK PATRISTIC TEXT BOUND / GOSPEL ATTRIBUTION EXPLICIT / NOT A PHYSICAL GOSPEL MANUSCRIPT**

## Scope

This control binds Gregory of Nyssa's direct testimony to the Luke 11:2 Holy-Spirit petition family.

It does not close the direct image-collation debt for Greek minuscules 162 or 700.

## Gregory's explicit attribution to Luke

Immediately before the variant wording Gregory writes that the same thought is interpreted more clearly by Luke and says that in that Gospel, instead of `Ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου`, the alternative follows.

Direct Greek attribution context:
`Ἢ τάχα, καθὼς ἡμῖν ὑπὸ τοῦ Λουκᾶ τὸ αὐτὸ νόημα σαφέστερον ἑρμηνεύεται ... οὕτω γὰρ ἐν ἐκείνῳ τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ φησὶν, ἀντὶ τοῦ, Ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου ...`

Evidence consequence:
- Gregory is not merely making a free theological association between Spirit and kingdom;
- he explicitly presents the Spirit petition as what Luke has in place of the kingdom petition.

That establishes Gregory's patristic textual testimony.

It does **not** establish what the autograph of Luke read.

## Direct Gregory quotation

The synchronized Greek continues:

`Ἐλθέτω τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμά σου ἐφ' ἡμᾶς καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς.`

Normalized accent/apostrophe presentation:

`Ἐλθέτω τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμά σου ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς.`

Functional translation:

`Let your Holy Spirit come upon us and cleanse us.`

## Gregory's own interpretation

Gregory immediately argues:

`ὅπερ γὰρ ὁ Λουκᾶς πνεῦμα ἅγιον λέγει, τοῦτο Ματθαῖος βασιλείαν ὠνόμασεν.`

That is, what Luke calls Holy Spirit, Matthew calls kingdom.

Evidence typing:
- the quoted Spirit petition = patristic textual testimony attributed by Gregory to Luke;
- Gregory's Spirit/kingdom equivalence = Gregory's theological/exegetical interpretation.

Do not collapse those two evidence classes.

## Internal wording control

Later in the same homily Gregory repeats:

`Ἐλθέτω τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμά σου ... καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς.`

Near the homily's conclusion he gives a sermonic exhortation with changed order:

`Ἀλλ' ἐλθέτω ἐφ' ἡμᾶς τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς ...`

That concluding exhortation is Gregory's prose use of the petition, not a second independent Gospel witness.

## Relation to manuscripts 700 and 162

Published textual commentary distinguishes:
- 700: a fuller form with `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς`;
- 162: a different possessive order and no `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς`.

Gregory's explicitly attributed form includes `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς` and therefore aligns more closely with the fuller 700-type form at that feature.

However:
- Gregory is centuries earlier than the surviving minuscules;
- wording similarity does not make Gregory a physical ancestor of 700;
- Gregory's testimony is an independent patristic evidence class, not a manuscript-image collation;
- the exact relationship among Gregory's exemplar, 700, 162, liturgical transmission, and other lost witnesses remains historical inference.

## Claim ceiling

Established:
- Gregory explicitly attributes a Spirit-for-kingdom petition to Luke;
- Gregory's direct Greek form is recoverable;
- his attributed form contains `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς` and a purification clause;
- Gregory explicitly interprets Luke's Spirit and Matthew's kingdom as corresponding.

Not established:
- that Gregory's form is original Luke;
- that Gregory copied directly from an extant ancestor of 700 or 162;
- that 700's or 162's manuscript pixels have been directly collated by Testament;
- that Marcion had this exact kingdom-substitution form;
- that theological suitability to Luke's pneumatology proves textual originality.
