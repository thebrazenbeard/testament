# Luke 11:2 — Maximus the Confessor Spirit-Petition Collation V1

Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY GREEK PATRISTIC TEXT BOUND / EVANGELIST ATTRIBUTION EXPLICIT / INDEPENDENCE FROM GREGORY UNRESOLVED**

## Scope

This control binds Maximus the Confessor's direct use of the Luke 11:2 Holy-Spirit petition family in his exposition of the Lord's Prayer.

It does not count Maximus as an independent Gospel-manuscript witness and does not close the direct image-collation debt for 162/700.

## Work and locus

Author: Maximus the Confessor
Work: *Orationis Dominicae brevis expositio* / short exposition of the Lord's Prayer
Patrologia Graeca: PG 90, 884B–C
Direct PG route:

## Direct Maximus text

Maximus comments on Matthew's `ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου` and writes:

`Ὃ γὰρ ἐνταῦθα Ματθαῖος φησι βασιλείαν, ἀλλαχοῦ τῶν εὐαγγελιστῶν ἕτερος Πνεῦμα κέκληκεν ἅγιον, φάσκων, Ἐλθέτω σου τὸ Πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς.`

Functional translation:

`For what Matthew here calls kingdom, elsewhere another of the evangelists has called Holy Spirit, saying: Let your Holy Spirit come and cleanse us.`

## Evidence consequence

Directly established:
- Maximus explicitly compares Matthew's kingdom petition with another evangelist's Holy-Spirit petition;
- his quoted form is `Ἐλθέτω σου τὸ Πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς`;
- his form lacks `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς`;
- the possessive precedes `τὸ Πνεῦμα`, unlike Gregory's directly bound attributed form.

Maximus says `another of the evangelists` rather than naming Luke in this sentence.

The identification with Luke 11:2 is supported by the textual tradition and the patristic comparison, but Testament preserves Maximus's actual wording rather than silently inserting Luke's name into his sentence.

## Relation to Gregory of Nyssa

Gregory's directly bound attributed form:
`Ἐλθέτω τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμά σου ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς`.

Maximus's direct form:
`Ἐλθέτω σου τὸ Πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, καὶ καθαρισάτω ἡμᾶς`.

They share the Spirit-for-kingdom substitution and purification clause but differ in word order and the presence/absence of `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς`.

The PG apparatus on Maximus notes:
- a comparison with Gregory of Nyssa's third Lord's Prayer homily;
- the editorial suggestion that Maximus appears to have drawn from Gregory.

Evidence ceiling:
- possible literary dependence of Maximus on Gregory is a published editorial judgment;
- dependence is **not independently demonstrated** by Testament;
- Maximus therefore adds direct patristic textual evidence, but Testament does not count Gregory + Maximus as two securely independent transmission streams.

## Relation to manuscripts 162 and 700

Published manuscript controls:
- 700 includes `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς`;
- 162 lacks `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς` and has a possessive-before-Spirit order.

Maximus's form is therefore closer to 162 in those two features.

This is a local wording comparison only.

It does not establish:
- that Maximus copied 162 or its ancestor;
- that 162 descends from Maximus;
- that the same exemplar underlies Maximus and 162;
- that 162's manuscript pixels have been directly collated by Testament.

## PG apparatus variant

The PG apparatus also notes that one `Reg.` codex has `ἐπελθέτω` instead of `Ἐλθέτω`.

Testament records that as an edition-apparatus variant in the Maximus textual tradition, not as a Gospel variant witness.

## Claim ceiling

Established:
- Maximus directly preserves a Spirit-petition form in his Lord's Prayer exposition;
- he presents it as wording from another evangelist in contrast with Matthew's kingdom language;
- exact Maximus form is directly recoverable from PG 90:884B;
- his form lacks `ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς` and differs from Gregory's direct form.

Not established:
- independent transmission from Gregory;
- original Luke;
- a direct genealogical relationship with 162 or 700;
- Marcion's exact wording;
- manuscript-image verification of 162/700.
