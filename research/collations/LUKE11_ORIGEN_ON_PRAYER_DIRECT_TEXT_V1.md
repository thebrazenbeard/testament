# Luke 11:2–4 — Origen, De oratione Direct Text Collation V1

Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY GREEK PATRISTIC TEXT BOUND / SHORTER LUKAN PRAYER FORM / NOT A PHYSICAL GOSPEL MANUSCRIPT**

## Scope

This control binds Origen's own Greek presentation of the Lukan Lord's Prayer in *De oratione* rather than relying on a modern summary of his testimony.

It is patristic textual testimony and exegesis.
It is not a physical Gospel manuscript and does not by itself establish Luke's autograph.

## Origen's side-by-side prayer quotation

In *De oratione* 18.2 Origen explicitly introduces Matthew and Luke and prints their prayer forms separately.

His Lukan form is:

`πάτερ, ἁγιασθήτω τὸ ὄνομά σου· ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου· τὸν ἄρτον ἡμῶν τὸν ἐπιούσιον δίδου ἡμῖν τὸ καθ' ἡμέραν· καὶ ἄφες ἡμῖν τὰς ἁμαρτίας ἡμῶν, καὶ γὰρ αὐτοὶ ἀφίεμεν παντὶ τῷ ὀφείλοντι ἡμῖν· καὶ μὴ εἰσενέγκῃς ἡμᾶς εἰς πειρασμόν.`

Directly visible features:
- opening address: `πάτερ` — simple Father;
- kingdom petition present;
- no Matthean `γενηθήτω τὸ θέλημά σου ...` will petition;
- Luke's sins/debtors wording is preserved;
- the quoted Lukan form ends with the testing/temptation petition;
- no deliverance-from-evil clause follows in Origen's quoted Luke form.

## Origen's explicit will-petition control

In his discussion of the Matthean will petition, Origen explicitly says:

`ὁ Λουκᾶς μετὰ τὸ ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου ταῦτα παρασιωπήσας ἔταξε· τὸν ἄρτον ἡμῶν τὸν ἐπιούσιον δίδου ἡμῖν καθ' ἡμέραν`

Evidence consequence:
- Origen does not merely omit the will petition accidentally in one quotation;
- he explicitly identifies it as absent from Luke relative to Matthew.

## Origen's explicit deliverance-petition control

In *De oratione* 29.1 Origen quotes:

`Καὶ μὴ εἰσενέγκῃς ἡμᾶς εἰς πειρασμὸν, ἀλλὰ ῥῦσαι ἡμᾶς ἀπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ`

and then states:

`τὸ δὲ ἀλλὰ ῥῦσαι ἡμᾶς ἀπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ παρὰ τῷ Λουκᾷ σεσιώπηται.`

That directly establishes Origen's testimony that Luke omits the deliverance clause.

## Opening-address control

Origen later explicitly discusses Luke's instruction:

`ὅταν προσεύχησθε, λέγετε· πάτερ`

and identifies it as written in Luke.

Therefore his simple-Father evidence is not merely reconstructed from a modern critical text.

## Evidence class

`DIRECT_PATRISTIC_TEXTUAL_TESTIMONY_AND_EXEGESIS`

Origen directly witnesses a shorter Lukan prayer form at three relevant points:
- simple `Father` opening;
- omission of the Matthean will petition;
- omission of the deliverance-from-evil petition.

## Historical ceiling

Established:
- Origen's own Greek text presents and discusses a shorter Lukan prayer form;
- his quotation independently preserves the relevant wording distinctions within his patristic text;
- he explicitly distinguishes Matthew's and Luke's forms rather than treating them as verbally identical.

Not established:
- that every feature of Origen's Lukan form is the autograph of Luke;
- that Origen's Gospel exemplar is textually independent of every surviving manuscript stream;
- that the shorter form is historically original merely because Origen is early;
- that Origen preserves the exact words spoken by Yeshua.

## Transmission guard

Patristic quotation can preserve valuable textual evidence while still being subject to:
- author's citation practice;
- memory;
- harmonization;
- exemplar variation;
- exegetical framing.

Accordingly, Origen is a materially useful independent evidence class from physical manuscripts, but not a substitute for them.
