# Luke 17:21 — Tertullian, Adversus Marcionem IV.35 V1

Status: **DIRECT CRITICAL LATIN PATRISTIC TEXT BOUND / RECEPTION-EXEGESIS CONTROL / NOT GOSPEL MANUSCRIPT EVIDENCE**

## Scope

This control binds Tertullian's own Latin exegesis of Luke 17:21 rather than relying on a prose summary of his position.

## Work and locus

- author: Tertullian
- work: *Adversus Marcionem* / *Against Marcion*
- book/chapter: IV.35
- critical-text route: Ernest Evans, *Adversus Marcionem*, Book IV, hosted at Tertullian.org with permission of Oxford University Press
- direct Latin route: `https://www.tertullian.org/articles/evans_marc/evans_marc_09book4.htm`

## Direct Latin

Tertullian quotes the Lukan wording and immediately interprets it:

`ecce enim regnum dei intra vos est. Quis non ita interpretabitur: Intra vos est, id est in manu, in potestate vestra, si audiatis, si faciatis dei praeceptum?`

Functional translation:

`The kingdom of God is within you ... that is, in your hand, in your power, if you hear and do God's commandment.`

## Deuteronomy control inside Tertullian's argument

Tertullian then connects this with the near-at-hand commandment tradition, paraphrasing the word as near, in the mouth, heart and hands, and returns to the Luke 17:21 wording.

Evidence consequence:
- Tertullian's `intra vos` is not treated by him as requiring a purely psychological 'inside the individual soul' interpretation;
- he explicitly glosses it as practical availability / agency: `in manu, in potestate vestra`;
- this creates an ancient reception category distinct from both a simple spatial 'among you' reading and an exclusively interior-mystical reading.

## Evidence class

`DIRECT_PATRISTIC_TEXTUAL_EXEGESIS`

Tertullian is:
- an ancient interpreter of Luke;
- evidence for Latin reception and exegetical possibilities;
- not a physical Gospel manuscript;
- not a lexical authority capable of overriding the Greek by fiat.

## Relationship to the Greek dispute

Established:
- Tertullian knew a Latin form equivalent to `intra vos`;
- he explicitly interpreted it as `in manu, in potestate vestra`;
- the reach/power family of interpretation is ancient, not a modern invention.

Not established:
- that Luke's Greek `ἐντὸς ὑμῶν` must mean 'within your power';
- that Tertullian preserves the historical wording or interpretation of Yeshua;
- that his Deuteronomy linkage was Luke's own intended intertext;
- that this settles the within/among dispute.

## Project-bias guard

Because Testament's authored agency thesis has an obvious affinity with Tertullian's 'in your power' reception, that thematic fit cannot increase the historical weight of the evidence.

Book III may present Tertullian as an ancient reception witness to an agency/reach reading, but must continue to preserve the lexical and contextual dispute over Luke 17:21.
