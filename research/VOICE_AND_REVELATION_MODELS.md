# Voice, Revelation, and Control-System Models

Status: CROSS-DISCIPLINARY RESEARCH + SPECULATIVE MODEL

## Historical/phenomenological starting point

Humans report:
- voices;
- visions;
- dreams;
- sensed presences;
- sudden convictions;
- revelations;
- possession;
- divine/demonic commands;
- radio/technological interpretations in modern settings.

The experience can be sincere regardless of causal explanation.

## Cultural interpretation model

A similar unexplained internal percept may be labeled using available ontology:
- god;
- angel;
- demon;
- ancestor;
- spirit;
- witchcraft;
- telepathy;
- radio;
- CIA;
- aliens;
- simulation controller.

Interpretation is evidence about the experiencer's model; it is not by itself evidence for the proposed external cause.

## Physical side-channel analogies

Known physics includes routes where non-obvious physical processes create internally perceived effects.

Examples for research:
- CRT horizontal-scan acoustic whine as a real machine side-channel some individuals detect more readily than others;
- microwave auditory/Frey effect as a physical route from pulsed RF exposure to internally perceived acoustic phenomena;
- implant/conductor interactions with electromagnetic fields in specific engineered conditions.

These establish that "no obvious external speaker" does not logically entail "no physical stimulus."

They do not establish that religious voices are radio reception.

## Predictive-controller hypothesis

Hypothetical loop:

1. controller predicts agent will perform X;
2. controller injects warning/correction;
3. agent experiences correction through available cognitive UI;
4. warning changes salience and behavior;
5. intervention becomes causal input to X;
6. controller's attempted prevention contributes to predicted event.

This is self-fulfilling intervention, not necessarily backwards time travel.

## Withdrawal-patch hypothesis

If direct interventions repeatedly contaminate the system, a controller may:
- disable them;
- reduce bandwidth;
- move below conscious awareness;
- restrict intervention conditions.

Internal cultural memory might then describe an earlier age of active gods followed by divine silence/hiddenness.

## Required countermodels

Every use must compare:
- neurological voice hearing;
- nonclinical voice experience;
- psychosis-spectrum phenomena without diagnosing individuals;
- sleep transitions;
- grief;
- trauma;
- drugs/toxins;
- temporal-lobe and seizure phenomena;
- cultural expectation;
- social reinforcement;
- confabulation and source-monitoring error.

The simulation explanation remains speculative unless it produces discriminating evidence.
