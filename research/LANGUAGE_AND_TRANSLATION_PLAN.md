# Language and Translation Plan

The project is written in English but its subject is multilingual.

## Relevant languages

- Hebrew;
- Aramaic varieties;
- Koine Greek;
- Latin;
- Coptic;
- Syriac;
- Ethiopic/Ge'ez for preserved traditions;
- Armenian/Georgian where they preserve important versions;
- Arabic for Qur'anic and Islamic reception.

## Translation hierarchy

For consequential wording:
1. identify original/source language;
2. identify critical edition;
3. inspect major variants;
4. compare multiple scholarly translations;
5. record semantic range;
6. only then choose literary English.

## Yeshua's speech

The Gospels preserve Greek literary texts.

Even if Yeshua normally taught in Aramaic, the project cannot pretend it has his exact Aramaic sentences unless independently preserved.

Back-translation can be used as a linguistic hypothesis only.

## High-risk terms

Maintain dedicated lexical packets for:
- kingdom/reign;
- spirit;
- Son of Man;
- Messiah/Christ;
- faith/trust;
- repentance/turning;
- righteousness/justice;
- sin;
- forgiveness/release/debt;
- Gehenna/Hades/Sheol;
- eternal/age-related language;
- Logos;
- soul/life;
- resurrection/rising.

## Literary rule

Readable English is allowed.

Invisible theology smuggled through translation is not.
