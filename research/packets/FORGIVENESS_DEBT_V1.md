## Debt is not merely metaphor

Matthew's prayer uses debt/debtor language.

Luke explicitly says "sins" while retaining "everyone indebted to us" in the reciprocal phrase.

This is evidence that financial and moral semantic fields were close enough to interact.

Benjamin Wold's 2022 study explicitly places Matthean debt remission within Jewish wisdom/economic traditions.

Reference:
- https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009305082.007

## Power and hierarchy

A 2023 *Journal of Theological Studies* article revisits the argument that New Testament forgiveness language can encode social hierarchy: creditors, rulers and those with power forgive downward.

Reference:
- "Forgiveness and Social Hierarchy in the New Testament":
  https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/74/2/493/7256066

Testament should therefore avoid reducing forgiveness to a purely private therapeutic feeling.

## Distinct questions

Keep separate:
- release of monetary debt;
- release of moral offense;
- divine forgiveness;
- interpersonal forgiveness;
- reconciliation;
- restoration of relationship;
- refusal of vengeance.

These are not synonyms.

## Ethical guard

Forgiveness must not be written as:
- denial that harm occurred;
- compulsory reconciliation;
- restoration of access to an unsafe person;
- cancellation of accountability.

Those are modern ethical distinctions, but they prevent the final book from weaponizing ancient forgiveness language.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:

Unresolved:

## Book VIII consequence

Forgiveness can be interpreted as ending a causal chain of retaliation without erasing truth, boundary or consequence.

That systems framing is authored theology, not a recovered first-century technical definition.
