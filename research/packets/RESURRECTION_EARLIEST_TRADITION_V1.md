# Earliest Resurrection Tradition V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## What history can study

History has access to:
- texts claiming Yeshua was raised;
- formulas/traditions transmitted by Paul;
- reports of appearances;
- later tomb narratives;
- the behavior and development of communities convinced something decisive occurred.

History does not have direct observational access to "the resurrection event" itself.

## Earliest extant layer

Paul's letters are the earliest extant Christian writings.

1 Corinthians 15:3–8 transmits a received tradition:
- death;
- burial;
- raising on the third day;
- appearance to Cephas;
- the Twelve;
- a larger group;
- James;
- apostles;
- and finally Paul.

A Cambridge overview states that the formula may date to the first decade after the crucifixion.

Reference:
- Markus Bockmuehl, "Resurrection":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-companion-to-jesus/resurrection/E61713771E1098825FA969DC87B4E11D

## Appearance divergence

Later sources do not preserve one uniform appearance chronology.

A *New Testament Studies* discussion identifies at least two competing first-appearance trajectories:
- Cephas/Peter priority;
- women/Mary Magdalene priority.

Reference:
- G. W. Trompf, "The First Resurrection Appearance and the Ending of Mark's Gospel":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/first-resurrection-appearance-and-the-ending-of-marks-gospel/52F96AC9AFC9660277D5C25D59C5E099

This divergence is data.

## Explanatory models to retain

Historical/theological literature includes:
- bodily resurrection;
- visions/revelatory experiences;
- bereavement/grief-related experiences;
- legendary development;
- social-memory processes;
- combinations.

Oxford's interdisciplinary survey explicitly notes the difficulty of discriminating objective/subjective vision categories from the surviving evidence.

Reference:
- Gerald O'Collins, "The Resurrection: The State of the Questions":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/12487/chapter-abstract/163184781

## Simulation model

Host-level re-instantiation is only a modal possibility under an already-granted simulation premise.

It adds no historical evidence.

Therefore, "re-instantiation is possible within the model" does not imply "Yeshua was historically re-instantiated."

## Manuscript consequence

Book IV narrates claims, experiences, grief, changed behavior and competing memories.

Book VII may ask what resurrection would mean under other ontologies.
