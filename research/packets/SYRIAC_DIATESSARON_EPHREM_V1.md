# Syriac Christianity, Diatessaron, and Ephrem V1

Status: BOOK_VI_RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Why Syriac reception matters

Greek and Latin Christianity do not exhaust early Christian reception.

Syriac-speaking communities preserve major:
- Gospel versions;
- harmonies;
- poetry;
- commentary;
- liturgy;
- ascetic traditions;
- Christological interpretation.

## Diatessaron

Tatian's late-second-century Diatessaron combines material from Matthew, Mark, Luke and John into one Gospel harmony.

A major recent Oxford study emphasizes that its original text is difficult to reconstruct because the work survives through multiple descendant traditions rather than one complete original manuscript.

Reference:
- William L. Petersen / later Diatessaron research represented by:
  *Tatian's Diatessaron: Composition, Redaction, Recension, and Reception*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/59230

The 2025 Oxford Handbook of textual criticism similarly stresses that for the Diatessaron the task is not recovering one simple manuscript but analyzing multiple textual traditions.

Reference:
- "The Syriac and Christian Palestinian Aramaic Versions of the New Testament":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61539/chapter-abstract/537143991

## Syriac use

The Diatessaron had major Syriac reception and was used liturgically in some communities.

A 2021 JTS study notes:
- Syriac use;
- Aphrahat;
- Ephrem's commentary;
- later displacement by the Peshitta.

Reference:
- "Tatian's Diatessaron: The Arabic Version, the Dura Europos Fragment, and the Women Witnesses":
  https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/72/1/192/6472569

## Ephrem

Ephrem's biblical theology uses:
- poetry;
- symbol;
- typology;
- paradox;
- liturgy;
rather than merely prose commentary.

Sebastian Brock's Oxford survey emphasizes Syriac exegesis as its own interpretive world rather than a derivative eastern footnote.

Reference:
- "Biblical Exegesis in the Syriac Churches":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/41986/chapter-abstract/355428401

## Historical ceiling

Syriac sources are high-value evidence for:
- reception;
- textual transmission;
- harmonization;
- theology;
- liturgy.

They do not become independent first-century memories merely because they preserve Semitic-language forms.

## Testament consequence

Book VI should make Syriac Christianity visible as one of the major civilizations through which Yeshua was remembered.

It also provides a concrete example of how four Gospel voices can be deliberately harmonized—exactly the move Testament's source-critical apparatus normally refuses to do invisibly.
