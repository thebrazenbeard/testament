# Zechariah — King, Shepherd, Jerusalem V1

Status: SCRIPTURAL_INTERTEXT_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Priority themes

Zechariah supplies major Gospel/Christian images involving:
- humble king entering Jerusalem;
- donkey/colt;
- shepherd;
- struck shepherd/scattered sheep;
- piercing/mourning;
- eschatological Jerusalem;
- Mount of Olives;
- Temple/cleansing imagery.

## Triumphal entry

Mark, Matthew, Luke and John all narrate entry traditions with different scriptural shaping.

Matthew's two-animal presentation is a particularly visible example of how scriptural interpretation can alter narrative form.

Tucker Ferda's 2020 JTS article examines Matthew's use of Zechariah 9:9 together with Genesis 49.

Reference:
- "Doubling Down: Zechariah's Oracle, Judah's Blessing, and the Triumphal Entry in Matthew":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/jts/flaa088

## Historical question

Possible layers:
- Yeshua intentionally performs a Zecharian royal sign;
- followers interpret an ordinary entry through Zechariah;
- Gospel authors intensify the scriptural staging;
- some combination.

The multiple-Gospel presence does not eliminate dependence and redaction.

## Political significance

If Yeshua intentionally enacted Zechariah 9 imagery near Passover, Roman/elite observers could plausibly read it as royal symbolism.

That possibility belongs in the execution-causality analysis, not as proof of Yeshua's divine status.

## Testament consequence

Book II should make the Jerusalem entry politically legible while keeping open how deliberately scriptural the original action was.

Matthew's two-animal detail should never be harmonized invisibly into Mark/Luke/John.
