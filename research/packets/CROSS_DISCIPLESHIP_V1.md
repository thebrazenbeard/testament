# Carrying the Cross / Discipleship V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Mark 8:34;
- Matthew 16:24;
- Luke 9:23;
- Matthew 10:38;
- Luke 14:27;
- Gospel of Thomas 55 / related family-renunciation material.

## Historicity problem

Cross-bearing language is often suspected of being post-crucifixion community formation:
after Yeshua's execution, "take up your cross" is an extraordinarily natural Christian metaphor.

That makes retrojection possible.

## Historical plausibility control

A 2021 *New Testament Studies* reassessment of non-Christian Greek sources concludes that carrying a *stauros* in relation to crucifixion is historically grounded practice and argues that it is not implausible that Yeshua could have used cross-bearing language before his own death.

Reference:
- "Carrying a σταυρός: A Re-Assessment of the Non-Christian Greek Sources":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/carrying-a-a-reassessment-of-the-nonchristian-greek-sources/18B697FDA9F6B7A7FD9330B13395854A

This establishes plausibility, not authenticity.

## Source development

Mark integrates the saying into a passion-prediction and discipleship sequence.

Luke adds "daily" in 9:23, which may represent Lukan redactional intensification.

Matthew preserves the saying in more than one literary context.

The forms cannot all be treated as separate historical occasions without argument.

## Historical ceiling

Possible:
- Yeshua may have invoked Roman execution imagery as a demand for dangerous discipleship.

Also possible:
- an early post-execution community placed crucifixion imagery into Yeshua's mouth to interpret suffering and martyrdom.

Current status:
historically plausible but not secure enough to quote as exact pre-death wording without disclosure.

## Manuscript consequence

Book II should not have Yeshua repeatedly predict his exact crucifixion because the Gospel says so.

Book III can include the cross-bearing tradition with an apparatus note exposing the post-Easter retrojection problem.
