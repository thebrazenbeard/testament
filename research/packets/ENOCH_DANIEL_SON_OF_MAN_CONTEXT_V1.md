# Enoch, Daniel, and Son of Man Context V1

Status: SECOND_TEMPLE_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Purpose

Yeshua's "Son of Man" language cannot be interpreted only from later Christian doctrine.

It belongs inside a larger Jewish apocalyptic conversation.

## Daniel 7

Daniel 7 presents:
- beastly empires;
- heavenly court/judgment;
- "one like a son of man / human being";
- the holy ones receiving kingdom.

The relation between the human-like figure and the holy ones is itself debated.

## 1 Enoch

1 Enoch is a composite corpus spanning multiple periods.

Its Enochic traditions include:
- Watchers;
- cosmic order;
- judgment;
- resurrection/es­chatology;
- heavenly journeys;
- later Son-of-Man/Chosen-One traditions in the Parables/Similitudes.

David deSilva argues that 1 Enoch was well positioned to influence Jewish thought in the environment from which Jesus/Jude traditions emerge, while separately treating the difficult dating and relationship of the Parables.

Reference:
- "The Book of Enoch":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195329001.003.0005

## Parables dating

The Parables of Enoch are especially important because they depict a transcendent eschatological agent called:
- Son of Man;
- Chosen One;
- Righteous One;
- Anointed One.

But exact dating has long been disputed.

M. A. Knibb's critical review notes that many scholars have regarded the Parables as Jewish and pre-70 CE while emphasizing the absence of consensus on exact dating.

Reference:
- "The Date of the Parables of Enoch: A Critical Review":

## 4 Ezra

Later Jewish apocalyptic tradition also develops Danielic human/son language in 4 Ezra 13.

This is useful reception evidence for how flexible Daniel 7's imagery became.

It is not automatically a source Yeshua used.

## Current Son-of-Man scholarship

Oxford Bibliographies summarizes the live problems:
- Aramaic idiom;
- generic "human being";
- self-reference;
- Daniel 7;
- Enoch/4 Ezra;
- Gospel redaction;
- disappearance of the title from ordinary early-Christian address.

Reference:
- Benjamin E. Reynolds, "Son of Man":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/obo/9780195393361-0231

## Testament consequence

Book III should place Son of Man sayings beside Daniel/Enochic/4 Ezra trajectories without saying:
"Yeshua obviously meant the pre-existent Enochic Son of Man."

The evidence supports a Jewish apocalyptic semantic field, not one single mandatory referent.
