# James, Peter/Cephas, and Paul V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Why these three matter

The earliest post-death movement cannot be written as "Paul invents Christianity" or "the Twelve remain one united church."

Paul's own letters preserve named earlier leaders and real conflict.

## James

Galatians 1:19 calls James "the brother of the Lord."

Galatians 2 presents James as a major Jerusalem authority.

1 Corinthians 15 includes an appearance tradition specifically naming James.

Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200, contains a passage about the execution of James, identified with Jesus "who was called Christ." Authenticity has been debated, but a 2025 Oxford study argues that the large majority of scholars regard the James passage as authentic and reviews its manuscript support.

Reference:
- T. C. Schmidt, "James the Brother of Jesus: Antiquities 20.200":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/60034/chapter/513641505

A 2026 Oxford Handbook chapter explicitly reopens authenticity questions for Josephus's John/Jesus/James passages, so Testament should record the dispute rather than call it closed.

Reference:
- Rivka Nir, "John the Baptist, Jesus, and Other New Testament Figures in Josephus' Writings":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197539101.003.0011

## Peter / Cephas

Paul knows Cephas personally and preserves conflict with him.

1 Corinthians 15 gives Cephas priority in one early appearance list.

Later Gospel and ecclesial traditions greatly expand Peter's role.

The historical Peter must therefore be separated from:
- Matthean ecclesial symbolism;
- Petrine pseudepigraphy questions;
- Roman episcopal succession claims;
- later martyrdom legends.

## Paul

Paul's authority is contested even within his own letters.

His disputes over gentile inclusion, circumcision and table fellowship are evidence of a movement negotiating identity rather than receiving a single finished doctrinal package.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- James, Cephas and Paul were major first-generation movement figures;
- James had family identification with Yeshua in Paul's language;
- Paul had direct contact/conflict with earlier leaders;
- early gentile/Jewish practice was contested.

Unresolved:
- exact Jerusalem governance structure;
- extent of James's authority over Peter;
- detailed chronology behind Acts versus Paul's own sequence;
- exact content of James's own theology.

## Manuscript consequence

Book V should give James, Peter and Paul genuinely different centers of gravity rather than using them as mouthpieces for one later church.
