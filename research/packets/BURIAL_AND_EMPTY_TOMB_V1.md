# Burial and Empty Tomb V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Separate questions

1. Was Yeshua buried?
2. Was Joseph of Arimathea involved?
3. What type of burial/tomb was used?
4. Was that burial place later found empty?
5. What does an empty tomb establish about resurrection?

These are distinct propositions.

## Roman disposal flexibility

John Granger Cook's survey of Roman legal and literary evidence concludes that crucified bodies were sometimes left exposed and in other cases were buried; Roman practice does not create a universal rule that makes Gospel burial impossible.

Reference:
- John Granger Cook, "Crucifixion and Burial," *New Testament Studies* 57 (2011):
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688510000214

## Jewish burial archaeology

Jodi Magness's archaeological work documents rock-cut tombs and Jewish burial customs in late Second Temple Jerusalem.

Reference:
- Jodi Magness, "Ancient Jewish Tombs and Burial Customs (to 70 C.E.)," *The Archaeology of the Holy Land*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/archaeology-of-the-holy-land/ancient-jewish-tombs-and-burial-customs-to-70-ce/0C0B48471EC795D191D583248B24FD92

Her 2005 work on ossuaries and burials emphasizes the importance of social class and burial practice when evaluating famous-person tomb claims.

## Joseph of Arimathea source development

All four canonical Gospels involve Joseph, but the portraits develop.

William John Lyons cautions against harmonizing the canonical Joseph into one composite without first reading each Gospel's literary/redactional version separately.

Reference:
- William John Lyons, *Joseph of Arimathea: A Study in Reception History*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/26537

## Empty tomb

The historicity of the empty-tomb narrative remains debated.

Important distinctions:
- Paul explicitly transmits death, burial, raising, and appearances but does not narrate women finding an empty tomb.
- Mark supplies the earliest extant canonical tomb narrative under common dating.
- Matthew, Luke, and John expand/diverge in visitors, angelic figures, appearances, guards, geography, and sequence.

Some scholars argue Paul's resurrection semantics imply an empty grave even though he does not narrate one.

Reference:
- John Granger Cook, "Resurrection in Paganism and the Question of an Empty Tomb in 1 Corinthians 15":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S002868851600028X

That is an argument, not an explicit Pauline tomb report.

## Historical ceiling

Probable:
- Yeshua died by crucifixion;
- his body was disposed of somehow;
- burial is early tradition and historically plausible.

Debated:
- Joseph's exact historical role;
- precise tomb ownership/type;
- the empty-tomb discovery;
- visitor sequence.

Not established:
- empty tomb -> resurrection mechanism.

## Manuscript consequence

Book IV must not let the narrator stand inside the tomb as an omniscient camera.

The most honest narrative strategy may be to follow surviving people and conflicting memories rather than narrate the inaccessible event.
