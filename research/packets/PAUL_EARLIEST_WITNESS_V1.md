# Paul as Earliest Extant Christian Witness V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Question

What does the earliest extant Christian correspondence establish about Yeshua/Jesus before the narrative Gospels are written?

## Source priority

The undisputed Pauline letters are earlier than the canonical narrative Gospels. A Cambridge chronology places 1 Corinthians around 53/54 CE, Galatians around 54 CE, 2 Corinthians around 54–56 CE, Philippians and Philemon around 56 CE, and Romans around 57 CE. Exact dating remains debated by individual letter, but their mid-first-century priority is secure enough for source ordering.

Reference:
- Cambridge, *Paul the Apostle*, appendix chronology:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/paul-the-apostle/appendix-1-extant-writings-attributed-to-saint-paul-in-rough-chronological-order/31C0B63A5A754ED5B4ADB7AFA66BD85C

## What Paul directly contributes

Priority passages:
- Galatians 1:18–19 — Paul says he met Cephas and James, whom he calls "the brother of the Lord."
- Galatians 2 — Cephas, James and John appear as major Jerusalem leaders; the Antioch conflict preserves disagreement rather than one seamless early church.
- 1 Corinthians 7 — Paul distinguishes some instructions he attributes to "the Lord" from his own judgments.
- 1 Corinthians 9:5 — "brothers of the Lord" and Cephas appear in discussion of itinerant support.
- 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 — Paul transmits a last-meal tradition he says he received.
- 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 — Paul transmits death, burial, raising, and appearance tradition, naming Cephas, the Twelve, a large group, James, apostles, then himself.
- Romans 1:3–4 — Davidic descent and exaltation/resurrection formula.

## 1 Corinthians 15

Cambridge's *Companion to Jesus* notes that Paul's letters to Thessalonica and Galatia already assume resurrection conviction around 50 CE and that the formula quoted in 1 Corinthians 15 may reach back into the first decade after the crucifixion.

Reference:
- Markus Bockmuehl, "Resurrection," *The Cambridge Companion to Jesus*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-companion-to-jesus/resurrection/E61713771E1098825FA969DC87B4E11D

James Ware's 2014 *New Testament Studies* article treats 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 as a pre-Pauline formula and reviews debate over its resurrection semantics.

Reference:
- James Ware, "The Resurrection of Jesus in the Pre-Pauline Formula of 1 Cor 15.3–5":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688514000150

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- a Jesus movement exists before Paul's letters;
- Paul knows named earlier leaders;
- James is identified by Paul as "brother of the Lord";
- death/crucifixion and resurrection proclamation predate 1 Corinthians;
- appearance claims involving Cephas and James are transmitted before the later Gospel narratives.

Not established merely by Paul:
- detailed chronology of Yeshua's ministry;
- infancy narratives;
- an empty-tomb narrative with women;
- exact words/actions from most Gospel episodes;
- the mechanism behind resurrection experiences.

## Manuscript consequence

Book IV should treat Pauline evidence as chronologically prior to Gospel resurrection narrative elaboration.

Book V should not make Paul the inventor of the entire movement: his own letters presuppose earlier leaders, traditions, and disputes.
