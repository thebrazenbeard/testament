# Temple Action and Execution Causality V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Question

What most plausibly connects Yeshua's Jerusalem activity to Roman execution?

## Source convergence

All four canonical Gospels contain a Temple confrontation/action, though John relocates it near the beginning of the public narrative while the Synoptics place it in the final Jerusalem sequence.

That chronological divergence must remain visible.

## Historical weight

E. P. Sanders famously treated the Temple action as one of the strongest historical anchors for explaining Yeshua's death; later scholarship continues to debate its meaning.

A *New Testament Studies* discussion explicitly notes the prominence of Sanders and Neusner in treating Mark 11 and parallels as historically significant.

Reference:
- George Wesley Buchanan, "Symbolic Money-changers in the Temple?":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688500015708

A broad historical reconstruction in *From Jesus Christ to Christianity* argues that ordinary legal disputes in Galilee are insufficient to explain execution and that kingdom language plus the Temple action during Passover provide a more plausible immediate pathway to Roman concern.

Reference:
- "The Death of Jesus":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/from-jesus-christ-to-christianity/death-of-jesus/9B771C1B4F3444E5F0CC63429CF69277

Andrade's 2025 reconstruction pushes this political reading further: location, Passover crowds, Temple confrontation, and regal/messianic implications could make Yeshua legible to Roman officials as a public-order threat.

Reference:
- Nathanael J. Andrade, *Killing the Messiah*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/59249

## Competing interpretations

The Temple action may be reconstructed as:
- protest against commercial practice;
- prophetic symbolic destruction/judgment action;
- critique of priestly leadership;
- eschatological sign;
- challenge to the sacrificial institution;
- later literary intensification of a smaller disturbance.

These are not equivalent.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- a Temple confrontation tradition is multiply embedded in Gospel tradition;
- Yeshua dies under Roman authority;
- "King of the Jews" is politically intelligible.

Plausible reconstruction:
- Jerusalem/Temple activity materially contributed to the arrest/execution pathway.

Unresolved:
- exact scale of the disturbance;
- exact sayings;
- whether Yeshua intended literal Temple destruction;
- whether chief-priestly and Roman surveillance operated exactly as narrated.

## Manuscript consequence

Book II should make the Temple sequence causally serious. It cannot be a brief religious tantrum followed by execution because "the prophecy needed it."
