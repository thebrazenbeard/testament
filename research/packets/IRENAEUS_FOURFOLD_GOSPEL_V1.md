## Historical importance

Irenaeus of Lyons, late second century, is one of the most important witnesses to the consolidation of:
- a fourfold Gospel;
- apostolic succession arguments;
- a "rule of truth/faith";
- anti-heretical classification;
- scriptural interpretation.

## He is a participant, not a neutral cataloguer

Irenaeus is invaluable because he preserves descriptions and quotations from opponents.

He is also arguing against them.

Therefore:
IRANAEUS_REPORTS_X
does not automatically entail
OPPONENT_SELF_DESCRIPTION_WAS_EXACTLY_X.

## Fourfold Gospel

Irenaeus famously argues for exactly four authoritative Gospels.

This is strong evidence that by his setting the fourfold collection had major normative force.

It is not evidence that Yeshua or Paul possessed a twenty-seven-book New Testament.

## Textual witness

Modern study of Irenaeus's Gospel quotations shows that his text itself participates in early textual variation.

Reference:
- Bingham and Todd, "Irenaeus's Text of the Gospels in Adversus haereses":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/40383/chapter-abstract/347148341

So even a church father quoting "the Gospel" does not give us a modern printed critical text by default.

## Interpretive framing

John Behr's study argues against reducing Irenaeus to a mere authoritarian suppressor and instead emphasizes his attempt to define a broad catholic identity through scripture, apostolic tradition and rule of truth.

Reference:
- *Irenaeus of Lyons: Identifying Christianity*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/2593

Other scholarship gives much more weight to power, exclusion and construction of heresy.

Testament should preserve that dispute.

## Testament consequence

Book V's "Church That Won" must not imply:
- victory proves truth;
- victory was purely cynical political suppression.

It should show how institutional, textual, theological and social convergence produced durable boundaries.
