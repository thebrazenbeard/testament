# Mary Magdalene, Revelation, and Authority Reception V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Purpose

Mary Magdalene is historically prominent in canonical passion/resurrection traditions and becomes even more significant in several later revelatory texts.

Those are related but not identical claims.

## Gospel of Mary

NASSCAL's witness record is unusually useful:
- Berlin Codex 8502 preserves the principal Coptic text;
- major pages are missing;
- less than half the work survives;
- two Greek fragments from the third century attest earlier circulation.

Reference:
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-mary/

The preserved narrative presents:
- a post-resurrection dialogue;
- Mary receiving/recounting revelation;
- dispute from Andrew and Peter;
- defense of Mary by Levi.

## Historical question

This establishes that some early-Christian communities could imagine or defend Mary as a privileged revelatory authority.

It does not establish:
- that the historical Peter literally spoke the preserved dialogue;
- that the historical Mary taught the surviving cosmology;
- that Mary was Yeshua's spouse;
- that the text was suppressed because it recorded a marriage.

## Comparative use

Book V can compare:
- Mark's Mary;
- Matthew/Luke/John;
- Gospel of Mary;
- Gospel of Philip;
- later Magdalene conflations.

The comparison should ask how communities use Mary to negotiate:
- witness;
- revelation;
- gender;
- apostolic authority;
- intimacy with Yeshua.

## Literary rule

Do not repair patriarchal erasure by inventing certainty in the opposite direction.

Mary's importance is strong enough without fictional promotion masquerading as discovery.
