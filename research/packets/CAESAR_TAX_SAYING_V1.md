# Caesar / Tax Saying V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Mark 12:13–17;
- Matthew 22:15–22;
- Luke 20:20–26;
- Gospel of Thomas 100.

## Historical setting

The Synoptic scene explicitly concerns the Roman poll/tribute tax.

A classic Cambridge study notes that the Greek wording concerns the *kensos* / head-tax and that the denarius functions as the relevant tax coin in the scene.

Reference:
- H. St J. Hart, "The coin of 'Render unto Caesar ...'":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511554834.015

Anthony Keddie's social-economic work distinguishes imperial direct tribute from provincial/municipal indirect taxation in Roman Palestine.

Reference:
- Anthony Keddie, "Taxation," *Class and Power in Roman Palestine*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/class-and-power-in-roman-palestine/taxation/895C51C0C1CE035D8591EEA76844BCF4

## Interpretation is not settled by the slogan

The saying can be read as:
- practical permission to pay;
- refusal of a political trap;
- delimitation of Caesar's claim;
- prophetic relativization of imperial power;
- ironic response built around image/inscription;
- later Christian accommodation.

Christopher Bryan reads Yeshua as accepting payment while subordinating rulers to God.

Reference:
- "Jesus and Empire: The Teacher and the Man of Deed":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/4523/chapter-abstract/146604657

David Lloyd Dusenbury's 2023 interpretation emphasizes refusal to become either political judge or rebel.

Reference:
- "Jesus among the Rulers":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/46147/chapter-abstract/404737108

These are interpretations, not the saying's one self-evident meaning.

## Thomas 100

Thomas preserves a related Caesar/God saying in a different sayings context.

Its value for independent historical attestation depends on the unresolved Thomas/Synoptic relationship at this logion.

Do not count it as an independent witness by default.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- a Caesar/tax saying is embedded in early Synoptic tradition.

Plausible:
- the saying belongs to a real political-economic environment in which Roman taxation was salient and contested.

Unresolved:
- exact historical trap scene;
- exact coin;
- whether the final wording directly endorses tax payment;
- Thomas independence.

## Testament consequence

Book III must preserve the saying's ambiguity.

Book VII/VIII may draw a distinction between external systems' legitimate claims and the part of agency that cannot be surrendered, but that is authored extension rather than recovered exegesis.
