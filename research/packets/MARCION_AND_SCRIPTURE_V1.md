## Why Marcion matters

Marcion of Sinope was active in Rome in the middle of the second century and formed a durable rival Christian community.

Reference:
- "Marcion and his Critics," *Oxford Handbook of Early Christian Biblical Interpretation*:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198718390.013.24

## Theology

Marcion sharply distinguished the creator associated with Jewish Scripture from the previously unknown good God revealed through Christ.

This theological distinction shaped his reading and selection of texts.

## Text collection

Marcion used:
- a Gospel related to Luke;
- a collection of Pauline letters.

His textual forms are reconstructed substantially through hostile later witnesses, especially Tertullian and Epiphanius.

Therefore:
Marcion's Gospel != simply "our Luke with verses cut out" as an automatic assumption.

Dieter Roth's work emphasizes that older reconstructions exaggerated how radically Marcion's text differed and that Marcion's Gospel/Apostolikon can preserve evidence relevant to the early textual tradition.

Reference:
- "Marcion and the Early New Testament Text":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199566365.003.0017

## Canon question

Marcion is important to canon formation but should not be reduced to the simplistic slogan "Marcion invented the New Testament canon."

Second-century gospel and Pauline collections existed in wider circulation and the canon developed through a longer regional process.

## Manuscript consequence

Book V should let Marcion be intellectually serious.

His importance is not that he was a cartoon villain who deleted the Bible.

He demonstrates how radically different conclusions early Christians could reach from the problem of Yeshua, Israel's scriptures, suffering, creation and divine goodness.
