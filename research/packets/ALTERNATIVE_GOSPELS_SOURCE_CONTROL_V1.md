# Alternative Gospels Source Control V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Purpose

"Lost gospel" is too blunt a category for Testament.

Different texts preserve different kinds of evidence:
- sayings collections;
- passion narratives;
- post-resurrection dialogues;
- infancy stories;
- fragmentary Jewish-Christian gospels;
- later harmonizations;
- mystical/revelatory compositions.

Each work must be registered independently.

## Gospel of Thomas

NASSCAL records:
- one complete Coptic copy in Nag Hammadi Codex II;
- three important Greek Oxyrhynchus fragments, each copied by a different hand;
- Greek witnesses dated broadly from the second to third centuries.

Thomas contains 114 sayings in the complete Coptic form.

Reference:
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-thomas/

Historical use:
- sayings comparison;
- source/dependence questions;
- early-Christian interpretive diversity.

Guard:
manuscript date, composition date, and date of any individual saying are separate questions.

## Gospel of Mary

NASSCAL describes the Gospel of Mary as a post-resurrection dialogue gospel.

Its principal Coptic witness, Berlin Codex 8502, is fourth/fifth century and incomplete; less than half the work survives. Two Greek fragments are third-century witnesses.

Reference:
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-mary/

Historical use:
- later early-Christian Mary reception;
- authority conflict;
- revelatory-dialogue tradition.

Guard:
Mary's prominence in this work cannot automatically be backdated to an apostolic office or romantic relationship.

## Gospel of Peter

The surviving Akhmim manuscript preserves a passion/resurrection narrative in a much later physical witness. NASSCAL's current entry identifies the main manuscript as a sixth/seventh-century codex and catalogs additional fragmentary evidence.

Reference:
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-peter/

Historical use:
- passion/resurrection reception;
- comparison with canonical narrative;
- possible earlier source relationships requiring separate argument.

Guard:
a vivid narrative detail is not early because it is vivid.

## Jewish-Christian gospel fragments

"Gospel of the Hebrews," "Gospel of the Nazarenes," and "Gospel of the Ebionites" are fragmentary/problematic modern source categories.

NASSCAL explicitly notes that "Gospel of the Ebionites" is a modern scholarly title for a non-extant text known substantially through Epiphanius.

References:
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-the-ebionites/
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-the-hebrews/
- https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-the-nazarenes/

These fragments must preserve:
- the later author who quotes/reports them;
- quotation versus paraphrase;
- uncertainty over whether modern labels describe one, two, or several ancient works.

## General rule

Noncanonical does not mean suppressed truth.

Canonical does not mean earliest historical form.

The question for each proposition is:
what is the source, when is it accessible, through what witness, and what exactly does it establish?
