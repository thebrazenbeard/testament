## Nazareth

Modern archaeology confirms a first-century settlement at Nazareth.

Ken Dark's 2023 synthesis argues that archaeology now supplies considerably more information about first-century Nazareth's physical settlement, economy, culture, and daily life than surviving non-Christian written sources do.

References:
- Ken Dark, *Archaeology of Jesus' Nazareth*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/45361
- "Archaeology and first-century Nazareth":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/45361/chapter-abstract/389286314

Dark explicitly states that archaeology cannot prove a specific excavated house was Yeshua's childhood home.

## Galilee

Galilee under Herod Antipas was not an isolated timeless peasant world.

Research questions include:
- Jewish identity and practice;
- Hellenistic/Greco-Roman cultural exposure;
- village/city relations;
- taxation;
- agriculture/fishing;
- debt and patronage;
- Antipas's building programs;
- Sepphoris and Tiberias;
- trade routes;
- material inequality.

Oxford's Galilee bibliography emphasizes the continuing scholarly debates over ethnic composition, Galilean Judaism, Roman/Herodian economic effects, and Hellenistic/Roman culture.

Reference:
- "Galilee," Oxford Bibliographies:
  https://academic.oup.com/reference/62341/reference-article-abstract/554113539

A recent Oxford history of Judea under Greek/Roman rule discusses Antipas, Galilee's economy/demography, and synagogue development.

Reference:
- David A. deSilva, *Judea under Greek and Roman Rule*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/58629

## Sepphoris guard

Sepphoris was close to Nazareth and was substantially developed under Antipas.

Reference:
- Richard A. Batey, "Sepphoris and the Jesus Movement":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688501000248

But proximity does not establish:
- that Yeshua worked there;
- that he attended the theater;
- that he spoke Greek fluently;
- that Cynic philosophy shaped him.

Those are hypotheses requiring evidence.

## Archaeological method

James Strange's overview of Gospel archaeology stresses reconstruction through settlements, houses, ritual baths, artifacts, pottery, glass, coins and stone vessels rather than treasure-hunting for named biblical persons.

Reference:
- "Archaeology of the Gospels":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28038/chapter-abstract/211924998

## Manuscript consequence

Book I should make Galilee materially specific:
fields, fishing, stone vessels, roads, tax extraction, household production, debt, Antipas, nearby urban development.

Do not make every Galilean destitute, every city pagan, or every village culturally sealed.
