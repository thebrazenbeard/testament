# Sabbath Controversies V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary clusters

- grain-plucking: Mark 2:23–28; Matthew 12:1–8; Luke 6:1–5;
- healing/withered hand: Mark 3:1–6; Matthew 12:9–14; Luke 6:6–11;
- additional Lukan/Johannine Sabbath healings.

## Jewish-context rule

Sabbath observance is not a silly rule invented by opponents.

It is a covenantal practice with scriptural, communal and legal meaning.

Historical disputes concern:
- what counts as prohibited work;
- danger/need;
- healing;
- scriptural precedent;
- human benefit;
- authority to interpret.

## Historicity debate

Maurice Casey argued that Mark's grain-plucking controversy can be reconstructed as intelligible within an Aramaic/Jewish setting and defended its basic historicity.

Reference:
- "Culture and Historicity: the Plucking of the Grain":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/culture-and-historicity-the-plucking-of-the-grain-mark-22328/F8A639E7707071D15C1B9CF9CD2A516B

That reconstruction is not final consensus.

## "Sabbath made for humankind"

Mark 2:27 is absent from Matthew/Luke.

Its authenticity, redactional status and relation to later Jewish parallels remain disputed.

Therefore it should not automatically control every Sabbath scene.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- early Jesus tradition remembers Sabbath interpretation/conflict.

Plausible:
- Yeshua defended human need/healing through scriptural/halakhic reasoning.

Unresolved:
- exact original cases and sayings;
- whether some controversies reflect later Christian-Jewish boundary disputes.

## Testament consequence

Book II should portray Sabbath arguments as Jewish legal/moral reasoning.

Yeshua's position can be radical without Judaism being caricatured as anti-human legalism.
