# Divorce and Marriage V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Mark 10:2–12;
- Matthew 5:31–32;
- Matthew 19:3–12;
- Luke 16:18;
- 1 Corinthians 7 as early reception of a "Lord" command;
- relevant Second Temple Jewish legal evidence.

## Why this is historically important

Divorce sayings are among the more strongly attested ethical/legal traditions associated with Yeshua, but the forms differ materially.

A Harvard Theological Review survey of Q/social context notes E. P. Sanders's judgment that the divorce saying is among the best-attested Gospel traditions.

Reference:
- "The Quest for the 'Community' of Q":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/quest-for-the-community-of-q-mapping-q-within-the-social-scribal-and-textual-landscapes-of-second-temple-judaism/EFBA03CDC9F5BC7DAEC281E0ECDF564A

## Jewish legal world

Second Temple interpretations of:
- Deuteronomy 24;
- Genesis 1–2;
- adultery;
- remarriage;
- marital obligation;
varied.

Jonathan Burnside's comparative work explicitly places Jesus and Qumran divorce interpretation inside Second Temple legal argument rather than treating Jesus as standing outside Jewish law.

Reference:
- "New Laws For A New Age":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/25931/chapter-abstract/193707444

## Matthew's exception clauses

Matthew adds exception language absent from Mark/Luke's absolute-looking forms.

William Loader argues that these clauses may make explicit an assumption about adultery rather than merely watering down a stricter original rule.

Reference:
- "Did Adultery Mandate Divorce?":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/did-adultery-mandate-divorce-a-reassessment-of-jesus-divorce-logia/A9EF47D3EA2855DAFC500F07B62E9265

This is one reconstruction, not consensus by existence.

## Gender

Mark uniquely includes a woman divorcing a husband, a formulation that may reflect a Greco-Roman legal/social setting more than Palestinian practice.

That feature needs redactional review before being put into Yeshua's historical mouth.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- Yeshua tradition contained a strikingly restrictive divorce teaching rooted in creation texts.

Unresolved:
- exact original exception structure;
- exact gender formulation;
- precise practical application;
- whether different settings preserve multiple historical occasions or redactional reuse.

## Testament consequence

Book III must present the rule inside marriage/property/gender realities, not as a floating moral slogan.

Book VIII must not turn an ancient restrictive saying into compulsory endurance of abuse.
