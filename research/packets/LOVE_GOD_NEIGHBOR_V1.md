# Love of God and Neighbor V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Mark 12:28–34;
- Matthew 22:34–40;
- Luke 10:25–28.

Scriptural sources:
- Deuteronomy 6:4–5;
- Leviticus 19:18.

## Jewish scriptural frame

The saying does not invent love of God or neighbor from nothing.

It joins two Torah commands and places them in a summarizing relation.

This must be narrated as Jewish interpretive practice, not Christianity replacing Jewish law with love.

## Mark

Mark contains a relatively extended exchange with a scribe:
- Shema language;
- love of God;
- love of neighbor;
- scribe's affirmation;
- statement that such love exceeds burnt offerings/sacrifices;
- Yeshua's positive reply.

## Matthew

Matthew substantially abbreviates Mark and makes the two commandments explicitly support "all the law and prophets."

Older scholarship notes that Matthew changes Mark's relation between first/second commandment and omits Mark's anti-cultic comparison.

Reference:
- "The Things that Defile ... and the Law in Matthew and Mark":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/things-that-defile-mark-vii-14-and-the-law-in-mathew-and-mark/5014A11D4EB7D3B261344C310E3DD176

## Luke

Luke relocates the double command into the lawyer/Good Samaritan sequence.

That literary setting changes the question from command ranking toward:
"who is my neighbor?"

Luke's form must therefore be treated as redactionally integrated into his own narrative.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- double-love tradition is early and rooted directly in Torah;
- Mark, Matthew and Luke attest its importance, though not independently at every wording level.

Plausible:
- Yeshua used Torah-summary reasoning centered on love of God and neighbor.

Unresolved:
- exact historical scene;
- exact ordering/wording;
- degree of Markan composition in the exchange.

## Manuscript consequence

Book III should retain Deuteronomy and Leviticus beside the Yeshua tradition.

The point is not "Jesus finally invented love."

The historically interesting move is how he is remembered as ordering and radicalizing already-Jewish ethical resources.
