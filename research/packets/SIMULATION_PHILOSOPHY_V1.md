## Bostrom's argument is not "we probably live in a simulation"

Nick Bostrom's 2003 simulation argument presents a trilemma: roughly, at least one of three claims must hold concerning civilization extinction, interest in ancestor simulations, or the prevalence of simulated observers.

Reference:
- Nick Bostrom, "Are We Living in a Computer Simulation?":
  https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9213.00309

Bostrom later explicitly clarified that the argument does not tell us which branch is true and does not itself amount to the simple conclusion "we are simulated."

Reference:
- "The Simulation Argument: some explanations":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/anp063

Testament must not turn Bostrom into an authority claiming our universe is simulated.

## Chalmers: simulated does not mean unreal

David Chalmers argues for a form of virtual realism:
- simulated/virtual objects can still be real;
- we may be unable to know we are not simulated;
- meaningful/good life remains possible in a simulation.

Reference:
- David Chalmers, "The Simulation Hypothesis: Metaphysics, Epistemology, Value":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/9780198924159.003.0017

This directly matters to Testament's ethics.

If our reality were simulated, the suffering, relationships and choices inside it would not thereby become meaningless.

## Criticism

Simulation arguments have serious philosophical critics.

Peter Godfrey-Smith argues against treating present simulation scenarios as a possibility we should take epistemically seriously.

Reference:
- "Simulation scenarios and philosophy":
  https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.13124

Testament should preserve disagreement rather than presenting simulation theory as established science.

## Ethical payoff

Simulation theory is useful to the manuscript only if the ethical thesis survives its failure.

If the universe is not simulated, Book VIII must still work.

If the book collapses when the simulation hypothesis is removed, the architecture has failed.
