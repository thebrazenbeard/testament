# Judas, Mary, Thomas, and Disciple-Authority Traditions V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Purpose

Preserve the Testament discussion about whether Judas Iscariot, Mary Magdalene, Judas Thomas, or other followers were remembered by different early-Christian communities as unusually close to Yeshua or unusually capable of understanding him.

Later literary authority claims must not be converted into first-century biography.

## Judas Iscariot

Canonical Gospels place Judas among the Twelve. That establishes insider status in the tradition, not "best friend" status. Peter, James, and John more clearly function as a recurring canonical inner group in selected scenes.

The Gospel of Judas instead gives Judas privileged disclosure relative to other disciples. Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.31.1, already knows a Judas-gospel tradition in which Judas is treated as uniquely knowledgeable regarding the betrayal.

Controls:
- privileged revelation != historical best-friend status;
- privileged knowledge != automatic moral approval or salvation;
- later cosmological dialogue != stenographic first-century conversation.

## Mary Magdalene

Canonical tradition already gives Mary unusual prominence across passion, burial/tomb, and resurrection traditions.

The Gospel of Mary intensifies her revelatory authority:
- Mary recounts revelation;
- Andrew and Peter challenge or resist;
- Levi defends her.

The Gospel of Philip also participates in later intimacy/companionship traditions.

Controls:
- later revelatory prominence != proof of a formal first-century office;
- intimacy language != proof of marriage;
- later Peter/Mary conflict may encode authority disputes rather than verbatim first-generation dialogue.

## Judas Thomas distinction

Didymos Judas Thomas in the Gospel of Thomas is not Judas Iscariot.

Do not collapse:
- Judas Iscariot in the Gospel of Judas;
- Judas Thomas in the Gospel of Thomas.

## Comparative pattern

A recurring literary pattern is worth testing:
- Judas Iscariot: privileged knowledge against the misunderstanding of the Twelve;
- Mary Magdalene: privileged revelation challenged by apostolic authority;
- Judas Thomas/Thomas: hidden or private sayings as an authority vehicle.

This does not establish a historical ranking of Judas first, Mary second, or a secret inner circle.

## Manuscript consequence

Testament may explore the possibility that Yeshua's followers did not agree on who understood him best.

The strongest current proposition is about reception history: different early-Christian communities authorized competing interpreters of Yeshua by portraying different disciples as recipients of distinctive access, witness, insight, or revelation.
