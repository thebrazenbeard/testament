## Category

"Valentinian" is more useful than the generic label "Gnostic" for a major family of second-century Christian thought associated with Valentinus and later teachers/schools.

The tradition is internally diverse.

## Primary-source ecosystem

Important materials include:
- *Gospel of Truth*;
- *Tripartite Tractate*;
- *Treatise on the Resurrection*;
- *Gospel of Philip* in later Valentinian reception/context;
- fragments preserved by Clement;
- reports/quotations in Irenaeus, Hippolytus and other opponents.

Hostile heresiologists and surviving Coptic texts must be compared rather than allowing either side to define the whole tradition.

## Gospel of Truth

A new 2025 Cambridge critical treatment argues that the Nag Hammadi *Gospel of Truth* is indeed the work known to Irenaeus and confirms significant Valentinian reception while cautioning that Valentinian use does not by itself prove Valentinian authorship.

Reference:
- Francis Watson, "Contexts of Reception," *The Gospel of Truth*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/gospel-of-truth/contexts-of-reception/E42F6B007768F70B6436380DE684DF6E

This is a good model for Testament:
identity, reception and authorship are separate propositions.

## Theological axes

Research:
- unknowable Father;
- emanation/plērōma;
- error/ignorance;
- revelation;
- Christ/Saviour;
- creation;
- human types;
- knowledge and salvation;
- resurrection;
- sacramental interpretation.

Do not reduce the entire tradition to:
"matter is evil and secret knowledge saves."

## Relationship to Yeshua

Valentinian sources are primarily evidence for how second-century Christians reconstructed the meaning of Christ/Yeshua.

They are not first-century biography by default.

## Testament consequence

Book V should let Valentinian theology feel like a serious answer to:
Why is reality fractured?
Why don't people know who they are?
What does revelation heal?

That makes its later rejection historically intelligible without turning "heresy" into shorthand for nonsense.
