# Beatitudes and Woes V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Matthew 5:3–12;
- Luke 6:20–26;
- Gospel of Thomas 54, 68, 69 and related sayings.

## Matthew / Luke divergence

Luke addresses:
- poor;
- hungry;
- weeping;
and adds corresponding woes to:
- rich;
- full;
- laughing;
- socially praised.

Matthew expands/spiritualizes/reframes:
- "poor in spirit";
- hunger/thirst "for righteousness";
- additional beatitudes such as meek, merciful, pure in heart, peacemakers.

These differences are theologically significant.

## Q problem

Under Two-Source models, a common beatitude core is reconstructed in Q.

But Q is not extant, and reconstruction should not simply choose Luke whenever Luke looks shorter or Matthew whenever Matthew looks more Jewish.

A 2025 *New Testament Studies* article on James and Q argues that James may preserve adaptation of traditions related to Q's woes, illustrating that sayings transmission can extend beyond the two Synoptic forms.

Reference:
- Christina Gousopoulos, "Matthew, Luke, and… James?":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/matthew-luke-and-james-the-reconstruction-of-q-and-the-epistle-of-james/C8895D14A0A7CB69CCC33C47D2CE09E4

This proposal itself needs review; it is not a new consensus.

## Poverty

Testament must resist two opposite simplifications:
- Luke = only economics;
- Matthew = only inner spirituality.

Material poverty, humility, eschatological reversal and righteousness language all belong to the interpretive environment.

The source forms should be allowed to differ.

## Thomas

Thomas 54 preserves a blessing of the poor linked to the kingdom.

Its relationship to the Synoptic forms is disputed.

Current Oxford scholarship emphasizes that Thomas/Synoptic comparison remains useful even where dependence cannot be globally solved.

Reference:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- blessing/reversal traditions attached to Yeshua are early and multiply transmitted within the Christian sayings tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact original number/order;
- whether "poor" or "poor in spirit" is closer to an earlier form;
- Thomas's independence in these logia;
- exact relation of woes to beatitudes.

## Manuscript consequence

Book III should show at least the Lukan and Matthean forms rather than erasing their ideological difference.

The final ethical synthesis can affirm both material justice and interior disposition without pretending the ancient sources had already harmonized them.
