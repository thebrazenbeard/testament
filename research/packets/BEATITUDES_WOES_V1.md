# Beatitudes and Woes V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / WITNESS_CONTROL_ACTIVE / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Matthew 5:3–12;
- Luke 6:20–26;
- Gospel of Thomas 54, 68, 69 and related sayings.

## Matthew / Luke divergence

Luke addresses:
- poor;
- hungry;
- weeping;
and adds corresponding woes to:
- rich;
- full;
- laughing;
- socially praised.

Matthew expands/spiritualizes/reframes:
- "poor in spirit";
- hunger/thirst "for righteousness";
- additional beatitudes such as meek, merciful, pure in heart, peacemakers.

These differences are theologically significant.

## Q problem

Under Two-Source models, a common beatitude core is reconstructed in Q.

But Q is not extant, and reconstruction should not simply choose Luke whenever Luke looks shorter or Matthew whenever Matthew looks more Jewish.

A 2025 *New Testament Studies* article on James and Q argues that James may preserve adaptation of traditions related to Q's woes, illustrating that sayings transmission can extend beyond the two Synoptic forms.

Reference:
- Christina Gousopoulos, "Matthew, Luke, and… James?":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/matthew-luke-and-james-the-reconstruction-of-q-and-the-epistle-of-james/C8895D14A0A7CB69CCC33C47D2CE09E4

This proposal itself needs review; it is not a new consensus.

## Poverty

Testament must resist two opposite simplifications:
- Luke = only economics;
- Matthew = only inner spirituality.

Material poverty, humility, eschatological reversal and righteousness language all belong to the interpretive environment.

The source forms should be allowed to differ.

## Thomas

Thomas 54 preserves a blessing of the poor linked to the kingdom.

Its relationship to the Synoptic forms is disputed.

Current Oxford scholarship emphasizes that Thomas/Synoptic comparison remains useful even where dependence cannot be globally solved.

Reference:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

The current extant Thomas evidence needs a hard witness-language boundary.

Nag Hammadi Codex II, tractate 2, is the direct extant witness for Thomas 54, 68 and 69 used in this cluster:
- Coptic papyrus codex;
- fourth century;
- Coptic Museum, Cairo, Inv. 10544;
- Gospel of Thomas occupies codex pages 32–51.

The surviving Greek Thomas fragments do not preserve these sayings:
- P.Oxy. 654 preserves the prologue and sayings 1–7;
- P.Oxy. 1 preserves material around sayings 26–33;
- P.Oxy. 655 preserves sayings 24 and 36–39.

Therefore Testament must not write or cite an "extant Greek Thomas 54/68/69." Any Greek back-translation of these logia is reconstruction.

### Poverty beatitude comparison

The extant forms remain visibly distinct:
- Matthew qualifies the poor with "in spirit";
- Luke addresses the poor without that qualifier;
- Thomas 54, in the extant Coptic witness, also presents an unqualified poor/kingdom blessing.

That resemblance is important comparative evidence, but it does not settle direction of dependence or priority.

Melissa Harl Sellew's 2023 Oxford Handbook treatment explicitly moves Thomas/Synoptic comparison away from a forced global choice between dependence and autonomy. Testament adopts that as a useful methodological guard: compare the forms closely without promoting one global literary-dependence theory beyond the evidence.

Reference:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

### What the witness does and does not prove

Established:
- Thomas 54, 68 and 69 are directly extant in NHC II in Coptic;
- the Thomas 54 poverty/kingdom form is materially comparable with Matthew 5:3 and Luke 6:20;
- Thomas 68–69 preserve related persecution/hunger blessing material within the same extant Coptic work.

Not established:
- exact Greek wording behind those Coptic logia;
- Thomasine independence from the Synoptics;
- Thomasine dependence on the Synoptics;
- Synoptic dependence on Thomas;
- that Luke's unqualified "poor" is automatically earlier than Matthew's "poor in spirit";
- one recoverable original number/order of beatitudes.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- blessing/reversal traditions attached to Yeshua are early and multiply transmitted within the Christian sayings tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact original number/order;
- whether "poor" or "poor in spirit" is closer to an earlier form;
- Thomas's independence in these logia;
- exact relation of woes to beatitudes.

## Manuscript consequence

Book III should show at least the Lukan and Matthean forms rather than erasing their ideological difference.

The final ethical synthesis can affirm both material justice and interior disposition without pretending the ancient sources had already harmonized them.
