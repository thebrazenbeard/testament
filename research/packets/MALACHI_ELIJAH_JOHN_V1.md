# Malachi / Elijah / John V1

Status: SCRIPTURAL_INTERTEXT_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Source problem

Malachi's messenger, coming Lord, and Elijah traditions become a major interpretive resource for Christian presentation of John and Yeshua.

But this interpretation had a pre-Christian Jewish reception history.

## Malachi

Current Oxford treatment highlights:
- textual transmission including Qumran witness 4QXIIa;
- Greek translation;
- redaction;
- return to YHWH;
- Day of YHWH;
- later relation to John/Yeshua.

Reference:
- Aaron Schart, "Malachi":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190673208.013.32

## Reception before/around Christianity

David Miller's *New Testament Studies* article compares:
- Ben Sira;
- 4Q521;
- Septuagint;
- Luke;
and shows that ancient interpreters already differed over how Malachi's messenger/Elijah material should map onto eschatological figures.

Reference:
- "The Messenger, the Lord, and the Coming Judgement in the Reception History of Malachi 3":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S002868850700001X

## Gospel use

Different Gospel authors configure John differently:
- Elijah identification;
- denial/ambiguity in John;
- forerunner;
- messenger;
- eschatological prophet.

This development must be preserved.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- John was interpreted through Elijah/Malachi traditions early.

Not established:
- John himself claimed every later scriptural role;
- Malachi's original author predicted John by name.

## Testament consequence

Book I can present John's followers and later Yeshua-followers wrestling with Elijah language rather than the narrator simply declaring prophecy fulfilled.
