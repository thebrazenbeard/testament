## Why this corpus matters

The Psalms of Solomon provide a pre-/early-Roman-era Jewish witness to:
- political disappointment;
- Jerusalem;
- sinners/rulers;
- divine judgment;
- Davidic messianic expectation.

They are especially useful because modern readers often imagine that "Messiah" already meant one universally agreed supernatural figure.

It did not.

## Psalms of Solomon 17–18

These psalms portray a Davidic ruler associated with:
- purification of Jerusalem;
- judgment of unrighteous rulers/nations;
- gathering Israel;
- righteous rule;
- dependence on God.

David deSilva treats the corpus as a particularly clear snapshot of messianic expectation shaped by Hasmonean/Herodian political experience.

Reference:
- "Military Messianism and Jesus' Mission: The Psalms of Solomon":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195329001.003.0006

## Comparison controls

Possible comparison with Yeshua:
- Son of David traditions;
- Jerusalem;
- kingdom;
- triumphal-entry symbolism;
- judgment;
- relation to violence.

Do not assume:
- Yeshua shared the Psalms' political program;
- the crowd had one fixed messianic expectation;
- "Messiah" itself implies deity.

## Manuscript consequence

Book I should give readers at least one concrete Jewish messianic model before Yeshua enters Jerusalem.

That makes later Christian transformation of "Messiah" historically visible rather than inevitable.
