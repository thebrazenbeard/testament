# Family Renunciation and Discipleship V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Primary forms

- Luke 14:26;
- Matthew 10:37;
- Mark 3:31–35 and parallels;
- Mark 10:28–30 and parallels;
- Luke 18:29–30;
- Thomas 55 and 101 as related family-renunciation sayings.

## "Hate father and mother"

Luke's wording is deliberately severe.

Matthew's parallel has:
"love father or mother more than me."

A 2023 *Harvard Theological Review* study argues that Luke's "hate" can be analyzed as hyperbole/metonymic intensification while also insisting that Luke and Matthew chose different wording appropriate to their literary contexts.

Reference:
- "Hyperbole and the Cost of Discipleship: A Case Study of Luke 14:26":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/hyperbole-and-the-cost-of-discipleship-a-case-study-of-luke-1426/2EE5C01E16DAF1A395D1F0D9E471EFCA

The article reports that many scholars judge the Lukan wording closer to the underlying shared tradition, but that remains reconstruction.

## Social cost

Itinerant/apocalyptic movements can disrupt:
- household labor;
- inheritance;
- marriage expectations;
- kin loyalty;
- patronage;
- village identity.

Family-renunciation sayings should therefore have concrete social costs in Testament, not merely metaphorical "put God first" sentiment.

## Counterweight

Other traditions preserve:
- care for parents;
- family members in the movement;
- James's later leadership;
- women/households supporting itinerants.

Therefore "Yeshua rejected family as such" is too simple.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- early sayings tradition includes severe demands that relativize kinship.

Plausible:
- following Yeshua could generate actual family rupture.

Unresolved:
- exact hyperbolic/original wording;
- whether family conflict was universal expectation or mission-specific radicalism.

## Testament consequence

Book II should let discipleship cost something.

Book VIII should not use ancient family-renunciation rhetoric to justify cultic isolation or abusive demands for loyalty.
