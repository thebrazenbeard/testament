## Modern-category warning

"Jewish Christianity" is itself a modern scholarly category.

Annette Yoshiko Reed's Oxford Bibliographies overview stresses the history and instability of labels such as:
- Jewish Christianity;
- Ebionites;
- Nazoraeans/Nazarenes;
- Jerusalem Church traditions;
- Pseudo-Clementine communities.

Reference:
- "Jewish Christianity":
  https://academic.oup.com/reference/62341/reference-article-abstract/554095335

## Lost-gospel problem

Texts conventionally called:
- Gospel of the Hebrews;
- Gospel of the Nazarenes;
- Gospel of the Ebionites;
survive largely through later quotations/descriptions.

The exact number and identities of the underlying works are disputed.

Jeremiah Coogan's 2022 study shows how late-antique heresiologists themselves created bibliographic categories around "Matthew" and "the Hebrews," meaning our source labels can partially inherit their classification systems.

## Anti-Judaism and intra-Jewish polemic

Petri Luomanen distinguishes different relationships to Judaism among early Christian apocrypha and notes that material associated with Gospel of the Hebrews may preserve something closer to prophetic/intra-Jewish critique than later anti-Jewish polemic.

Reference:
- "Judaism and Anti-Judaism in Early Christian Apocrypha":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34876/chapter-abstract/298345177

## Testament consequence

Book V should not treat "Jewish Christianity" as an early sect that Christianity naturally outgrew.

Yeshua's earliest movement was Jewish.

The historical question is how different Jewish and gentile Christ-following identities differentiated over time.
