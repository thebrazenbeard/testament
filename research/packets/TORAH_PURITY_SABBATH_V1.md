# Torah, Purity, Sabbath, and "Sinners" V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Jewish-context rule

Yeshua's disputes about Torah, Sabbath, purity, divorce, food, vows and sinners occur within Jewish argument.

They must not be narrated as "Jesus versus Judaism."

## Purity in the material record

Yonatan Adler's archaeological-historical work identifies substantial textual and archaeological evidence for widespread first-century observance of ritual-purity practices, including immersion pools and chalk/stone vessels.

Reference:
- Yonatan Adler, "Ritual Purity," *The Origins of Judaism*:
  https://academic.oup.com/yale-scholarship-online/book/46390/chapter-abstract/405902581

This gives purity arguments real material context.

## Earliest Palestinian tradition and later gentile reception

James Crossley argues that purity concerns in early Palestinian tradition could be transformed as Jesus memories circulated in gentile contexts, with morality increasingly foregrounded and ritual-purity concerns reinterpreted or diminished.

Reference:
- James G. Crossley, "'Sinners', Law, and Purity":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/41755/chapter-abstract/354220589

This is a useful warning against reading later Christian law-versus-grace binaries directly into every historical dispute.

## Manuscript consequence

Book II must make legal disputes intelligible from inside Jewish covenantal life.

No Pharisee is automatically a hypocrite.

No purity practice is automatically irrational.
