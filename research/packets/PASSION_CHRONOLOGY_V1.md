# Passion Chronology V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Core contradiction

The Synoptic Gospels and John do not map the Last Supper and crucifixion onto Passover in the same way.

Synoptic chronology:
- Last Supper presented as Passover meal;
- crucifixion follows on Nisan 15 under the normal reading.

Johannine chronology:
- last meal occurs before Passover;
- Yeshua dies on the preparation day, Nisan 14.

Reference:
- Jonathan Klawans, "Passover and Last Supper Revisited":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688513000076
- Cambridge Companion to Nicaea discussion of the inherited chronological conflict:

## Precise death date

Astronomical and calendar reconstructions often prefer candidate dates such as 7 April 30 CE or 3 April 33 CE.

Helen Bond argues that the precise date is no longer recoverable with historical certainty and that a safer conclusion is death around Passover between roughly 29 and 34 CE.

Reference:
- Helen K. Bond, "Dating the Death of Jesus: Memory and the Religious Imagination":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/dating-the-death-of-jesus-memory-and-the-religious-imagination/0458FC0CA55BEF810BCC411AC2765860

## Harmony proposals

Scholars have proposed:
- different calendars;
- Synoptic priority;
- Johannine priority;
- theological reshaping;
- variant memories.

No harmony should be silently installed as fact.

## Historical floor

All four Gospels broadly converge that:
- arrest/execution occur around Passover;
- Yeshua is crucified under Pilate;
- death occurs before a Sabbath;
- burial follows quickly.

Even that convergence requires source-dependence awareness.

## Manuscript consequence

Book II should avoid timestamping the final week more precisely than the evidence warrants.

If a literary chronology is chosen for narrative continuity, the apparatus must state which Gospel chronology is being followed and why.

The contradiction register remains authoritative over prose convenience.
