# Greco-Roman Religious World V1

Status: COMPARATIVE_CONTEXT_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Why it belongs

Yeshua's public ministry is Jewish and Palestinian.

Christianity's rapid expansion occurs inside a Greco-Roman religious world full of:
- gods;
- local cults;
- household religion;
- sacrifice;
- festivals;
- healing cults;
- divination;
- magic;
- ruler cult;
- philosophical theology;
- voluntary associations.

Book V cannot explain developing Christian language without this environment.

## Saviour language

Sōtēr/sōtēria already had broad Greek religious and political use before Christianity.

Theodora Suk Fong Jim's 2022 study shows "saviour" gods and saving/divine rescue as widespread phenomena with strongly this-worldly meanings.

Reference:
- *Saviour Gods and Soteria in Ancient Greece*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/41429

Therefore:
Christian "Saviour" language is neither unique vocabulary nor reducible to pagan borrowing.

It enters a shared linguistic world and is reshaped through Jewish scripture and Christ claims.

## Son of God / divine sonship

Roman imperial culture publicly used divine-son language.

Christopher Bryan and Michael Peppard both stress that claims about Jesus as God's son existed in an environment where emperor/divine-son claims were publicly visible.

References:
- "Sons of God in the Greco-Roman World":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/44536/chapter-abstract/376971032
- "Divinity and Divine Sonship in the Roman World":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/8692/chapter-abstract/154700370

This creates comparison and possible political resonance.

It does not prove Christian Son-of-God language was copied from emperor worship.

## Lived religion

A modern Oxford study of New Testament Christianity emphasizes gods, sacrifice, festivals, temples, magic, households and civic life as overlapping lived religious environments.

Reference:
- *New Testament Christianity in the Roman World*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/3544

## Testament consequence

Book I remains primarily Jewish/Palestinian.

Book V widens the camera:
when the Jesus movement enters Greek-speaking cities, its titles and rituals are heard in semantic worlds already populated by lords, saviours, divine sons, sacred meals and healing gods.
