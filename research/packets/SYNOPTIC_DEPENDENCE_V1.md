## Why this matters

Matthew, Mark and Luke cannot be counted as three independent witnesses every time they tell the same story.

The Synoptic Problem exists because the Gospels sometimes share near-verbatim wording and sequence while diverging sharply elsewhere.

Reference:
- John S. Kloppenborg, "The History and Prospects of the Synoptic Problem," *Oxford Handbook of the Synoptic Gospels*:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45760/chapter-abstract/398723290

## Current scholarly landscape

Some form of the Two-Source hypothesis remains dominant in scholarship:
- Markan priority;
- Matthew and Luke using Mark;
- a hypothetical sayings source conventionally called Q for much shared non-Markan material.

But serious alternatives remain:
- Farrer hypothesis: Luke uses Matthew, no Q required;
- Two-Gospel/Griesbach families;
- more complex oral/written-source models;
- minor-source and publication-process models.

Oxford's 2023 handbook explicitly describes renewed debate around Q, Matthew-Luke independence, orality, performance, ancient composition and textual instability.

Reference:
- *Oxford Handbook of the Synoptic Gospels*:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45760

## Manuscript variation warning

The Synoptic Problem is usually modeled using reconstructed critical texts, while actual manuscripts exhibit textual variation.

Reference:
- "Manuscripts: The Problem with the Synoptic Problem":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45760/chapter-abstract/398724180

Therefore exact wording arguments must bind a critical edition/witness where wording is consequential.

## Testament counting rule

For historical convergence:

Mark + Matthew copying Mark + Luke copying Mark = one dependent tradition at that point, not three.

Shared Matthew/Luke material may represent:
- Q/common source;
- Luke's use of Matthew;
- shared oral tradition;
- other source relationships.

Do not assign independence until argued.

## Book III consequence

"The Sayings" must maintain a saying-level ledger:
- Markan;
- double tradition;
- Matthean special;
- Lukan special;
- Johannine;
- Thomas parallel;
- other apocryphal/fragmentary parallel;
- scriptural antecedent;
- redaction indicators;
- translation issues;
- independence estimate.

The literary voice may converge.

The apparatus may not pretend the convergence was recovered from one tape recorder.
