# Qur'anic Jesus / Isa and Maryam V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Position in Testament

The Qur'an is a seventh-century source in a late-antique religious environment.

It is indispensable for the later reception of Yeshua/Jesus and Mary/Maryam.

It is not a contemporary first-century biography.

## Qur'anic Jesus

Major themes include:
- Isa son of Maryam;
- Messiah;
- messenger/prophet;
- miraculous conception;
- signs/miracles by God's permission;
- "word" and "spirit" language;
- disciples;
- rejection of divine sonship and Trinitarian formulations;
- disputed crucifixion language;
- eschatological/revelatory significance.

## Late-antique setting

Angelika Neuwirth's work situates the Qur'an within the plural scriptural and literary cultures of Late Antiquity and treats its biblical figures as active reframings rather than simple copies.

Reference:
- *The Qur'an and Late Antiquity: A Shared Heritage*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/11627

This is useful because Testament should ask what Christian, Jewish, homiletic, apocryphal and Arabic religious worlds are being engaged without assuming one direct textual source for every parallel.

## Crucifixion

Q 4:157–158 has generated multiple Islamic interpretations.

A common later interpretation holds that someone else was made to appear like Jesus and was crucified in his place, but the Qur'anic wording itself and its rhetorical target require separate analysis.

Reference:
- David Lloyd Dusenbury, "Christ Was Not the One Crucified":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/39846/chapter-abstract/339999517

## Christology

Gabriel Said Reynolds summarizes the Qur'an's Jesus as human prophet/messenger while also noting unusually elevated language such as spirit-from-God.

Reference:
- "The Islamic Christ":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199641901.013.10

That tension should be preserved rather than reduced to "Islam says Jesus was just a normal man."

## Maryam

Maryam is not a minor appendage. Qur'anic tradition honors her, affirms miraculous conception and makes her central to Isa's identity while disputing Christian conclusions about divine sonship.

Comparative work must distinguish:
- Christian Marian development;
- Jewish countertraditions about Miriam;
- Qur'anic Maryam;
- later Islamic exegesis.

## Historical ceiling

The Qur'an is high-value evidence for:
- seventh-century proclamation;
- late-antique reception;
- Islamic Christology;
- Maryam tradition;
- polemical/revelatory reframing.

It is not independent first-century eyewitness evidence for:
- birth details;
- crucifixion mechanism;
- Yeshua's exact self-understanding.

## Manuscript consequence

Book VI gives Isa and Maryam their own chapter in the Qur'an's conceptual world.

Book IV does not use the Qur'an to override first-century execution evidence.

Book VII may compare Qur'anic "word/spirit" language with other metaphysical systems only after source semantics are established.
