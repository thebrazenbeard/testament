# Wealth, Debt, and Economy V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

Modern readers routinely flatten ancient economic language into "rich" and "poor" as though both were stable modern income brackets.

They were not.

The 2023 Oxford Handbook treatment emphasizes that wealth and poverty labels cover a wide range of material positions and uses explicit economic scaling to compare the social worlds presupposed by Q, Matthew, and Luke.

Reference:
- Thomas R. Blanton IV, "Wealth, Poverty, Economy":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.6

## Source differences matter

Blanton's comparative work argues that Q, Luke, and Matthew imagine economic situations differently; Matthew can use economically counterfactual scenarios in parables for theological purposes.

That means a parable's economy cannot simply be treated as a documentary photograph of Galilean village life.

## Ethical guard

The book's agency thesis must never turn poverty into personal failure.

"Take responsibility for your life" is compatible with:
- exploitation;
- debt traps;
- imperial taxation;
- illness;
- inherited status;
- slavery;
- structural inequality.

Agency is bounded by circumstance.

## Manuscript consequence

Economic language should feel concrete: wages, grain, rent, debt, meals, land, patrons, tolls, household risk.

Book VIII may demand agency without pretending everyone begins with equal power.
