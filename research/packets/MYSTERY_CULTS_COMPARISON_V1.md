## Category warning

"Mystery religions" is a modern comparative category and has often been distorted by attempts either:
- to prove Christianity unique;
- or to prove Christianity copied pagan saviour cults.

The Oxford Classical Dictionary notes that older scholarship often defined mystery religions through a Christian-looking model of dying/rising gods and personal salvation, making the comparison circular.

Reference:
- Richard Gordon, "mysteries":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549687338

## What can responsibly be compared

Greco-Roman initiation cults include:
- Eleusinian mysteries;
- Isis-related initiations;
- Mithraic mysteries;
- other local/initiation practices.

Early Christianity also develops:
- baptism/initiation;
- communal meal/Eucharist;
- anointing;
- ritual transformation;
- group boundary;
- sacred narratives.

Oxford's ritual handbook explicitly places early Christian initiation in a wider Greco-Roman environment of initiation rites.

Reference:
- "Initiation":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28032/chapter-abstract/211911527

## Mithras

Roman Mithraism flourishes especially in the second and third centuries CE and is known heavily through archaeology/iconography.

Reference:
- Roger Beck, *The Religion of the Mithras Cult in the Roman Empire*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/6200

The cult's developed Roman form should not be projected backward into Yeshua's Galilee.

The popular claim "Mithras had twelve disciples, died, and rose after three days just like Jesus" must never enter Testament without primary evidence; the standard material record does not justify that meme.

## Ritual convergence

Early Christian ritual should be compared structurally with surrounding ritual life.

But:
shared meal != copied Eucharist;
washing/initiation != copied baptism;
salvation language != same salvation doctrine.

## Testament consequence

Book V/VI can show Christianity as one ancient ritualizing movement among others while preserving its Jewish genealogy and distinctive developments.

Comparison is not debunking.
Comparison is not proof of borrowing.
