## Family evidence

Early Christian sources preserve:
- a mother named Mary/Miriam;
- brothers, including James;
- sisters in Mark/Matthew without names;
- conflict or distance between Yeshua and family in some traditions;
- later major leadership by James.

Paul's reference to James as "the brother of the Lord" is especially important because it predates the narrative Gospels.

## Mark 6:3

On-Theo's dedicated packet finds that the mainstream critical reading calls Yeshua "the carpenter, the son of Mary" without a patronymic.

The paternity-slur reading is possible and defended by some scholars, but competing scholarship shows that a matronymic does not automatically encode illegitimacy.

Current ceiling:
- "son of Mary" reading: strong textual proposition;
- paternity stigma: contested interpretation;
- Panthera paternity: unsupported by Mark 6:3.

## Panthera / Pandera

- Celsus's explicit Panthera accusation as preserved by Origen;

- Vienna Tosefta and Yerushalmi variants/traditions linking Jesus-name forms with Panteira/Pandera;
- no established direct lineage from Celsus to the rabbinic complexes;

## Circularity guard
