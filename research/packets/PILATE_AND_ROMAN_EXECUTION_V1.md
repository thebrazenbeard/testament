# Pontius Pilate and Roman Execution V1

## Material anchor

The Caesarea inscription names Pontius Pilatus and identifies him as *praefectus Iudaeae*.

A Cambridge discussion notes that the inscription discovered at Caesarea in 1961 establishes "prefect" rather than the later familiar "procurator" as Pilate's official title under Tiberius.

Reference:
- "Sejanus, Pilate, and the Date of the Crucifixion":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/abs/sejanus-pilate-and-the-date-of-the-crucifixion/1E484D644E8264D507BA93B2E20BCC3E

Joan Taylor combines Pilate's inscription and coinage with Philo's report to argue that Pilate's administration expressed Roman imperial religious symbolism.

Reference:
- Joan E. Taylor, "Pontius Pilate and the Imperial Cult in Roman Judaea":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688506000300

## Tenure and source portrait

Standard chronology places Pilate's Judaean administration roughly 26–36/37 CE.

For historical reconstruction, keep distinct:
- Philo;
- Josephus;
- material inscription/coins;
- each Gospel's literary Pilate;
- later Christian rehabilitation/demonization.

Helen Bond's *Pontius Pilate in History and Interpretation* remains a major source-history study.

## Crucifixion as Roman state violence

All canonical Passion narratives converge on Roman crucifixion under Pilate even while distributing moral blame differently.

The "King of the Jews" charge/titulus belongs to a political register.

A Cambridge overview notes that Roman historical framing would naturally place such an execution in the context of religio-political agitation and Roman maintenance of the *pax Romana*.

Reference:
- Joel B. Green, "Crucifixion," *Cambridge Companion to Jesus*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-jesus/crucifixion/87CCF95768E96AD9015565B735313E80

Recent scholarship by Nathanael Andrade argues that Pilate could plausibly have interpreted Yeshua's Temple activity and regal/messianic implications as politically dangerous conduct.

Reference:
- Nathanael J. Andrade, *Killing the Messiah* (2025):
  https://academic.oup.com/book/59249

This is a scholarly reconstruction, not a recovered Roman case file.

## Archaeological crucifixion evidence

The remains commonly identified as Yehohanan/Yehohanan son of Hagkol include a first-century calcaneus pierced by an iron nail and provide rare direct archaeological evidence of Roman crucifixion practice in Judaea.

Medical/historical overview:
- PMID 35333959:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35333959/

## Manuscript consequence

Book II should depict crucifixion first as Roman political punishment and public degradation before later atonement theology is allowed to reinterpret it.

Pilate must not be softened merely because some Gospel trajectories increasingly shift responsibility away from Rome.
