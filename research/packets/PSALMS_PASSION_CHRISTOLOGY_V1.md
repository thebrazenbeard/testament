## Priority psalms

### Psalm 22
Later Gospel passion narratives employ language/images associated with Psalm 22:
- abandonment;
- mockery;
- garments/lots;
- righteous suffering.

The historical task is to distinguish:
- events independently remembered;
- scripturally shaped narration;
- direct quotation;
- later liturgical interpretation.

### Psalm 69
Provides righteous-sufferer/zeal/reproach imagery important to early-Christian reception.

### Psalm 2
Royal/divine-son language becomes central to messianic/Christological interpretation.

### Psalm 110
"Sit at my right hand" becomes one of the most important scriptural texts for exaltation Christology.

## Early Christian interpretive method

Matthew Bates's work on prosopological exegesis argues that early Christians could read scriptural dialogues as speech between divine/Christological persons, including Psalms 2 and 110.

Reference:
- *The Birth of the Trinity*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/9997
- "Divine Dialogues from the Dawn of Time":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/9997/chapter-abstract/157371510

Whether Bates's "earliest high Christology" thesis persuades in full, the interpretive technique is important reception evidence.

## Historical ceiling

High:
- early Christians used Psalms to interpret Yeshua's suffering/exaltation.

Separate:
- whether Yeshua himself used each psalm self-referentially;
- whether passion details are remembered events that happened to echo scripture;
- whether some narrative details arose through scriptural narration.

## Testament consequence

Book IV should sometimes let scripture be what grieving followers reach for to make sense of execution.

That is historically more honest than making every passion detail a supernatural prediction fulfilled on schedule.
