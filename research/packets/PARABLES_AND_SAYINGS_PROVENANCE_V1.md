# Parables and Sayings Provenance V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

Parables are central to Yeshua's literary memory, but "Jesus told parables" does not mean every extant parable form is an exact transcript.

Parables exist inside:
- Mark;
- Matthew;
- Luke;
- hypothesized/shared sayings traditions;
- Gospel of Thomas;
- later Christian literature.

## Gospel of Thomas

Thomas contains 114 sayings/parables, lacks a continuous life/death/resurrection narrative, and overlaps substantially with Synoptic sayings while also differing in theology and literary setting.

Reference:
- Stephen J. Patterson, "Gospel of Thomas," Oxford Bibliographies:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/obo/9780195393361-0047
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

Thomas survives completely in a later Coptic manuscript and partly in earlier Greek Oxyrhynchus fragments. Composition history and dependence remain debated.

## Provenance rule

For every parable/saying:
- identify every extant version;
- identify likely literary dependence;
- preserve significant variants;
- test Jewish scriptural/wisdom/apocalyptic context;
- distinguish core image from Gospel-specific interpretation;
- note allegorical expansion where argued;
- record whether Thomas supplies an independent, dependent, or unresolved parallel.

## No canonical default

Canonical status does not automatically make the canonical version earliest.

Noncanonical status does not automatically make another version earlier.

Each unit must be evaluated.

## Literary opportunity

Book III can place materially different versions beside one another and sometimes allow the contradiction to remain visible.

A literary synthesis can create one elegant form only if the apparatus records that the elegance is ours.

## Manuscript consequence

Build a saying-level ledger before sustained Book III prose.

Do not reconstruct a hypothetical "original saying" merely because a shorter version feels more primitive.
