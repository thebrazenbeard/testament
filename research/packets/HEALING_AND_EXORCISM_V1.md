# Healing and Exorcism V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Source prominence

Healing and exorcism are not decorative side stories in the Synoptic tradition.

They occupy a large portion of Yeshua's represented public activity and are tied to authority and kingdom proclamation.

Reference:
- Graham Stanton, "Miracles and Exorcisms," *The Gospels and Jesus*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/50340/chapter-abstract/422785916

Oxford Bibliographies' current exorcism overview notes the extraordinary prominence of exorcism in Synoptic Jesus traditions and the way those traditions connect exorcism/healing with the kingdom.

Reference:
- Graham H. Twelftree, "Exorcism":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/obo/9780195393361-0272

## Ancient-body guard

Do not translate every possession story into a modern psychiatric diagnosis.

Do not translate every healing claim into a supernatural event.

Ancient categories of body, illness, spirit and social personhood differ from modern biomedical categories.

Reference:
- Meghan Henning, "Healing and Exorcism," *Oxford Handbook of the Synoptic Gospels*:
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45760/chapter-abstract/398727877

Henning argues that exorcism narratives should be studied within ancient healing discourse rather than treated as a completely separate category.

## Historical reconstruction

Strong at reputation level:
- Yeshua was remembered very early and extensively as a healer/exorcist.

Plausible:
- some kind of healing/exorcistic practice was a major feature of the historical ministry.

Unresolved:
- medical diagnoses;
- mechanism;
- exact number of events;
- nature-miracle historicity;
- whether specific stories are independent or literary doubles.

## Competing explanatory layers

Retain:
- genuine physical recovery;
- psychosomatic/expectation effects;
- ritual/social healing;
- altered-state phenomena;
- misdiagnosed illness;
- literary/theological construction;
- supernatural interpretation;
- mixtures.

Risto Uro's ritual-healing work cautions against reducing all healing/possession phenomena to one social-scientific model.

Reference:
- https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199661176.003.0005

## Manuscript consequence

Book II should depict healing as relational and social as well as bodily.

The narrator should describe what witnesses experience and what changes, rather than supplying modern diagnoses or omniscient supernatural mechanics.
