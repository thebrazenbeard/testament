# Yohanan / John the Baptist V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Question

What can be established independently about John, and what can responsibly be said about Yeshua's relationship to him?

## Josephus

Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.116–119, describes John as a good man who urged righteousness toward others and piety toward God and used baptism in connection with a life already morally purified. Josephus says Herod Antipas feared John's influence over crowds and had him imprisoned and killed at Machaerus.

Primary access:
- Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.116:
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0146%3Abook%3D18%3Asection%3D116

This is independent of the Gospel narratives at the level of source tradition.

## Gospel tradition

The canonical Gospels agree that:
- John was a public baptizing/repentance figure;
- Yeshua was associated with him;
- Yeshua was baptized by John in the Synoptic tradition;
- John's arrest/death precedes or overlaps Yeshua's developing ministry;
- John is interpreted through scriptural and theological frames that differ by Gospel.

The Gospel of John complicates the picture further by portraying overlapping baptizing activity around Jesus' movement.

## Historical relationship

Recent scholarship does not reduce the issue to one consensus.

A 2020 *Journal of Biblical Literature* article argues that Yeshua's public identity developed in the shadow of John's earlier and more popular ministry and that the relationship itself likely required explanation.

Reference:
- Tucker S. Ferda, "The Historicity of Confusion: Jesus, John the Baptist, and the Construction of Public Identity":
  DOI 10.15699/JBL.1394.2020.7

Graham Twelftree has argued for a closer ongoing baptist discipleship model:
- DOI 10.1163/174551909X447356

Other scholarship challenges whether Yeshua was ever John's close disciple.

Current state:
- Yeshua baptized by John: strong historical reconstruction.
- Yeshua as John's close disciple: plausible but not settled.

## John's death traditions

Mark/Matthew and Josephus should not be mechanically harmonized.

Ross Kraemer argues that the Herodias/daughter banquet story is historically suspect and that Josephus and the Gospel death narratives should be treated as separate narratives rather than fused.

Reference:
- Ross S. Kraemer, "Implicating Herodias and Her Daughter in the Death of John the Baptizer":
  DOI 10.2307/27638363

## Manuscript consequence

Book I ends with Yeshua approaching John because John is not scenery. He is an independently attested major movement leader whose relationship to Yeshua may be one of the most important causal transitions in Yeshua's public life.

Book II must not have John publicly identify Yeshua as the uniquely predicted Messiah as historical fact merely because later Gospel theology does so.
