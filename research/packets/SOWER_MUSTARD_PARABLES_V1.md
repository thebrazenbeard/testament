# Sower and Mustard Seed Parables V1

Status: SAYING_PACKET / WITNESS_CONTROL_ACTIVE / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Sower versions

- Mark 4:3–20;
- Matthew 13:3–23;
- Luke 8:5–15;
- Thomas 9.

Mark distinguishes the parable and an extended interpretation.

Historical work must ask whether:
- both belong together;
- the interpretation reflects Yeshua;
- the interpretation reflects early community allegory;
- later Synoptics reshape Mark.

Alicia Myers's 2021 Oxford overview treats the Sower as integral to Mark's literary account of hearing, fear and faith, reminding Testament that Mark's form is already narrative theology rather than a neutral container.

Reference:
- Alicia D. Myers, "Mark's Story":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190926809.003.0002

Thomas 9 preserves the sower without Mark's full interpretive discourse.

That difference is important but does not by itself prove Thomas preserves the earlier form.

## Mustard versions

- Mark 4:30–32;
- Matthew 13:31–32;
- Luke 13:18–19;
- Thomas 20.

The image moves from very small seed to unexpectedly large growth.

Each version alters detail and placement.

The project must resist the automatic rule:
shorter = earlier.

## Thomas comparison

Current Oxford scholarship emphasizes that Thomas/Synoptic comparison is valuable even where dependence cannot be globally decided and that the texts represent very different literary/theological strategies.

Reference:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

Thomas 9 and Thomas 20 are directly extant in the Coptic Gospel of Thomas preserved in Nag Hammadi Codex II.

The surviving Greek Thomas fragments do not cover either saying:
- P.Oxy. 654 preserves the opening through logion 7;
- P.Oxy. 1 preserves portions of logia 26–33;
- P.Oxy. 655 preserves logion 24 and portions of 36–39.

Therefore Testament has no currently bound Greek manuscript witness for:
- Thomas 9;
- Thomas 20.

Any Greek back-translation of those parables is reconstruction.

### Sower

The comparison must keep separate:
- Mark's parable plus extended interpretation;
- Matthew and Luke as Synoptic reworkings within a dependence problem;
- Thomas 9 as a distinct Coptic textual form without Mark's extended interpretation.

Thomas's lack of the Markan interpretation is real textual evidence.
It is not proof that Thomas preserves the earlier historical parable.

The common shortcut:

> shorter / less allegorized = earlier

is prohibited as a method.

### Mustard

Mark, Matthew, Luke and Thomas 20 all preserve the small-seed/growth image in materially related but non-identical forms.

Again:
- compactness is not a date;
- one Coptic Thomas witness is not an independent historical vote by itself;
- Matthew and Luke cannot be counted independently from Mark without dependence analysis.

### Per-parable redactional-fingerprint review

The dependence problem is not identical for Thomas 9 and Thomas 20.

#### Thomas 9 — Sower

Christopher Tuckett has included Thomas 9 among local cases where Synoptic redactional influence may be detectable.

But the autonomy case remains substantial.

Thomas 9 lacks:
- Mark's narrative introduction;
- Mark's interpretive invitation;
- the transitional material leading to explanation;
- Mark's extended allegorical interpretation.

Stephen Patterson uses precisely that absence as evidence compatible with an autonomous rendering of the oral parable tradition.

John Kloppenborg has likewise argued that a case for autonomy or even priority can be made because Thomas 9 avoids many recognizable Synoptic redactional elements.

The opposite argument remains live:
several Sower studies treat Thomas 9 as a reworked Synoptic/Markan form.

Current state:

**RELATION_DISPUTED / AUTONOMY ARGUMENT SUBSTANTIAL / SYNOPTIC-REDACTION ARGUMENT PRESENT.**

The absence of Markan interpretation is evidence.
It is not proof of independence.

#### Thomas 20 — Mustard seed

Thomas 20 produces a somewhat different balance.

Tuckett also includes Thomas 20 among local possible Synoptic-influence cases.

Published dependence arguments point to structural similarities with Mark:
- small-seed → growth structure;
- the "smallest seed" motif;
- similar temporal/growth sequencing;
- soil/ground correspondences.

Some scholars therefore treat Thomas 20 as a reworked Markan form.

Counterarguments remain serious.

Hedrick, Patterson, Koester and others have argued that Thomas's simpler plant imagery and differences from the Synoptic apocalyptic-tree development are compatible with independent/common oral tradition.

Current state:

**RELATION_DISPUTED / MARKAN-DEPENDENCE ARGUMENT LOCALLY WEIGHTY / INDEPENDENCE-COMMON-TRADITION COUNTERMODEL LIVE.**

### Shared methodological result

Neither parable permits the shortcut:

> Thomas is shorter, therefore Thomas is earlier.

Nor does structural similarity permit:

> Thomas resembles Mark, therefore Thomas copied Mark directly.

The two logia remain one Thomas source, but their dependence problems are evaluated separately.

References:
- Christopher M. Tuckett, "Thomas and the Synoptics," *Novum Testamentum* 30 (1988): 132–157.
- Stephen J. Patterson, *The Gospel of Thomas and Christian Origins* (Brill, 2013).
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics" (2023), DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27.

### Dependence rule

Melissa Harl Sellew's 2023 treatment remains the current methodological guard for Thomas/Synoptic comparison: useful close comparison does not require pretending that one global dependence theory is already solved.

Reference:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics":
  DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27

For Book III, the practical rule is:

**Show the transmitted forms; expose dependence; do not turn formal simplicity into chronology.**

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- sower and mustard traditions are early and widely transmitted in the Jesus-sayings tradition.

Unresolved:
- exact earliest wording;
- whether Mark's sower interpretation is secondary;
- Thomas independence;
- exact kingdom meaning of each image.

## Manuscript consequence

Book III may present the compact parable forms with source variants around them.

It should not print a reconstructed "original parable" unless the reconstruction rules are explicit.
