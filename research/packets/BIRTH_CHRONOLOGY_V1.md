# Birth Chronology and Infancy Traditions V1

Status: RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Core problem

Matthew and Luke both place Yeshua's birth in the reign of Herod the Great, but Luke also associates the birth with a census under Quirinius.

Standard historical chronology places Herod's death in 4 BCE and Quirinius's census of Judaea in 6/7 CE, creating a real chronological tension.

## Matthew

Narrative features include:
- Herod;
- magi;
- star;
- Bethlehem;
- massacre;
- flight to Egypt;
- settlement in Nazareth after return.

## Luke

Narrative features include:
- annunciations;
- census;
- travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem;
- shepherds;
- presentation in Jerusalem;
- return to Nazareth.

## Non-harmonization rule

Do not silently create one continuous Christmas story containing every element from both Gospels.

The geographic logic differs.

The genealogies differ.

The chronology creates tension.

The literary/theological purposes differ.

## Historical ceiling

Strong:
- Matthew and Luke preserve distinct infancy/birth traditions associating Yeshua with Bethlehem and Davidic identity.

Plausible:
- Yeshua was raised in Nazareth/Galilee.

Unresolved:
- exact birthplace;
- exact birth year;
- census mechanism;
- historicity of magi/star;
- massacre;
- flight to Egypt;
- historical relationship between the two infancy traditions.

## Manuscript consequence

Book I chapter "A Child With Too Many Stories" should present the birth narratives as competing literary/traditional narratives surrounding a person whose adult life became important enough to draw scriptural meaning backward into infancy.

The literary text may dramatize uncertainty; it may not disguise harmonization as recovered biography.
