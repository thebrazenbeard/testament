# Islamic Crucifixion Reception V1

Status: BOOK_VI_RESEARCH_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Qur'anic base

Qur'an 4:157–158 denies or reframes the opponents' claim that they killed/crucified the Messiah Jesus son of Mary and states that it was made to appear so to them, followed by divine raising.

The wording has generated multiple interpretive traditions.

## Later substitution tradition

A widespread later interpretation has another person substituted/made to resemble Jesus.

That specific narrative detail should not automatically be read into the Qur'anic wording.

David Lloyd Dusenbury's historical study traces the non-crucifixion claim and its interpretive history rather than assuming one timeless Muslim explanation.

Reference:
- *I Judge No One* / relevant crucifixion scholarship;
- "Christ Was Not the One Crucified":

## Research strata

Keep separate:
- Qur'anic wording;
- early qira'at/readings;
- early tafsir;
- hadith;
- prophetic stories;

- modern apologetics.

Each later layer can add narrative mechanisms not explicit in the Qur'an.

## Historical method

For first-century biography:
Qur'an 4:157 is a seventh-century theological/revelatory claim.

It does not outweigh earlier Roman/Christian historical evidence merely because a believer treats it as revelation.

For reception history:
it is central.

## Comparative opportunity

Islam preserves a striking alternative:
- miraculous conception;
- Messiah title;
- miracles;
- prophetic status;
- rejection of divine sonship;
- rejection/reframing of crucifixion;
- divine raising.

That combination prevents Testament from treating later religious reception as a simple continuum from one Christian premise.

## Testament consequence

Book VI should present Islamic interpretation in its own theological integrity, not merely as "Christianity with the Trinity removed."

Book IV remains governed by first-century historical evidence.
