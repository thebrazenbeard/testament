# Pharisees, Sadducees, Scribes, and Jewish Diversity V1

Status: HISTORICAL_CONTEXT_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Core correction

"Pharisee" is not a synonym for hypocrite.

"Sadducee" is not a synonym for corrupt priest.

"Scribe" is not a religious party.

These categories overlap differently across sources.

## Pharisees

The evidence comes from:
- Josephus;
- New Testament texts;
- Dead Sea Scroll polemics sometimes linked to Pharisaic positions;
- much later rabbinic sources.

The Cambridge History of Judaism warns that later rabbinic self-history can inform but cannot simply function as a blueprint for first-century Pharisaism.

Reference:
- "The Pharisees":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-judaism/pharisees/3650D7D863465D7000DFFA8E9A457661

The 2021 multidisciplinary volume edited by Joseph Sievers and Amy-Jill Levine explicitly attempts to correct historically damaging caricatures and emphasizes that Pharisaic legal interpretation could be adaptive, lenient and innovative rather than merely severe.

Reference:
- *The Pharisees*:
  https://www.eerdmans.com/9780802879295/the-pharisees/

## Gospel polemic

Matthew's harshest anti-Pharisee rhetoric may reflect later community boundary conflict as well as memories of historical dispute.

A 2014 JBL article on Matthew's "serpents/vipers" language explicitly situates the invective in community-definition and postwar polemical contexts.

Reference:
- Michael P. Knowles, "Serpents, Scribes, and Pharisees":
  DOI 10.1353/JBL.2014.0008

Thus:
Gospel polemic is evidence that conflict mattered.
It is not neutral ethnography of every Pharisee.

## Sadducees

Sadducees are strongly associated in literary sources with priestly/aristocratic circles and particular legal/theological positions.

But "Sadducee" should not automatically be applied to every chief priest or Temple official.

## Scribes

A scribe is a role/expertise category involving texts/law/administration, not one sect.

A scribe may have Pharisaic alignment, priestly connection, another affiliation, or none recoverable.

## Ordinary Jews

Most Jews in Galilee/Judaea were not reducible to named parties.

Book I must represent:
- households;
- villages;
- pilgrims;
- artisans;
- farmers;
- fishers;
- women;
- children;
- priests;
- local synagogue participants;
without forcing everyone into Pharisee/Sadducee/Essene boxes.

## Manuscript consequence

When Yeshua disputes a Pharisee, write a dispute between Jews.

Do not let later Christian identity transform every disagreement into "Jesus versus Judaism."
