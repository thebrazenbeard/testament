# Gospel of Thomas / Synoptic Dependence — Research Matrix V1

Status: RESEARCH_MATRIX / SOURCE_LEVEL_CONTROL

Purpose:
- prevent a single global dependence theory from silently deciding every Thomas parallel;
- preserve the strongest arguments on both dependence and autonomy/common-tradition sides;
- give Book III a repeatable saying-level method.

## Core problem

The Gospel of Thomas contains numerous sayings with Synoptic parallels.

The live scholarly questions are not exhausted by:
- dependent;
- independent.

Possible transmission routes include:
- direct literary use of one or more Synoptic Gospels;
- indirect literary influence;
- secondary orality after written Gospel circulation;
- common oral tradition;
- shared written sources now lost;
- mixed composition using several of these routes;
- saying-specific histories inside one compiled work.

Therefore Testament rejects one global Boolean dependence flag.

## Position matrix

### Mark Goodacre — strong familiarity / dependence case

Primary work:
- Mark Goodacre, *Thomas and the Gospels: The Case for Thomas's Familiarity with the Synoptics* (2012).

Position:
- Thomas is familiar with and draws on the Synoptic Gospels.
- Key evidence includes apparent Matthean/Lukan redactional features and "diagnostic shards" of characteristic Synoptic wording.

Methodological value:
- redactional vocabulary or sequence that is difficult to explain from generic oral tradition can be strong dependence evidence;
- very close verbal agreement deserves more weight than vague thematic similarity.

Guard:
- diagnostic overlap must still be evaluated per saying;
- familiarity with one Synoptic form does not prove every Thomas parallel is borrowed.

### Simon Gathercole — Synoptic influence, mechanism not always recoverable

Primary works:
- Simon Gathercole, *The Composition of the Gospel of Thomas: Original Language and Influences* (2012).
- Simon Gathercole, "Thomas Revisited" (2014), DOI 10.1177/0142064X14520658.

Position:
- Thomas is influenced by the Synoptic Gospels;
- order and genre do not decide dependence;
- Thomas also draws on known and unknown sources;
- the means of Synoptic influence may remain unknown.

Methodological value:
- a text can be influenced without preserving the source in Synoptic-like order;
- mixed-source composition is compatible with genuine Synoptic dependence.

Guard:
- "influenced by" is weaker than a claim of direct copying in each saying.

### Christopher Tuckett — dependence highly likely for at least some sayings

Primary review:
- Christopher Tuckett, "The Gospel of Thomas: Gathercole and Goodacre" (2013).
- DOI 10.1017/S0036930613000070.

Position:
- broadly agrees that Thomas depends directly or indirectly on at least Matthew and Luke;
- emphasizes that redactional elements can provide strong evidence;
- explicitly cautions against premature polarization.

Methodological value:
- dependence can be highly likely locally without requiring a single totalizing composition model.

### Stephen Patterson — oral/common-tradition and autonomy pushback

Primary response:
- Stephen J. Patterson, "Twice More—Thomas and the Synoptics" (2014).
- DOI 10.1177/0142064X14521947.

Position:
- Goodacre/Gathercole give too much weight to ambiguous dependence evidence;
- brief verbatim agreements may be formulaic or arise from common tradition;
- some parallels may reflect Q-like or oral tradition;
- numerous Thomas traditions may be independent of canonical Gospels.

Methodological value:
- short verbal identity is weak when the phrase is formulaic or easily preserved orally;
- oral circulation remains a live alternative explanation.

Guard:
- independence is not presumed merely because Thomas is shorter or non-narrative.

### John Kloppenborg — mixed anthology / multiple streams

Primary response:
- John S. Kloppenborg, "A New Synoptic Problem: Mark Goodacre and Simon Gathercole on Thomas" (2014).
- DOI 10.1177/0142064X14520653.

Position:
- the case for Thomas's Synoptic knowledge is partial and sometimes unconvincing;
- some Thomas material is substantially autonomous;
- Thomas may be better modeled as a school text / anthology drawing on multiple parallel streams.

Methodological value:
- composition-level plurality is a serious alternative to one-source dependence models.

### Melissa Harl Sellew — comparison without requiring a global verdict

Primary synthesis:
- Melissa Harl Sellew, "The Gospel of Thomas and the Synoptics," *Oxford Handbook of the Synoptic Gospels* (2023).
- DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190887452.013.27.

Position:
- deliberately moves away from dependence-versus-autonomy as the only useful question;
- comparative analysis of style, theology, narrative absence, genre and community formation is valuable even when literary direction remains unresolved.

Methodological value:
- Book III can compare forms rigorously without pretending every comparison must produce a dependence verdict.

## Testament dependence hierarchy

For each Thomas/Synoptic saying cluster, weigh evidence in roughly this order:

1. **Distinctive redactional fingerprint**
   - vocabulary, sequence, theological framing or editorial combination strongly characteristic of a known Synoptic redactor.
   - Strongest local evidence for dependence.

2. **Extended non-formulaic verbal agreement**
   - substantial wording that is difficult to explain as generic oral preservation.
   - Strong, but language/transmission history must be considered.

3. **Order / combination agreement**
   - especially meaningful if an otherwise unusual combination recurs.
   - Not decisive by itself.

4. **Short formulaic verbal agreement**
   - weak to moderate evidence;
   - oral/common tradition remains highly plausible.

5. **Shared motif / theme**
   - weak dependence evidence by itself.

6. **Relative brevity**
   - NOT a chronology test.
   - Shorter does not automatically mean earlier.

7. **Absence of Synoptic context in Thomas**
   - NOT proof of independence.
   - Thomas's genre itself strips narrative context.

## Greek-fragment rule

Where a Thomas saying has surviving Greek evidence:
- prioritize the actual Greek fragment over retroversion from Coptic;
- compare Greek-to-Greek wording before making claims from Coptic translation;
- mark every restoration;
- do not use Greek fragments from other logia as generic evidence.

## Historical-attestation guard

Even if Thomas is independent of a Synoptic Gospel at one saying:
- that does not automatically make the saying historical Yeshua speech.

Even if Thomas depends on a Synoptic Gospel:
- that does not make the form historically worthless;
- it remains evidence for reception, redaction and early Christian interpretation.

Dependence and historicity are separate axes.

## Current Testament consequence

Existing Book III controls should retain their local dependence labels.

This matrix supplies source-level method and bibliography.

No existing cluster is silently upgraded or downgraded merely because this file exists.

Future hostile review should attack:
- local dependence claims that exceed their evidence;
- unacknowledged redactional fingerprints;
- formulaic agreements treated as decisive copying;
- shorter-form-equals-earlier reasoning;
- duplicated same-work Thomas forms counted as independent attestation.
