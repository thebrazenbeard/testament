# Roman and Provincial Context

## Political frame

The narrative must distinguish:
- Herodian client kingship;
- tetrarchies after Herod;
- direct Roman administration in Judaea;
- Galilee under Antipas during much of Yeshua's life;
- prefect/procurator terminology by period;
- Pilate's office and tenure;
- high-priestly politics;
- Roman execution authority.

## Crucifixion

Treat crucifixion as:
- Roman state violence;
- public degradation;
- deterrent spectacle;
- socially and politically meaningful punishment.

Do not reduce it to an abstract theological symbol before depicting what the practice meant in Roman power.

## Imperial ideology

Research:
- emperor cult and titles;
- "good news" / euangelion in wider Greek usage without claiming every Christian use is direct borrowing;
- savior/lord language;
- peace and victory propaganda;
- taxation and census;
- military presence;
- citizenship.

## External witnesses

Tacitus, Pliny, Suetonius and others are sources for later Roman knowledge of Christians and imperial response, not eyewitnesses to Yeshua's ministry.

## Material lane

Use coins, inscriptions, papyri, administrative records, roads, settlement archaeology, burial evidence, and military records to constrain the world even when they say nothing about Yeshua personally.
