# Resurrection Research Model

The project must keep at least four questions separate.

## 1. What was proclaimed?

Very early Christian sources proclaim that Yeshua was raised and appeared.

This is a historical claim about the movement's proclamation.

## 2. What experiences were reported?

Sources describe appearances in differing ways and sequences.

Research:
- Paul's appearance language;
- Gospel narratives;
- James tradition;
- Peter/Cephas;
- women;
- group experiences;
- later expansions.

## 3. What historical event produced those reports?

Competing models include:
- bodily resurrection;
- visionary/revelatory experiences;
- grief-related experiences;
- cognitive/social contagion models;
- legendary development;
- combinations rather than one mechanism.

No model is selected merely because it suits the author's metaphysics.

## 4. What becomes possible under simulation theory?

If host-level state restoration is granted as a hypothetical premise, re-instantiation becomes mechanically conceivable.

That observation changes modal possibility, not historical probability.

It cannot be used to convert resurrection proclamation into proof of a simulator.

## Personal identity problem

Even a perfect copied state raises questions:
- same person or duplicate?
- continuity of subjectivity?
- memory continuity?
- branch/fork identity?
- body continuity?

Book VII may explore these without pretending Book IV has solved them.
