# Agency Ethics

The project's moral conclusion must survive every metaphysical branch.

## Branch A — personal creator God exists

Agency still matters because delegated moral responsibility is not erased by creation.

## Branch B — divine immanence / panentheism

If persons participate in divinity, passivity becomes less coherent, not more.

## Branch C — simulation

Being instantiated inside a controlled environment does not eliminate local causation, responsibility, suffering, or choice as experienced by agents.

## Branch D — naturalism

No external rescuer makes human action more urgent.

## Branch E — metaphysics unknown

Uncertainty does not suspend responsibility.

## Core tests

A moral claim should ask:
- Does it increase the reader's capacity to choose deliberately?
- Does it preserve responsibility for consequences?
- Does it reduce obedience-based moral outsourcing?
- Does it avoid blaming victims for conditions outside their control?
- Does it distinguish agency from omnipotence?

## Important distinction

"Take control of your life" does not mean:
- everything that happens to you is chosen;
- structural power is imaginary;
- illness or trauma is voluntary;
- success is guaranteed by mindset.

Agency is bounded.

The book's claim is that bounded agency is still yours.
