## Question audited

Patrick recalled a more specific story in which Miriam/Mary was raped by Tiberius Pantera, returned to her carpenter husband, was socially shunned, and the geography aligned with where Jesus/Yeshua was born.

This audit decomposes that memory into separately testable propositions. No component is promoted into biography merely because the pieces form a coherent story.

## Primary / ancient evidence

### Celsus as preserved by Origen

Origen, Contra Celsum 1.28 preserves Celsus's accusation that Jesus was born in a Jewish village to a poor woman, that her carpenter husband turned her out after she was accused/convicted of adultery, that she wandered for a time, and that she then gave birth to Jesus as an illegitimate child.

Origen, Contra Celsum 1.32 preserves the more specific accusation that, while pregnant, she was turned out by the carpenter to whom she had been betrothed because of adultery and bore a child to a soldier named Panthera.

Controls:
- Celsus supplies adultery/illicit-sex accusation, not sexual assault.
- Celsus supplies a carpenter betrothed/husband who rejects her; he does not say she later returned to him.
- Celsus does not identify the soldier as Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera.
- Celsus does not describe village/community shunning after a return.
- Celsus's "certain Jewish village" is not an independently specified conception or birth location that can be matched to the later Bingen soldier.

### Rabbinic ben Stada / ben Pandera material

Babylonian Shabbat 104b preserves a ben Stada / ben Pandeira complex in which Pappos ben Yehuda is named as the mother's husband, Pandeira as the man who had relations with her, and Miriam as the mother; the wordplay explains that she "strayed" from her husband.

Controls:
- this is not a rape narrative;
- the husband is Pappos ben Yehuda, not identified there as a carpenter;
- Pandeira is not identified as Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera;
- the passage is a later rabbinic complex whose identification with Jesus of Nazareth requires separate control;
- it does not provide the remembered return-and-shunning sequence.

Other rabbinic/Tosefta "Jesus son of Pantera/Pandera" material is relevant to the existence of a Pantera/Pandera countertradition but does not by itself supply the remembered assault / Tiberius / carpenter-return / shunning chain.

## Epigraphic evidence

CIL XIII 7514 records a real soldier:

- Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera;
- from Sidon;
- age 62;
- 40 years of military service;
- associated with cohors I sagittariorum;
- buried at/near Bingen in Germania.

This establishes the man and inscription. It does not establish:
- presence in Nazareth, Bethlehem, or another relevant Galilean/Judean location at the conception window;
- contact with Mary;
- sexual contact;
- coercion;
- biological paternity;
- connection to Joseph;
- connection to Celsus's Panthera.

A modern Roman-military inscription database notes that cohors I sagittariorum is securely attested in Judaea only much later, around 90 CE, and that several early imperial members of the unit are instead epigraphically attested at Bingen. It also warns against overconfidently fixing this Pantera's birth/service chronology so as to maximize overlap with Jesus's conception.

Therefore "Sidon is geographically near Galilee" is only broad regional proximity, not a demonstrated service-location bridge.

## Modern reconstruction layer

The specific rape hypothesis is modern, not an ancient source statement.

Jane Schaberg has been cited as a major modern defender of a rape-by-soldier reconstruction. James D. Tabor, who has extensively discussed Pantera traditions and the Bingen inscription, explicitly distinguishes that reconstruction from the ancient evidence and states that Celsus does not say rape. Tabor also rejects treating the Bingen Pantera as historically established as Jesus's father.

This matters because a modern narrative can easily fuse:
1. Celsus: adultery accusation + carpenter rejection + soldier Panthera;
2. rabbinic material: Miriam + Pandera/Pandeira + marital infidelity;
3. CIL XIII 7514: a real Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera from Sidon;
4. modern rape reconstruction;
5. broad Sidon/Galilee proximity;
into one continuous story that no ancient source actually tells.

## Historical ceiling

The strongest defensible historical statement at this cut is:

There is an ancient polemical/countertraditional Panthera/Pandera strand concerning Jesus's paternity, and there is independently a first-century soldier named Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera. The reviewed evidence does not identify them as the same man, does not make rape part of the ancient Panthera accusation, and does not support the remembered return/shunning/geographic-match sequence as one ancient narrative.

## Consequence for Testament

- Do not assign Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera as Yeshua's father.
- Do not portray rape as recovered history.
- Do not portray a return to Joseph/carpenter or community shunning as sourced history.

## Audit discipline

This audit is source classification, not a verdict on metaphysical claims about Jesus's conception. It only asks what the reviewed historical sources actually say and what they do not say.
