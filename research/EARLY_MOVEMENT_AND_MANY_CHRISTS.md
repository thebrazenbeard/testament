# Early Movement and the Many Christs

The post-death movement should not be narrated as though Nicene Christianity existed on Easter morning.

## Earliest nodes

Track separately:
- Jerusalem leadership;
- James;
- Peter/Cephas;
- the Twelve traditions;
- women witnesses/followers;
- Hellenist networks;
- Paul and gentile assemblies.

## Major fault lines

- Torah observance;
- circumcision;
- food/table fellowship;
- gentile inclusion;
- authority and apostleship;
- resurrection embodiment;
- wisdom/Logos Christology;
- pre-existence;
- adoption/exaltation models;
- Temple and sacrifice;
- relation to Israel;
- apocalypse and delay.

## Second-century plurality

Track without flattening:
- Marcion and Marcionite communities;
- Valentinian teachers;
- Thomasine trajectories;
- Ebionite/Jewish-Christian reports;
- proto-orthodox networks;
- Montanist prophecy;
- docetic tendencies;
- diverse gospel harmonies and local canons.

## Canon formation rule

The eventual New Testament canon is evidence for later reception and institutional convergence.

It does not retroactively erase texts and communities that lost.
