# Hamartia — Sin / Fault / Failure V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

English "sin" is a developed theological category.

Greek *hamartia* has a broader pre-Christian semantic history and is used in the Septuagint/Christian texts to carry biblical moral-religious concepts.

The Oxford Classical Dictionary stresses that Greek and Roman religion did not possess one exact equivalent of the later Christian concept "sin." It lists *hamartia/hamartēma* among several terms for fault, error, wrongdoing and transgression.

Reference:
- H. S. Versnel, "sin":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/550499269

## Etymology warning

The popular teaching:
"*hamartia* literally means missing the mark, like an archer, therefore biblical sin simply means making a mistake"
is inadequate.

Earlier Greek uses can involve error/failure, but Septuagintal and early-Christian usage participates in Jewish scriptural categories of:
- wrongdoing;
- guilt;
- rebellion;
- impurity;
- transgression;
- estrangement;
- divine judgment.

Etymology does not overrule corpus usage.

## Jesus-tradition questions

For each "sin" saying ask:
- individual act or condition?
- debt metaphor?
- ritual/moral boundary?
- forgiveness by God?
- interpersonal release?
- eschatological judgment?
- social exclusion?

## Testament consequence

Do not soften every *hamartia* into "mistake."

Do not inflate every instance into inherited metaphysical depravity either.

Translate proposition by proposition.
