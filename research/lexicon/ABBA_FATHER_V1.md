# Abba / Father V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

Popular Christian teaching often glosses Aramaic *abba* as "Daddy."

That is too narrow.

## Evidence

The New Testament preserves *abba* transliterated into Greek in:
- Mark 14:36;
- Romans 8:15;
- Galatians 4:6.

Christopher Bryan's 2022 study notes that *abba* could be used by children for fathers but also as a respectful address beyond small-child speech. His argument is not that divine father-language was uniquely un-Jewish; rather, Yeshua is remembered as characteristically using filial address to God and inviting followers into that relation.

Reference:
- Christopher Bryan, "The Synoptics and the Son of God: The Prayers of Jesus and the Prayers of His Followers":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197651261.003.0011

## Guardrails

Do not translate:
*abba* = "Daddy" as a lexical identity.

Possible English renderings depend on register:
- Father;
- my Father;
- dear Father;
- Father as intimate/respectful address.

The exact emotional tone is contextual.

## Historical significance

The interesting historical proposition is not:
"no Jew before Yeshua ever called God Father."

That is false as a broad claim.

The more defensible question is whether filial address to God was unusually characteristic in the remembered prayer language of Yeshua and his movement.

## Testament consequence

Book III may preserve *Abba* untranslated in selected places with a note.

Book VIII must not use "Daddy" theology as if it were philology.
