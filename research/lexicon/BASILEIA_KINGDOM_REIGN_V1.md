# Basileia — Kingdom / Kingship / Reign V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

"Kingdom of God" sounds to many modern readers like:
a place called Heaven.

That is not an adequate default.

Greek *basileia* can refer to:
- kingship;
- royal rule;
- reign;
- sovereignty;
- a kingdom/domain.

## Jewish scriptural context

A Cambridge treatment of Yeshua's teachings notes that while the exact phrase translated *basileia tou theou* is not common in the Hebrew canon, God's kingly rule is prominent in Psalms and Isaiah and becomes especially important in Second Temple eschatological/apocalyptic contexts.

Reference:
- "The Teachings of Jesus":
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/from-jesus-christ-to-christianity/teachings-of-jesus/CB58C5E40F9E48B0A59D0606407DFB67

## Translation strategy

Use:
- kingdom of God when preserving familiar wording matters;
- reign of God when dynamic ruling activity is foregrounded;
- kingship/sovereignty in explanatory notes where appropriate.

No global replacement.

## Political dimension

Ancient kingship language is inherently political even when divine.

That does not mean every kingdom saying is a coded anti-Roman manifesto.

It does mean Testament should not sterilize the language into private spirituality.

## Simulation firewall

"Governance-state transition" is a useful Book VII systems analogy.

It is not the lexical definition of *basileia*.

## Testament consequence

Book III should allow the kingdom sayings to retain multiple temporal/social dimensions:
- present;
- future;
- divine action;
- human response;
- judgment;
- restoration;
- community;
without forcing one system onto all sayings.
