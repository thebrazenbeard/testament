# Aiōnios — Age / Enduring / Eternal V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

Greek *aiōnios* is related to *aiōn* ("age"), but etymology alone does not determine meaning in a given sentence.

Both errors must be blocked:

1. *aiōnios* always mechanically means "eternal" in the later philosophical sense;
2. because it derives from "age," it can never mean everlasting/unending.

## Context rule

Semantic value comes from actual Greek usage and textual context, not root-word arithmetic.

A 2024 *Journal of Theological Studies* critique of universalist lexical arguments specifically rejects treating *aiōnios* as necessarily temporary or merely "of the world to come"; it argues that context and stylistic variation matter.

Reference:
- "Ilaria Ramelli's History of the 'Apokatastasis Doctrine': A Critical Assessment":
  https://academic.oup.com/jts/article-abstract/75/1/134/7614015

Older Greek/Jewish usage also shows temporal range rather than one magical gloss.

## High-risk phrases

Require passage-level treatment:
- *zōē aiōnios* — eternal/age-enduring life;
- *kolasis aiōnios* — age-enduring/eternal punishment;
- eschatological age language;
- present/future age contrasts.

## Testament consequence

Do not resolve universalism versus eternal punishment by dictionary trick.

Book III translates contextually and exposes ambiguity where real.

Book VI/VIII can survey later doctrinal developments separately.
