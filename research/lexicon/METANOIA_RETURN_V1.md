# Metanoia / Repentance / Return V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

English "repent" often suggests:
feel guilty, feel sorry, confess wrongdoing.

That semantic package cannot simply be projected backward onto every Hebrew/Greek source.

## Historical development

David Lambert's *How Repentance Became Biblical* argues that "repentance" as a unified inward religious concept develops historically, especially in Hellenistic Judaism and Christianity, and that later interpreters can read that concept backward into earlier biblical language.

Reference:
- https://academic.oup.com/book/7837

His philological discussion of "return to YHWH" warns against assuming that Hebrew prophetic "return" always means the later technical concept of inward repentance.

Reference:
- "A Material '(Re)turn to YHWH'":
  https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190212247.003.0005

## New Testament

Greek *metanoia/metanoeō* can involve changed mind/orientation and transformed conduct, but its specific meaning is contextual and participates in Hellenistic Jewish conceptual development.

Reference:
- Lambert, "The Genealogy of Repentance":
  https://academic.oup.com/book/7837/chapter-abstract/153040191

## John and Yeshua

John's proclamation and Synoptic Yeshua tradition use repentance/turning in an apocalyptic/judgment environment.

Testament should represent this as demanded reorientation/action, not merely private remorse.

## Translation strategy

Possible renderings depending on context:
- repent;
- turn;
- change course;
- reorient yourselves;
- return.

No single modern gloss is mandatory.

## Simulation/theology guard

"Rewrite your code" may be a Book VII/VIII analogy.

It is not a translation of *metanoia*.
