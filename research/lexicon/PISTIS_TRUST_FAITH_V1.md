# Pistis — Trust / Faith / Faithfulness V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

English "faith" can imply belief without evidence, assent to propositions, loyalty, trust, religion, or confidence.

Greek *pistis* and its cognates occupy a broader relational/social field.

## Semantic range

Teresa Morgan's major historical study maps *pistis/fides* across Greco-Roman, Jewish and early-Christian contexts, including:
- trust;
- trustworthiness;
- faithfulness;
- good faith;
- credit;
- guarantee;
- legal trust;
- proof/evidence;
- religious belief.

Reference:
- Teresa Morgan, *Roman Faith and Christian Faith*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/7335
- introduction:
  https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198724148.003.0001

Morgan's later work argues that relational trust, trustworthiness, faithfulness and entrustedness are central to early-Christian *pistis* language.

Reference:
- *The New Testament and the Theology of Trust*:
  https://academic.oup.com/book/43028

## Current debate

No single English word captures every use.

Modern scholarship debates emphases such as:
- trust;
- loyalty/allegiance;
- faithfulness;
- belief;
- relationship/practice.

A 2020/2022 Cambridge article argues for a combined trust-loyalty account rather than exclusive identification with one component.

Reference:
- Pace & McKaughan, "Judaeo-Christian faith as trust and loyalty":
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412520000153

## Yeshua tradition

In sayings such as "your *pistis* has saved/healed you," translation should test:
- trust;
- confidence;
- faith;
- fidelity;
rather than mechanically inserting later doctrinal "faith."

## Testament consequence

The literary edition may often prefer "trust" where relationship/action is foregrounded.

It must not globally replace every *pistis* with "trust."

Passage-level lexical decisions belong in the apparatus.
