# Pneuma — Spirit / Breath / Wind V1

Status: LEXICAL_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Problem

"Pneuma = Holy Spirit" is not a lexical rule.

Greek *pneuma* can participate in semantic fields involving:
- breath;
- wind;
- spirit;
- life-force;
- divine spirit;
- human spirit;
- other spirits.

Context and construction decide.

## Jewish and Hellenistic worlds

Greek-speaking Jewish writers inherit Hebrew *ruach* traditions through Greek *pneuma* while also living in a Hellenistic world where *pneuma* has philosophical/medical senses.

Johannine scholarship continues to debate Jewish Wisdom/apocalyptic and Hellenistic philosophical contexts for Logos/Pneuma language.

Reference:
- Gitte Buch-Hansen, "The Johannine Literature in a Greek Context":
  https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38583/chapter-abstract/334610066

## High-risk Jesus passages

Research passage by passage:
- Spirit descending at baptism;
- driven/led by Spirit;
- blasphemy against Holy Spirit;
- spirit versus flesh;
- "spirit is willing";
- exorcism/spirit language;
- Johannine wind/spirit wordplay in John 3.

## Simulation firewall

"Pneuma = signal" or "API" is forbidden as translation.

Book VII may compare invisible causal influence, information flow or interface to ancient Spirit language only as PROJECT_ANALOGY.

## Testament consequence

Where source ambiguity between breath/wind/spirit matters, preserve it rather than resolving it invisibly in English.
