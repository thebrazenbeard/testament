# ENTOS — "Within" / "Among" / "Within Reach" V1

Status: LEXICON_PACKET / TRANSLATION_DISPUTE_ACTIVE

Primary target:
- Luke 17:21: `hē basileia tou theou entos hymōn estin`

## Why this entry exists

The Testament has an authored interest in "the kingdom within."

That makes Luke 17:21 a high-bias lexical site.

This file exists to prevent an attractive theological rendering from becoming evidence by repetition.

## New Testament distribution

`entos` is rare in the New Testament.

The other New Testament occurrence is Matthew 23:26, where the word refers to the inside/interior of the cup.

That occurrence supports the lexical reality of an interior sense.

It does not prove that Luke 17:21 must mean an interior kingdom, because syntax, referent, discourse, and idiom still differ.

Colin H. Roberts additionally notes that Luke has `en tō mesō` available for "among/in the midst" and uses that idiom repeatedly.

That fact weighs against pretending `entos` simply equals Luke's ordinary "among" expression.

It still does not make "within you" certain.

## Live translation field

Keep at least these options visible:

1. **within you**
   - straightforward interior sense;
   - supported by the lexical force visible in Matthew 23:26 and by ancient reception;
   - vulnerable to over-individualizing kingdom language.

2. **among you / in your midst**
   - reads the saying as kingdom-presence among the interlocutors, often associated with Jesus's presence or present kingdom manifestation;
   - widely defended in modern interpretation;
   - should not be represented as the only grammatically possible reading.

3. **within your power / within your reach**
   - defended by Colin H. Roberts from Greek, papyrological, and patristic evidence;
   - roughly: the kingdom is presently available / within the hearers' power to receive or reject;
   - less common in contemporary English translation practice;
   - remains an attested scholarly option and must not be silently dropped.

## Roberts 1948 control

Colin H. Roberts, "The Kingdom of Heaven (Lk. XVII.21)," Harvard Theological Review 41 (1948): 1–8.
DOI: 10.1017/S0017816000019313

Roberts surveys:
- the older "within" interpretation;
- the "among/in your midst" interpretation;
- Greek usage;
- papyrological evidence;
- patristic reception;
- Thomas/Oxyrhynchus reception.

His own conclusion favors a "within your power" sense.

Testament records that as:
**SCHOLARLY_ARGUMENT / NOT PROJECT FACT**.

## Thomas control

Thomas 3 is relevant reception/comparative evidence because its extant forms explicitly develop kingdom-location language.

But:
- Thomas 3 is not Luke 17:21;
- its Greek and Coptic forms themselves are not textually flat;
- Thomas cannot settle Luke's lexical semantics by retrojection.

Thomas 113 further develops non-obvious kingdom presence but is Coptic-only in the currently bound witness set.

## Semantic ceiling

Established:
- `entos` can carry an interior sense;
- Luke 17:21 has generated multiple ancient and modern readings;
- "within," "among/in your midst," and "within your power/reach" all have documented interpretive histories.

Not established:
- that one English gloss exhausts the Greek;
- that Matthew 23:26 mechanically determines Luke 17:21;
- that Luke's other "midst" idiom makes "among" impossible;
- that Thomas determines Luke;
- that the verse provides lexical proof for Testament's authored agency thesis.
