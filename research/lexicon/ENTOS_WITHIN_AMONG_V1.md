# ENTOS — "Within" / "Among" / "Within Reach" V1

Status: LEXICON_PACKET / CORPUS_REVIEW_ADVANCED / TRANSLATION_DISPUTE_ACTIVE

Primary target:
- Luke 17:21: `hē basileia tou theou entos hymōn estin`

## Why this entry exists

The Testament has an authored interest in "the kingdom within."

That makes Luke 17:21 a high-bias lexical site.

This file exists to prevent an attractive theological rendering from becoming evidence by repetition.

## New Testament distribution

`entos` is rare in the New Testament.

The other New Testament occurrence is Matthew 23:26, where the word refers to the inside/interior of the cup.

That occurrence supports the lexical reality of an interior sense.

It does not prove that Luke 17:21 must mean an interior kingdom, because syntax, referent, discourse, and idiom still differ.

Colin H. Roberts additionally notes that Luke has `en tō mesō` available for "among/in the midst" and uses that idiom repeatedly.

That fact weighs against pretending `entos` simply equals Luke's ordinary "among" expression.

It still does not make "within you" certain.

## Broader Greek corpus review

The lexical review is now broader than the New Testament and Roberts 1948.

### LSJ / classical range

LSJ's core range is:
- within;
- inside;
- within the bounds or limits of something.

Representative extensions include:
- spatial enclosure: within walls, within a city, within range;
- group-bounded position: troops within their own lines;
- idiomatic self-control / possession of oneself: expressions such as being "within oneself";
- figurative boundedness such as being within one's reasoning or competence.

This matters because the semantic center is not an abstract English synonym "among."

The word normally marks **interiority or bounded inclusion**.

A plural genitive can therefore yield a contextual group reading without making "among" the lexical core.

Reference:
- LSJ, s.v. `ἐντός`.

### Papyrological breadth

The Cologne papyri word-index infrastructure records `ἐντός` across multiple documentary corpora.

That confirms the word is not a rare New-Testament technical term.

At the same time, the index itself does not adjudicate Luke 17:21; individual papyrus contexts must be interpreted rather than counted mechanically.

Reference:
- Kölner Papyri Wörterlisten, `ἐντός`.

### Holmén 1996

Tom Holmén's peer-reviewed study is specifically framed around the semantic restrictions of `entos hymon`.

Later scholarly summaries cite Holmén among the major objections to treating "among/in your midst" as an uncomplicated lexical equivalent.

Testament records:
- the article as a major semantic study;
- no single inferred conclusion beyond what accessible scholarship securely reports.

Reference:
- Tom Holmén, "The Alternatives of the Kingdom: Encountering the Semantic Restrictions of Luke 17,20–21 (ἐντὸς ὑμῶν)," ZNW 87 (1996): 204–229.
- DOI 10.1515/zntw.1996.87.3-4.204.

### Ramelli 2009/2011

Ilaria Ramelli argues explicitly for "inside you."

Her case combines:
- Greek corpus usage anterior to and contemporary with Luke;
- the `entos + genitive` construction;
- Luke's recurring use of `en mesō` when he means "among/in the midst";
- ancient Syriac versions;
- patristic interpretation.

This is a substantial counterweight to the modern-commentary majority preference for "among."

It is still an argument, not project fact.

Reference:
- Ilaria Ramelli, "Luke 17:21: 'The Kingdom of God is Inside You'," Hugoye 12.
- DOI 10.31826/hug-2011-120112.

### Modern commentary majority

A substantial modern commentary stream prefers "among you / in your midst."

Published scholarship commonly grounds that preference less in the lexical core of `entos` than in:
- the plural addressee;
- the Pharisee setting;
- Luke's broader kingdom theology;
- the idea that the kingdom is present in Jesus's ministry rather than inside hostile interlocutors.

A University of Chicago survey names Fitzmyer, Luke Timothy Johnson, Tannehill, Green and others within this majority line.

That evidence is exegetical/contextual.

It must not be mislabeled as a lexical demonstration that `entos` means "among."

### Translator's Handbook three-way field

The UBS translator tradition explicitly preserves three possible senses:
- within you / within your heart;
- among you;
- within your reach.

Its preference for "among" is therefore an exegetical choice made after acknowledging the wider semantic field.

### Corpus-level result

The bounded lexical conclusion is now:

1. **Interior / within-bounds meaning is lexically primary and well attested.**
2. **A group-bounded "among/in your midst" construal is possible in context, but should not be represented as the ordinary dictionary meaning of `entos`.**
3. **A control/reach/power extension is genuinely within the attested semantic family and cannot be dismissed as invented.**
4. **The Greek corpus does not by itself choose the Luke 17:21 translation.**
5. **The final choice is necessarily syntactic + contextual + theological/exegetical, not lexical alone.**

This changes the project's control language from:
"three roughly equal lexical options"

to:

**one lexical center with multiple contextual construals, none of which may be promoted to a historical or theological certainty merely from the word itself.**

## Live translation field

Keep at least these options visible:

1. **within you**
   - straightforward interior sense;
   - supported by the lexical force visible in Matthew 23:26 and by ancient reception;
   - vulnerable to over-individualizing kingdom language.

2. **among you / in your midst**
   - reads the saying as kingdom-presence among the interlocutors, often associated with Jesus's presence or present kingdom manifestation;
   - widely defended in modern interpretation;
   - should not be represented as the only grammatically possible reading.

3. **within your power / within your reach**
   - defended by Colin H. Roberts from Greek, papyrological, and patristic evidence;
   - roughly: the kingdom is presently available / within the hearers' power to receive or reject;
   - less common in contemporary English translation practice;
   - remains an attested scholarly option and must not be silently dropped.

## Roberts 1948 control

Colin H. Roberts, "The Kingdom of Heaven (Lk. XVII.21)," Harvard Theological Review 41 (1948): 1–8.
DOI: 10.1017/S0017816000019313

Roberts surveys:
- the older "within" interpretation;
- the "among/in your midst" interpretation;
- Greek usage;
- papyrological evidence;
- patristic reception;
- Thomas/Oxyrhynchus reception.

His own conclusion favors a "within your power" sense.

Testament records that as:
**SCHOLARLY_ARGUMENT / NOT PROJECT FACT**.

## Thomas control

Thomas 3 is relevant reception/comparative evidence because its extant forms explicitly develop kingdom-location language.

But:
- Thomas 3 is not Luke 17:21;
- its Greek and Coptic forms themselves are not textually flat;
- Thomas cannot settle Luke's lexical semantics by retrojection.

Thomas 113 further develops non-obvious kingdom presence but is Coptic-only in the currently bound witness set.

## Semantic ceiling

Established:
- the lexical center of `entos` is interiority / bounded inclusion;
- classical usage permits extensions involving group bounds, range, self-control and being within one's power;
- Luke 17:21 has generated multiple ancient and modern readings;
- "within," "among/in your midst," and "within your power/reach" all have documented interpretive histories;
- modern preference for "among" is substantially contextual/exegetical rather than a simple dictionary result.

Not established:
- that the lexical center alone determines Luke 17:21;
- that "among" is lexically impossible;
- that "within you" necessarily means an individualized private spiritual state;
- that "within your reach/power" is the one intended idiom;
- that Matthew 23:26 mechanically determines Luke 17:21;
- that Luke's other "midst" idiom makes "among" impossible;
- that Thomas determines Luke;
- that the verse provides lexical proof for Testament's authored agency thesis.
