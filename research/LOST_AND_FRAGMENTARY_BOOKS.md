# Lost, Mentioned, and Fragmentary Works

The project will search for these works, but a matching later title must never be treated as the lost original without provenance.

## Lost works mentioned in the Hebrew Bible

Examples include:
- Book of the Wars of YHWH — Numbers 21:14;
- Book of Jashar / the Upright — Joshua 10:13; 2 Samuel 1:18;
- Book/Acts of Solomon — 1 Kings 11:41;
- chronicles/books of the kings of Israel and Judah repeatedly cited in Kings/Chronicles;
- records of Samuel the seer, Nathan the prophet, and Gad the seer — 1 Chronicles 29:29;
- prophecy of Ahijah and visions of Iddo — 2 Chronicles 9:29;
- records of Shemaiah and Iddo — 2 Chronicles 12:15;
- records of Jehu son of Hanani — 2 Chronicles 20:34.

Status:
Mostly lost as independently identifiable works. Biblical citations may preserve information from or about them, but the lost works' full contents cannot be reconstructed by imagination.

## Lost or disputed early Christian writings

Priority:
- earlier letter of Paul to Corinth referenced in 1 Corinthians 5:9;
- "severe" or tearful letter associated with 2 Corinthians 2/7, whose relation to canonical 2 Corinthians is debated;
- letter from/to Laodicea referenced in Colossians 4:16, identity disputed;
- Papias's Expositions of the Sayings of the Lord, surviving in fragments through later authors;
- Hegesippus's Memoirs, fragmentarily preserved;
- writings of Quadratus;
- works of early Jewish-Christian groups known through hostile citations;
- Gospel of the Hebrews/Nazarenes/Ebionites, known fragmentarily and with modern taxonomy debated;
- Celsus's True Doctrine, lost except through Origen's rebuttal;
- Porphyry's Against the Christians, fragmentarily reconstructed through opponents.

## False recovery guard

Later works titled "Jasher," "Laodiceans," or similar are not promoted to the ancient lost work merely because the name matches.

## Literary use

The manuscript may acknowledge the silence created by lost works.

It may not invent their contents and then cite the invention as recovered tradition.
