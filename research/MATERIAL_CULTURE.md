# Material Culture Research Plan

Text-heavy Jesus research can become self-referential. Material evidence constrains the world independently.

## Sites

Research excavation reports and current scholarship for:
- Nazareth;
- Capernaum;
- Magdala;
- Sepphoris;
- Cana candidates;
- Bethsaida candidates;
- Jerusalem;
- Temple Mount/Haram context where archaeology permits;
- pools and mikvaot;
- Mount of Olives;
- Jericho;
- Caesarea Maritima.

## Artifacts and evidence classes

- coins of Herodians and Roman governors;
- Pilate inscription;
- ossuaries and naming distributions;
- synagogue remains;
- domestic architecture;
- fishing/agricultural equipment;
- ritual baths;
- stone vessels;
- roads;
- tombs;
- crucifixion evidence;
- military inscriptions;
- papyri documenting taxation, debt, labor, contracts and administration.

## Famous-object guard

High-profile artifacts require special skepticism:
- "James ossuary";
- purported Jesus-family tomb identifications;
- relics;
- objects with unverified provenance.

An object can be ancient while the story attached to it is modern.
