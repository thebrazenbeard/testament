## Immediate source work

### 1. Afallennau / Oianau stratigraphy
Goal: separate the older-looking wild-man nucleus from later political prophecy.

Current control:
The National Library of Wales notes that Myrddin is not named in these two poems and that Norman-era prophecies are later additions around an older nucleus.

Source:
https://www.library.wales/discover-learn/digital-exhibitions/manuscripts/the-middle-ages/the-black-book-of-carmarthen

Questions:
- Which lines are assigned to the proposed ninth/tenth-century nucleus?
- What linguistic features drive those dates?
- Which proper names and political predictions are demonstrably later?
- Does the nucleus actually mention Arfderydd, Gwenddolau, Rhydderch, or Coed Celyddon?

### 2. Ymddiddan Myrddin a Thaliesin
Goal: determine the earliest secure linkage of the name Myrddin with Arfderydd material.

Current control:
The Welsh Classical Dictionary summarizes Jarman's analysis as two poems joined together, with an Arfderydd section and a proposed eleventh-century composition.

Source:
https://www.library.wales/fileadmin/docs_gwefan/new_structure/discover/digital_exhibitions/printed_material/welsh_classical_dictionary/08_M-N.pdf

### 3. Lailoken corpus
Goal: map every surviving occurrence before and around Geoffrey.

Questions:
- Which Lailoken text is earliest in manuscript terms?
- Does any independent Lailoken witness mention Arfderydd?
- Which features precede Geoffrey and which may be Geoffrey-influenced?
- Is the threefold death attached independently to Lailoken or imported through common folklore?

### 4. Historical North Britain
Goal: reconstruct the political geography without using Arthurian romance.

Anchor:
Andrew Breeze's review of Arfderydd treats the battle as historical and notes that the oldest Annales Cambriae witness merely records `Bellum Armterid`.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/british-battles-493937/573-legends-of-merlin-at-arfderydd-or-arthuret-cumbria/C1EB5FB46F82422A6FC781AB30CACBB6

Build independent dossiers for:
- Rhydderch Hael;
- Gwenddolau;
- Peredur and Gwrgi;
- Eliffer;
- Alt Clut;
- Rheged;
- possible Arfderydd/Arthuret landscape.

### 5. Geoffrey's construction process
Goal: distinguish what Geoffrey inherited from what he recombined.

Current 2026 synthesis:
Jones and Flood describe early Merlin as the product of multiple and potentially multidirectional insular traditions, centering on two figures first conflated by Geoffrey: the northern wild man and the Carmarthen-associated child prophet.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-arthurian-literature-and-culture/early-merlin-and-early-myrddin/2B7AA8308A180AFA6591A12A4730D0FC

### 6. Myrddin name problem
Goal: test the Jarman/Padel line that Myrddin developed through Caerfyrddin/Moridunum rather than preserving a historical personal name.

Do not resolve from etymological plausibility alone.

## Literary questions generated by research

- Does the protagonist ever hear the name "Myrddin" applied to himself?
- Should "Lailoken" be another person, a nickname, or an explicitly unresolved tradition?
- Can the forest sections be written without naming the protagonist at all?
- Can later interpolations interrupt the prose so the reader experiences textual accretion?
- Should Geoffrey be a character, a framing consciousness, or an epilogue-level assembler?
- Does the final "Merlin" ever appear directly, or only emerge through accumulated texts?

## Current hostile frontier

> If the old forest poems do not name Myrddin and the name itself may be secondary, then a novel centered on a confidently identified sixth-century "Merlin" would reproduce the very conflation the research has exposed.

Current literary response:
Keep the sixth-century protagonist unnamed until the tradition names him.
