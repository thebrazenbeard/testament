## Hard constraints inherited from ON_THEO

- The Battle of Arfderydd is historically anchored; Merlin at Arfderydd is not independently early-attested.
- The oldest surviving Black Book manuscript is medieval, not sixth-century.
- `Yr Afallennau` and `Yr Oianau` preserve an unnamed forest-dwelling prophetic figure; the identification of that figure as Myrddin is itself part of the transmission history.
- The personal name Myrddin may be secondary and may relate to medieval interpretation of Caerfyrddin/Carmarthen.
- Lailoken is securely a medieval northern wild-man/seer tradition; a sixth-century historical Lailoken remains unresolved.
- Rhydderch and the sixth-century northern British political milieu are better historically anchored than Merlin.
- Geoffrey of Monmouth's Merlin is composite: Ambrosius/Vortigern child-prophet material + Welsh prophecy + northern wild-man material + Geoffrey's literary construction.
- Merlin's central Arthurian role is secondary.
- Parallel wild-man traditions such as Suibhne reduce the historical specificity of madness/wilderness/prophecy motifs.
- Historical Merlin/Myrddin remains UNRESOLVED.

## Literary permissions

Testament may:
- create an unnamed survivor of Arfderydd;
- construct interiority, memory, trauma, dreams, dialogue, sensory detail, and chronology for narrative purposes;
- let different witnesses call the figure different names;
- dramatize the process by which Lailoken, Myrddin, Ambrosius, and Merlin become fused;
- use Geoffrey of Monmouth as a later narrative layer;
- explore whether prophecy can emerge from pattern recognition, altered states, trauma, poetic convention, political forecasting, supernatural experience, or combinations of these without deciding the metaphysics.

Testament may not:
- state that a historical man named Merlin or Myrddin definitely fought at Arfderydd;
- present Geoffrey's Arthurian wizard as sixth-century fact;
- make invented dialogue into a recovered quotation;
- use resemblance to Suibhne, Taliesin, Yeshua, Fionn, shamans, or other figures as evidence of historical transmission;
- convert later prophecy into proof of supernatural foresight.

## Literary thesis

The strongest current literary opportunity is not "the true biography of Merlin."

It is:

**How does a nameless or uncertain human being become a prophet, then a name, then a wizard, then an institution of memory?**

The story can therefore preserve all four levels simultaneously:

1. a real historical world;
2. a possible but unproven survivor;
3. centuries of prophetic and folkloric accretion;
4. the conscious medieval manufacture of "Merlin."
