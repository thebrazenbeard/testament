# Project Constitution

## Purpose

Testament exists to produce a source-critical literary reimagining of the life, world, death, reception, and continuing meaning of Yeshua/Jesus without pretending that one surviving tradition owns the entire historical subject.

The project may be spiritually serious, skeptical, mystical, literary, philosophical, and speculative at the same time. It may not blur those categories.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

1. History is not theology.
2. Theology is not history.
3. Later tradition is evidence for later tradition before it is evidence for an earlier event.
4. A manuscript witness is not identical to the work it preserves.
5. A source that reports a claim is not automatically evidence that the reported event happened.
6. Similarity does not prove dependence.
7. Canonical status does not equal historical priority.
8. Suppression, omission, or non-canonicity does not equal historical authenticity.
9. Hostile testimony is not automatically true or false.
10. Simulation language is a modern interpretive layer and must never be back-projected as ancient technical knowledge.
11. Uncertainty is allowed to remain unresolved.
12. Narrative beauty never upgrades confidence.

## Authorial freedom

The manuscript is allowed to invent connective tissue, interiority, dialogue, imagery, pacing, composite scenes, and symbolic structures where the evidence cannot supply them.

Invented material must be traceable as authored construction in the apparatus.

## Ethical center

The project is not anti-God and is not an argument that God does not exist.

It opposes the outsourcing of agency.

Whatever reality ultimately is, the reader remains responsible for choices made inside it.

## Success condition

A reader should be able to finish the work with stronger agency and a more complicated understanding of Yeshua, not merely a replacement orthodoxy.
